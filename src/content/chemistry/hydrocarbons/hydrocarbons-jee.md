## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you finish the Hydrocarbons lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 5 on Section D. If a structure is unclear, name the functional group and the reagent first; the product often follows.

## Conditions sheet

| Reagent | Typical transformation |
| --- | --- |
| Cl₂ or Br₂, $h\nu$ | free-radical substitution at an alkane or side chain |
| HBr | Markovnikov addition to an alkene |
| HBr + peroxide | anti-Markovnikov addition (HBr only) |
| H₂, Lindlar | alkyne → cis alkene |
| Na or Li in liquid NH₃ | alkyne → trans alkene |
| H₂O, HgSO₄/H₂SO₄ | alkyne → enol → ketone (ethyne → ethanal) |
| O₃ then Zn/H₂O | cleave C=C or C≡C to carbonyls/acids |
| Br₂/FeBr₃ | aromatic bromination |
| conc. HNO₃/conc. H₂SO₄, 323–333 K | aromatic nitration |
| KMnO₄, heat | side-chain oxidation to benzoic acid |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Fast sanity checks">

If a product has one more hydrogen than the starting alkene, hydrogen was added; if it has one more halogen, HX was added. If an aromatic product has lost aromaticity, the reaction is probably an addition under forcing conditions, not the usual substitution.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Wurtz reaction

Bromoethane treated with sodium in dry ether gives:

- A. ethane
- B. butane
- C. ethene
- D. propane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Two C₂ fragments couple: 2CH₃CH₂Br + 2Na → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ + 2NaBr.

</details>

### 2. Monochloro derivatives

How many monochloro derivatives can 2-methylpropane form?

- A. 1
- B. 2
- C. 3
- D. 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The nine primary hydrogens are equivalent; the single tertiary hydrogen is different. Products: 1-chloro-2-methylpropane and 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.

</details>

### 3. Alkene stability

Which alkene is the most stable?

- A. ethene
- B. propene
- C. but-1-ene
- D. 2-methylpropene

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** It is the most substituted and has the largest number of α-hydrogens for hyperconjugation.

</details>

### 4. Peroxide effect

Propene reacts with HBr in the presence of a peroxide to give:

- A. 1-bromopropane
- B. 2-bromopropane
- C. 1,2-dibromopropane
- D. propane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The radical pathway is anti-Markovnikov, so Br ends up on the terminal carbon.

</details>

### 5. Alkyne to cis alkene

Which reagent converts an alkyne into a cis alkene?

- A. H₂, Ni
- B. H₂, Lindlar catalyst
- C. Na in liquid NH₃
- D. Br₂ in CCl₄

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Lindlar catalyst gives syn addition of hydrogen, so the hydrogens end up on the same side.

</details>

### 6. Terminal alkyne test

A terminal alkyne gives a white precipitate with:

- A. bromine water
- B. ammoniacal silver nitrate
- C. sodium chloride
- D. dilute sulphuric acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Silver acetylide is white. Internal alkynes do not react.

</details>

### 7. Hydration of propyne

Propyne with aqueous HgSO₄/H₂SO₄ gives:

- A. propan-1-ol
- B. propan-2-ol
- C. propanal
- D. propanone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Markovnikov hydration gives the enol CH₃C(OH)=CH₂, which tautomerises to propanone.

</details>

### 8. Aromatic chlorination

Benzene with Cl₂ and FeCl₃ gives:

- A. chlorobenzene
- B. benzene hexachloride
- C. cyclohexane
- D. benzyl chloride

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The Lewis acid generates Cl⁺, and substitution preserves aromaticity.

</details>

### 9. Side-chain oxidation

Ethylbenzene with hot alkaline KMnO₄ gives:

- A. benzene
- B. benzoic acid
- C. benzaldehyde
- D. phenol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The benzylic carbon is oxidised all the way to –COOH.

</details>

### 10. Directing effects

Which group directs electrophilic substitution to the meta position?

- A. –OH
- B. –CH₃
- C. –NO₂
- D. –Cl

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** –NO₂ withdraws by resonance and destabilises the arenium ion most at ortho and para positions.

</details>

## Section B — 10 numerical questions

### 11. Monochloro derivatives of propane

How many structural monochloro derivatives does propane form?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two: 1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane.

</details>

### 12. Monochloro derivatives of a branched alkane

How many structural monochloro derivatives does 2-methylbutane form?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Four, from the four distinct hydrogen environments.

</details>

### 13. Monobromo derivatives

How many structural monobromo derivatives does 2-methylpropane form?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two.

</details>

### 14. Degrees of unsaturation

Find the degrees of unsaturation of benzene.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$(12+2-6)/2=4$: one ring and three π bonds.

</details>

### 15. Hydrogenation stoichiometry

How many moles of H₂ are needed to hydrogenate one mole of but-1-yne completely?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two moles — one for each π bond.

</details>

### 16. HBr addition stoichiometry

How many moles of HBr add to one mole of ethyne to give 1,1-dibromoethane?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two moles.

</details>

### 17. Combustion oxygen

What volume of O₂ at STP is needed for the complete combustion of one mole of methane?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, so 2 mol O₂ = 44.8 L at STP.

</details>

### 18. Combustion carbon dioxide

What mass of CO₂ is produced by the complete combustion of one mole of propane?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O; 3 mol CO₂ = 3 × 44 = 132 g.

</details>

### 19. Alpha hydrogens

How many α-hydrogens does 2-methylpropene have?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Six — the two methyl groups attached to the double-bond carbon.

</details>

### 20. Alkene isomers

How many open-chain structural alkene isomers have the formula C₄H₈?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Three: but-1-ene, but-2-ene and 2-methylpropene.

</details>

## Section C — 5 mixed-method questions

### 21. Explain the peroxide effect

Why does the peroxide effect occur with HBr but not with HCl or HI?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The radical chain needs a propagation step in which a halogen atom abstracts hydrogen from the alkene. For HBr this step is exothermic enough to sustain the chain. For HCl it is strongly endothermic, and for HI the H–I bond is so weak that hydrogen abstraction by an iodine radical is also endothermic, so the chain does not propagate. Only HBr gives the anti-Markovnikov product.

</details>

### 22. Distinguish two gases

Describe one chemical test that distinguishes ethyne from ethene.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Add ammoniacal silver nitrate. Ethyne has a terminal hydrogen and gives a white precipitate of silver acetylide; ethene does not.

</details>

### 23. Reactivity order

Arrange phenol, benzene and nitrobenzene in order of increasing rate of electrophilic substitution.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Nitrobenzene < benzene < phenol. –NO₂ deactivates; –OH activates.

</details>

### 24. Acidity of a terminal alkyne

Why is ethyne weakly acidic? Write its reaction with sodium amide.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The $sp$ hybridised carbon has high s-character, so the C–H bond is polarised and the acetylide anion is relatively stable.

$$
\mathrm{HC{\equiv}CH + NaNH_2 \rightarrow HC{\equiv}C^{-}Na^{+} + NH_3}
$$

</details>

### 25. Ozonolysis deduction

An alkene with formula C₅H₁₀ gives propanone and ethanal on ozonolysis. Identify the alkene.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Join the carbonyl carbons with a double bond: (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃, which is **2-methylbut-2-ene**.

</details>

## Section D — 2 full-length numericals

### 26. Mechanism and regiochemistry

Propene reacts with HBr.

1. Give the major product and the mechanism.
2. Give the product when a peroxide is present, and explain the difference.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**1.** The proton adds to C1 to give the more stable secondary carbocation at C2. Bromide then attacks C2:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH{=}CH_2 + HBr \rightarrow CH_3CHBrCH_3}
$$

Major product: **2-bromopropane**.

**2.** With peroxide, a bromine radical adds to the terminal carbon to give the more stable secondary radical, and hydrogen abstraction then gives **1-bromopropane**. The radical pathway is anti-Markovnikov because radical stability, not carbocation stability, controls the regiochemistry.

</details>

### 27. Conversions from ethyne

Give the reagent and conditions for each conversion starting from ethyne:

1. ethyne → ethanal
2. ethyne → benzene
3. ethyne → polyacetylene

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Hydration with dilute H₂SO₄ and HgSO₄: the enol ethenol tautomerises to ethanal.
2. Pass ethyne through a red-hot tube at about 873 K; three molecules cyclise to benzene.
3. Polymerise with a Ziegler–Natta catalyst to give linear polyacetylene.

</details>
