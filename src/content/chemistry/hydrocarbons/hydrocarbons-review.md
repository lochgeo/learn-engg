## Your study plan for this chapter

Hydrocarbons are best learned as four connected reaction families rather than four separate chapters. A focused pass takes about **1 hour 45 minutes**:

1. **Alkanes** — 25 min
2. **Alkenes** — 30 min
3. **Alkynes** — 25 min
4. **Aromatic hydrocarbons** — 30 min
5. **This mastery check** — 25 min

After each lesson, cover the reaction tables and write the reagents and products from memory. The goal is a reflex: given a starting material and a reagent, name the product and the reason.

After this core pass, use the optional **JEE Main timed practice** module for a $50$-minute mixed set.

## Choose the tool before you write a product

| The question gives… | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| an alkane and a halogen | free-radical substitution; rank the hydrogens |
| an alkene and HX | Markovnikov addition through the more stable carbocation |
| an alkene, HBr and peroxide | anti-Markovnikov radical addition |
| an alkyne and H₂ with Lindlar or Na/NH₃ | cis or trans alkene |
| an alkyne and aqueous HgSO₄/H₂SO₄ | enol, then ketone (ethyne gives ethanal) |
| O₃ followed by Zn/H₂O | cleave the π bond; place carbonyls on both fragments |
| a benzene ring and an electrophile | substitution; check the directing group |
| a side chain on benzene and hot KMnO₄ | oxidation to benzoic acid |

## Mixed practice

1. What is the product of the Wurtz reaction between two molecules of bromoethane?
2. How many monochloro derivatives does 2-methylpropane form?
3. Which is more stable: but-1-ene or 2-methylpropene? Why?
4. What is the product of propene with HBr in the presence of a peroxide?
5. Which reagent converts but-2-yne into cis-but-2-ene?
6. How would you distinguish ethyne from ethene?
7. What is the product of the hydration of propyne?
8. Write the equation for the bromination of benzene with FeBr₃.
9. What happens when ethylbenzene is heated with alkaline KMnO₄?
10. Which group is a meta director: –OH, –CH₃ or –NO₂?
11. How many moles of H₂ are needed to hydrogenate one mole of but-1-yne completely?
12. Why does benzene not decolourise bromine water?
13. Give the ozonolysis products of 2-methylbut-2-ene.
14. Why is a terminal alkyne acidic?
15. Why does Friedel–Crafts alkylation sometimes give a rearranged product?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Butane.
2. Two: 1-chloro-2-methylpropane and 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.
3. 2-Methylpropene; it is more substituted and has more α-hydrogens.
4. 1-Bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov).
5. Lindlar catalyst (H₂, Pd/CaCO₃, quinoline).
6. Add ammoniacal silver nitrate: ethyne gives a white precipitate of silver acetylide, ethene does not. Bromine water is not a useful distinction here because both decolourise it.
7. Propanone.
8. C₆H₆ + Br₂ → C₆H₅Br + HBr, with FeBr₃ as catalyst.
9. It is oxidised to benzoic acid.
10. –NO₂.
11. Two moles.
12. Benzene has no isolated C=C bond; its delocalised π system is not reactive enough to add bromine without a catalyst.
13. Propanone and ethanal.
14. The $sp$ carbon has high s-character, stabilising the acetylide anion.
15. The initial carbocation can rearrange (hydride or methyl shift) before the alkyl group attaches.

</details>

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Reaction errors that cost marks">

- Writing Markovnikov addition for HBr plus peroxide.
- Using Lindlar catalyst and sodium in liquid ammonia interchangeably.
- Hydrating ethyne to a ketone instead of ethanal.
- Forgetting that halogen substituents are deactivating but ortho/para directing.
- Oxidising a benzene ring when only the side chain reacts.

</learning-block>
