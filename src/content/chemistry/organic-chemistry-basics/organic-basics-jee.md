## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you finish the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 5 on Section D. If a question takes more than 90 seconds without progress, mark it and move on.

Before writing an answer, say which tool you are using: naming rules, isomer counting, stability ranking, purification choice, or an analysis formula.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name a structure | longest chain containing the principal group | principal group numbered first; prefixes alphabetical |
| Structure from a name | number the parent chain | place the principal group, then multiple bonds, then branches |
| Count isomers | degrees of unsaturation | skeleton → position → functional group → stereo |
| Compare stability | carbocation $3^\circ>2^\circ>1^\circ$; alkene α-H count | do not compare a cation with an anion |
| Compare acidity | stability of the conjugate base | −I and −M groups stabilise the anion |
| Separate a mixture | boiling point, solubility or adsorption | steam distillation needs immiscibility with water |
| Element percentage | Liebig, Dumas, Kjeldahl or Carius | match the method to the element and the data given |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Fast sanity checks">

If an isomer count exceeds $2^n$ for $n$ stereocentres, you have double-counted. If a percentage composition sums above 100, an arithmetic slip has occurred. If a name uses a prefix for the principal functional group, the priority order has been ignored.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Naming a branched alkane

The IUPAC name of CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃ is:

- A. 3-methylpentane
- B. 2-methylpentane
- C. 4-methylpentane
- D. 2-ethylbutane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The longest chain has five carbons; numbering from the left gives the methyl locant 2.

</details>

### 2. Chiral centres

How many chiral carbon atoms are in CH₃–CH(Cl)–CH(OH)–CH₃?

- A. 0
- B. 1
- C. 2
- D. 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** C2 carries H, Cl, CH₃ and the rest of the chain; C3 carries H, OH, CH₃ and the rest of the chain.

</details>

### 3. Carbocation stability

Which carbocation is the most stable?

- A. CH₃⁺
- B. CH₃CH₂⁺
- C. (CH₃)₂CH⁺
- D. (CH₃)₃C⁺

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Three alkyl groups and the largest number of α C–H bonds stabilise the empty p orbital.

</details>

### 4. Geometrical isomerism

Which compound shows geometrical isomerism?

- A. but-1-ene
- B. pent-2-ene
- C. 2-methylpropene
- D. propene

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Each double-bond carbon of pent-2-ene carries two different groups. In the others, at least one alkene carbon carries two hydrogens or two methyls.

</details>

### 5. Priority and naming

The IUPAC name of HOCH₂CH₂CHO is:

- A. 3-hydroxypropanal
- B. 1-hydroxypropan-3-al
- C. propan-1-ol-3-al
- D. 3-oxidopropan-1-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The aldehyde carbon is C1, the –OH is on C3, and –CHO outranks –OH, so the alcohol appears as a prefix.

</details>

### 6. Ether isomers

How many ethers have the molecular formula C₄H₁₀O?

- A. 2
- B. 3
- C. 4
- D. 5

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** 1-methoxypropane, 2-methoxypropane and ethoxyethane.

</details>

### 7. Functional isomerism

Which pair are functional isomers?

- A. propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol
- B. propanal and propanone
- C. butane and 2-methylpropane
- D. pent-1-ene and pent-2-ene

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Both are C₃H₆O, but one is an aldehyde and the other a ketone.

</details>

### 8. Lassaigne's test

A Prussian blue colour in Lassaigne's test confirms the presence of:

- A. sulphur
- B. nitrogen
- C. chlorine
- D. phosphorus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Iron(III) hexacyanoferrate(II) is Prussian blue; it forms from cyanide in the sodium fusion extract.

</details>

### 9. Chromatography

A spot moves 3.0 cm while the solvent front moves 12.0 cm. Its $R_f$ is:

- A. 0.25
- B. 0.40
- C. 4.0
- D. 0.75

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** $R_f=3.0/12.0=0.25$.

</details>

### 10. Percentage carbon

Which compound has the highest percentage of carbon by mass?

- A. CH₄
- B. C₂H₆
- C. C₃H₈
- D. C₄H₁₀

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The carbon fraction is $12n/(14n+2)$, which rises towards 12/14 as $n$ increases: 82.8% for butane.

</details>

## Section B — 10 numerical questions

### 11. Degrees of unsaturation

Find the degrees of unsaturation of C₇H₈.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$(14+2-8)/2=4$ — one ring and three π bonds, as in toluene.

</details>

### 12. Degrees of unsaturation

Find the degrees of unsaturation of C₅H₈.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$(10+2-8)/2=2$ — two π bonds, or one π bond and one ring.

</details>

### 13. Alkane isomers

How many structural isomers does C₅H₁₂ have?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Three: pentane, 2-methylbutane and 2,2-dimethylpropane.

</details>

### 14. Stereoisomers

How many stereoisomers does 2-chlorobutane have?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Two. It has one chiral centre, so it exists as a pair of enantiomers.

</details>

### 15. Percentage carbon

Find the percentage of carbon in ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$M=60$; carbon mass $=24$. $\%C=24/60\times100=40\%$.

</details>

### 16. Percentage nitrogen

Find the percentage of nitrogen in ethanenitrile, CH₃CN.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$M=41$. $\%N=14/41\times100=34.1\%$.

</details>

### 17. Hydrogen by combustion

0.20 g of a compound gives 0.36 g of water. Find the percentage of hydrogen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\%H=\frac{2}{18}\times\frac{0.36}{0.20}\times100=20\%$.

</details>

### 18. Nitrogen by Dumas

0.30 g of a compound gives 56 mL of nitrogen at STP. Find the percentage of nitrogen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\%N=\frac{28}{22400}\times\frac{56}{0.30}\times100=23.3\%$.

</details>

### 19. Nitrogen by Kjeldahl

0.40 g of a compound consumes 20 mL of 0.25 N acid. Find the percentage of nitrogen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\%N=\frac{1.4\times0.25\times20}{0.40}=17.5\%$.

</details>

### 20. Empirical formula

A compound contains 40.0% C, 6.67% H and 53.33% O. Find its empirical formula.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Moles: C 3.33, H 6.67, O 3.33. Divide by 3.33: CH₂O.

</details>

## Section C — 5 mixed-method questions

### 21. Name and justify

Name CH₂=C(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₃ and explain why the numbering starts where it does.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**2-methylbut-1-ene.** The double bond must receive the lowest possible locant, so the chain is numbered from the CH₂ end; the methyl is then at C2. Numbering from the other end would place the double bond at C2.

</details>

### 22. Basicity

Explain why aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The nitrogen lone pair in aniline is delocalised into the aromatic ring by resonance, so it is less available to accept a proton. In ethylamine the lone pair is localised on nitrogen and is further enriched by the +I ethyl group.

</details>

### 23. Acidity

Which is more acidic, phenol or ethanol? Justify your answer.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Phenol. Its conjugate base, the phenoxide ion, is stabilised by resonance with the ring, which spreads the negative charge. Ethoxide has no such delocalisation.

</details>

### 24. Purification choices

Choose a method for each mixture and give one reason.

1. Camphor mixed with sodium chloride
2. Two liquids boiling at 350 K and 352 K
3. Aniline mixed with water

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Sublimation — camphor sublimes; the salt does not.
2. Fractional distillation — the boiling points are only 2 K apart, so a fractionating column is needed.
3. Steam distillation — aniline is steam-volatile and immiscible with water.

</details>

### 25. Identify from a test

A compound with formula C₃H₆O gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Identify it and its functional isomer.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The silver mirror indicates an aldehyde: **propanal**, CH₃CH₂CHO. Its functional isomer is **propanone**, CH₃COCH₃.

</details>

## Section D — 2 full-length numericals

### 26. Counting alkene isomers

Consider all open-chain alkenes with molecular formula C₅H₁₀.

1. How many structural isomers are there?
2. How many stereoisomers in total?
3. Which structural isomer shows cis–trans isomerism?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Degrees of unsaturation: $(10+2-10)/2=1$, so each isomer has exactly one double bond and no ring.

Structural isomers: **pent-1-ene, pent-2-ene, 2-methylbut-1-ene, 3-methylbut-1-ene and 2-methylbut-2-ene** — five in total.

Pent-2-ene has two different groups on each double-bond carbon, so it has cis and trans forms. The other four have no geometric isomers.

Total stereoisomers: $5+1=6$.

</details>

### 27. Full elemental analysis

A 0.30 g sample of an organic compound gives 0.22 g of CO₂ and 0.18 g of H₂O on combustion. A separate 0.30 g sample consumes 20.0 mL of 0.50 N acid in a Kjeldahl determination.

1. Calculate the percentage of carbon, hydrogen and nitrogen.
2. Find the percentage of oxygen by difference.
3. Determine the empirical formula.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Carbon:**

$$
\%C=\frac{12}{44}\times\frac{0.22}{0.30}\times100=20.0\%
$$

**Hydrogen:**

$$
\%H=\frac{2}{18}\times\frac{0.18}{0.30}\times100=6.67\%
$$

**Nitrogen:**

$$
\%N=\frac{1.4\times0.50\times20.0}{0.30}=46.7\%
$$

**Oxygen:** $100-(20.0+6.67+46.7)=26.6\%$.

Moles per 100 g: C $20.0/12=1.67$; H $6.67/1=6.67$; N $46.7/14=3.33$; O $26.6/16=1.66$.

Divide by 1.66: C 1.0, H 4.0, N 2.0, O 1.0. The empirical formula is **CH₄N₂O**, which is urea, CO(NH₂)₂.

</details>
