## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** set for after you finish the Chemical Bonding lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 5 on Section D. In bonding questions the fastest wins come from three tables — domain counts, hybridisations and the MO filling order — so drill those before attempting the set.

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Formal charge in ozone

The formal charge on the *central* oxygen atom of $\mathrm{O_3}$ (structure O=O–O) is:

- A. $-1$
- B. $0$
- C. $+1$
- D. $+2$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Central oxygen: $V = 6$, one bonding lone pair ($N = 2$), one double bond plus one single bond ($B = 6$, so $\tfrac{B}{2} = 3$). FC $= 6 - (2 + 3) = +1$.

</details>

### 2. Lattice enthalpy ranking

Which solid has the most exothermic lattice enthalpy?

- A. KCl
- B. NaCl
- C. NaF
- D. MgO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** $|U| \propto |z_+z_-|/(r_+ + r_-)$. MgO carries $\pm 2$ charges on compact ions, giving a lattice enthalpy near $-3890\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ versus roughly $-800$ for the $\pm1$ pairs.

</details>

### 3. Sigma and pi in propyne

The number of σ and π bonds in propyne, $\mathrm{CH_3{-}C{\equiv}CH}$, is:

- A. 6 σ, 2 π
- B. 7 σ, 1 π
- C. 8 σ, 0 π
- D. 5 σ, 3 π

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Every bond is one σ: three C–H on the methyl carbon, one C–H on the terminal carbon, one C–C single and one σ within the triple bond = 6. The triple bond adds two π bonds on top.

</details>

### 4. Shape of xenon difluoride

$\mathrm{XeF_2}$ is:

- A. bent
- B. trigonal planar
- C. linear
- D. seesaw

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\mathrm{AX_2E_3}$: five domains, all three lone pairs equatorial, two fluorines opposite each other → linear.

</details>

### 5. Hybridisation in the tri-iodide ion

The hybridisation of the **central** iodine in $\mathrm{I_3^-}$ is:

- A. $sp^3$
- B. $sp^3d$
- C. $sp^3d^2$
- D. $sp^2$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Five domains on the central iodine (two bonds + three lone pairs) → $sp^3d$; the molecular shape is nevertheless linear.

</details>

### 6. Bond order of superoxide

The bond order of $\mathrm{O_2^-}$ is:

- A. 1
- B. 1.5
- C. 2
- D. 2.5

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Bonding $10$, antibonding $5$ ($\sigma^*2s^2$, $\pi^{*}2p^3$): $\tfrac12(10-5) = 1.5$.

</details>

### 7. Diamagnetic diatomic

Which is diamagnetic?

- A. $\mathrm{O_2}$
- B. $\mathrm{B_2}$
- C. $\mathrm{NO}$
- D. $\mathrm{N_2}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** $\mathrm{N_2}$ fills its MOs with all electrons paired (bond order 3, diamagnetic). $\mathrm{O_2}$ (two $\pi^*$ singles), $\mathrm{B_2}$ (two $\pi 2p$ singles) and NO (one $\pi^*$ single) each carry unpaired electrons.

</details>

### 8. Zero dipole moment

Which molecule has zero dipole moment?

- A. $\mathrm{NH_3}$
- B. $\mathrm{H_2O}$
- C. $\mathrm{CCl_4}$
- D. $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Four identical C–Cl dipoles in a perfect tetrahedron cancel vectorially. $\mathrm{NH_3}$, $\mathrm{H_2O}$ (lone-pair asymmetry) and $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$ (a C–H versus three C–Cl) are all asymmetric.

</details>

### 9. Hydride boiling points

Which hydride boils highest?

- A. HF
- B. HCl
- C. $\mathrm{NH_3}$
- D. $\mathrm{H_2O}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Water's two donor H atoms plus two acceptor lone pairs per molecule knit the densest hydrogen-bonded network (bp 373 K) — more than HF's zigzag chains (293 K) or ammonia's (240 K).

</details>

### 10. sp³d² geometry

An $sp^3d^2$ hybridised central atom normally adopts which electron-pair geometry?

- A. trigonal bipyramidal
- B. octahedral
- C. square planar
- D. tetrahedral

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Six hybrids point to the corners of an octahedron. (Molecular shapes can still vary: $\mathrm{SF_6}$ octahedral, $\mathrm{XeF_4}$ square planar from two trans lone pairs.)

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 1. Lone pairs on xenon tetrafluoride

How many lone pairs does the central atom of $\mathrm{XeF_4}$ carry?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$2$. Six domains (four bonds + two lone pairs); the lone pairs sit trans, giving square planar.

</details>

### 2. Unpaired electrons in superoxide

How many unpaired electrons are there in $\mathrm{O_2^-}$?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$1$. Three $\pi^*$ electrons fill one orbital doubly and one singly: one unpaired electron, paramagnetic.

</details>

### 3. Hydrogen bonds per water molecule

In liquid water, each molecule can participate in how many hydrogen bonds (given + received) in the tetrahedral network?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$4$ — two through its own H atoms as donors, two through its lone pairs as acceptors. This is the geometry behind the open ice lattice.

</details>

### 4. Equatorial lone pairs in chlorine trifluoride

How many lone pairs sit in equatorial positions of $\mathrm{ClF_3}$?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$2$. $\mathrm{AX_3E_2}$: the two lone pairs take equatorial slots to avoid unnecessary $90^\circ$ contacts, leaving a T-shaped molecule.

</details>

### 5. Bond order of C₂

What is the bond order of $\mathrm{C_2}$?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$2$. Valence filling with the $\mathrm{N_2}$-type order: $\sigma 2s^2\,\sigma^{*}2s^2\,\pi 2p_x^2\,\pi 2p_y^2$; bond order $= \tfrac12[(2+4)-2] = 2$ (or $\tfrac12(8-4) = 2$ including the cancelling 1s pair). All electrons paired, so it is diamagnetic.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

Mark your answer: (a) both statements true and the reason *correctly explains* the assertion; (b) both true but the reason is *not* the correct explanation; (c) assertion true, reason false; (d) assertion false, reason true.

### 1. Oxygen's magnetism

**Assertion:** $\mathrm{O_2}$ is paramagnetic.
**Reason:** Two electrons occupy separate antibonding $\pi^*2p$ orbitals with parallel spins.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a).** MO theory fills the last two $\mathrm{O_2}$ electrons into the degenerate $\pi^*$ orbitals singly (Hund's rule): two unpaired spins, hence paramagnetism. The reason *is* the molecular explanation.

</details>

### 2. Fluorine and the ammonia angle

**Assertion:** $\mathrm{NF_3}$ has a smaller F–N–F angle than the H–N–H angle in $\mathrm{NH_3}$.
**Reason:** Fluorine's higher electronegativity pulls the N–F bond pairs away from nitrogen, reducing their mutual repulsion.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a).** Electronegativity-driven pull ($102^\circ$ vs $107^\circ$) is exactly the accepted explanation. Note both are about *bond pair* positions, not extra lone pairs — the lone pair count is one in both molecules.

</details>

### 3. Water boils, hydrogen sulfide doesn't

**Assertion:** $\mathrm{H_2S}$ boils far below $\mathrm{H_2O}$.
**Reason:** The intramolecular O–H bond is stronger than the intramolecular S–H bond.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b).** Boiling separates *molecules*, so the relevant quantity is intermolecular hydrogen bonding in water, absent for $\mathrm{H_2S}$. The stated bond-enthalpy comparison is true but beside the point.

</details>

## Section D — 1 matching + 1 multi-statement

### 1. Match species to shape

| List I | List II |
| --- | --- |
| P. $\mathrm{SF_4}$ | 1. trigonal bipyramidal |
| Q. $\mathrm{XeF_2}$ | 2. tetrahedral |
| R. $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ | 3. seesaw |
| S. $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ | 4. linear |

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1. $\mathrm{SF_4}$: five domains, one equatorial lone pair → seesaw. $\mathrm{XeF_2}$: five domains, three equatorial lone pairs → linear. $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$: four bonds, no lone pair → tetrahedral. $\mathrm{PCl_5}$: five bonds, $\mathrm{AX_5}$ → trigonal bipyramidal.

</details>

### 2. Statements about the oxygen-family ions

Consider:

- **(i)** $\mathrm{O_2^+}$ has a higher bond dissociation enthalpy than $\mathrm{O_2}$.
- **(ii)** $\mathrm{O_2^-}$ is diamagnetic.
- **(iii)** The O–O bond length increases in the order $\mathrm{O_2^+ < O_2 < O_2^-}$.
- **(iv)** $\mathrm{O_2^{2-}}$ has the longest O–O bond of this family.

Which statements are correct?

- A. (i) and (ii) only
- B. (i), (iii) and (iv) only
- C. (ii) and (iv) only
- D. all four

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Bond orders run $\mathrm{O_2^+\ 2.5 > O_2\ 2.0 > O_2^-\ 1.5 > O_2^{2-}\ 1.0}$, so bond length increases as given and (i), (iii), (iv) hold. (ii) is false: $\mathrm{O_2^-}$ has three $\pi^*$ electrons, one of them unpaired, so it is paramagnetic.

</details>
