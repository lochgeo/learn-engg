This is a **50-minute, no-notes** paper for after you finish all six concept lessons and the mastery check. Budget roughly **15 minutes** for Section A, **15** for Section B, **12** for Section C and **8** for Section D. If any single calculation exceeds 90 seconds, mark it and move on — the mole concept rewards the fastest students precisely because every step is one bridge.

Before writing anything, name the tool: which term converts to moles, which recipe fixes the ratio, which concentration term matches the given denominator. Most wrong answers here come from using a ratio whose denominator does not match the question (grams where moles are asked, solution where solvent is meant).

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Moles to mass

The mass of $0.5$ mol of oxygen gas (O2) is:

- A. $8\ \text{g}$
- B. $16\ \text{g}$
- C. $32\ \text{g}$
- D. $64\ \text{g}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $m=nM=0.5\times32=16\ \text{g}$. Option A would be right for $0.5$ mol of O **atoms** — the particle present is O2.
</details>

### 2. Counting atoms

The number of atoms in $4.25\ \text{g}$ of ammonia is closest to:

- A. $1.5\times10^{23}$
- B. $2.4\times10^{23}$
- C. $6.0\times10^{23}$
- D. $2.4\times10^{24}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $n=4.25/17=0.25$ mol; molecules $=1.5\times10^{23}$; atoms $=4\times1.5\times10^{23}=6.0\times10^{23}$.
</details>

### 3. Empirical formula from percentages

A compound is $50\%$ S and $50\%$ O by mass. Its empirical formula is:

- A. SO
- B. SO2
- C. SO3
- D. S2O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Per $100\ \text{g}$: S $=50/32=1.56$ mol; O $=50/16=3.13$ mol; ratio $1:2$, giving SO2.
</details>

### 4. Molarity from mass and volume

$4\ \text{g}$ of NaOH is dissolved and made up to $250\ \text{mL}$ of solution. The molarity is:

- A. $0.1\ \text{M}$
- B. $0.2\ \text{M}$
- C. $0.4\ \text{M}$
- D. $1.6\ \text{M}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $n=4/40=0.1$ mol; $M=0.1/0.250=0.4\ \text{mol/L}$.
</details>

### 5. Equal molecule counts

Which sample contains the same number of molecules as $11\ \text{g}$ of CO2?

- A. $2\ \text{g}$ of H2
- B. $0.5\ \text{g}$ of H2
- C. $4\ \text{g}$ of He
- D. $16\ \text{g}$ of O2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $11/44=0.25$ mol of CO2; $0.5$ g H2 $=0.25$ mol too. Check the distractors: $2$ g H2 $=1$ mol; $4$ g He $=1$ mol; $16$ g O2 $=0.5$ mol.
</details>

### 6. Naming the law

Every pure sample of magnesium oxide, from any source, contains magnesium and oxygen in the mass ratio $3:2$. This observation is the law of:

- A. conservation of mass
- B. definite proportions
- C. multiple proportions
- D. combining volumes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** One compound, one fixed ratio — definite proportions. Multiple proportions would need **two** compounds of the same pair being compared.
</details>

### 7. Limiting reagent in moles

$2$ mol of H2 is sparked with $2$ mol of O2: $\mathrm{2\,H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2\,H_2O}$. The maximum mass of water formed is:

- A. $18\ \text{g}$
- B. $36\ \text{g}$
- C. $54\ \text{g}$
- D. $72\ \text{g}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Per coefficient: H2 $=2/2=1$; O2 $=2/1=2$, so hydrogen limits. Water $=2$ mol $=36\ \text{g}$, with $1$ mol O2 left over.
</details>

### 8. Gas volume to moles

The number of moles in $5.6\ \text{L}$ of nitrogen at STP is:

- A. $0.1$
- B. $0.25$
- C. $0.5$
- D. $2.5$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $n=5.6/22.4=0.25$ mol.
</details>

### 9. What temperature changes

Which concentration term changes with temperature?

- A. molarity
- B. molality
- C. mole fraction
- D. mass percentage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Only molarity carries solution volume in its denominator, and volume drifts with temperature; masses do not.
</details>

### 10. Multiple proportions

For a fixed mass of carbon ($12\ \text{g}$), the oxygen taken up in CO and in CO2 stands in the ratio:

- A. $1:1$
- B. $1:2$
- C. $2:1$
- D. $1:4$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $16\ \text{g}$ in CO against $32\ \text{g}$ in CO2: a whole-number ratio $1:2$.
</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Decomposition mass

What mass of quicklime (CaO) is obtained on complete decomposition of $50\ \text{g}$ of pure limestone (CaCO3)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n=50/100=0.5$ mol; $m=0.5\times56=28\ \text{g}$ of CaO.
</details>

### 12. Hydrogen at STP

Find the volume, in litres at STP, occupied by $4\ \text{g}$ of hydrogen gas.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n=4/2=2$ mol; $V=2\times22.4=44.8\ \text{L}$.
</details>

### 13. Molality

$18\ \text{g}$ of glucose ($M=180$) is dissolved in $250\ \text{g}$ of water. Find the molality.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n=0.1$ mol; $m=0.1/0.250=0.4\ \text{mol/kg}$.
</details>

### 14. Percentage yield

Decomposing $10\ \text{g}$ of CaCO3 should give $5.6\ \text{g}$ of CaO. A student collects $5.04\ \text{g}$. What is the percentage yield?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
\frac{5.04}{5.6}\times100=90\%.
$$
</details>

### 15. Same atom count, heavier element

What mass of silver (Ag $=108$) contains the same number of atoms as $4\ \text{g}$ of oxygen **atoms**?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Oxygen atoms: $4/16=0.25$ mol. Same moles of silver: $0.25\times108=27\ \text{g}$.
</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

Choose the option: **(a)** both statements true and R explains A; **(b)** both true but R does not explain A; **(c)** A true, R false; **(d)** A false, R true.

### 16. Equal volumes

**A:** One mole of any ideal gas occupies $22.4\ \text{L}$ at STP.
**R:** At the same temperature and pressure, equal numbers of molecules of all ideal gases occupy equal volumes.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a).** The fixed volume per mole is exactly Avogadro's hypothesis in operational form: volume scales with molecule count, so equal moles give equal volumes regardless of the gas. R is the reason A holds.
</details>

### 17. Molality on heating

**A:** The molality of a stored solution decreases slightly when it is warmed.
**R:** Molality uses the mass of solvent in its denominator, and mass does not change with temperature.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d).** A is false — molality is the temperature-independent term precisely because its denominator is a mass. R is a true statement. The term that drifts on warming is molarity.
</details>

### 18. Why mass is safe

**A:** In the reaction of hydrogen with oxygen to form water, the total mass before and after is the same.
**R:** Chemical reactions rearrange atoms; no atom is created or destroyed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a).** Conservation of mass follows directly from atom conservation — Dalton's founding insight. R explains A.
</details>

## Section D — matching and multi-statement

### 19. Match the concentration term

Match each term with its correct definition:

| Term | |
| --- | --- |
| (a) molarity | |
| (b) molality | |
| (c) mole fraction | |
| (d) mass percentage | |

(i) moles of solute per kilogram of solvent
(ii) moles of solute per litre of solution
(iii) mass of solute per 100 parts by mass of solution
(iv) moles of the species divided by total moles of all species

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a)–(ii), (b)–(i), (c)–(iv), (d)–(iii). The two easily swapped pairs are (a)/(b) — solution versus solvent — and (c)/(d), which differ in whether the denominator counts moles or mass.
</details>

### 20. Which statements hold?

Consider:

- **I.** One mole of CH4 has a mass of $16\ \text{g}$ and contains $6.022\times10^{23}$ molecules.
- **II.** $44\ \text{g}$ of CO2 contains $6.022\times10^{23}$ atoms.
- **III.** $22.4\ \text{L}$ of oxygen at STP has a mass of $32\ \text{g}$.
- **IV.** The empirical formula of C6H12O6 is CH2O.

Which statements are correct?

- A. I and III only
- B. I, III and IV
- C. II and IV only
- D. I, II, III and IV

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** I is standard ($16$ g, $6.022\times10^{23}$ molecules). III: $22.4$ L at STP is one mole, $32\ \text{g}$ for O2. IV: divide the formula by 6 to get CH2O. II is false: $44\ \text{g}$ of CO2 is one mole of *molecules*, i.e. $3\times6.022\times10^{23}=\,1.8\times10^{24}$ atoms (C + 2O), not $6.022\times10^{23}$.

</details>
