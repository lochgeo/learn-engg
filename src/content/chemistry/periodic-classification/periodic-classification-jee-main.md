## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 10 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 10 on Section D, leaving 5 to review marks and correct slips. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; a periodic-trend paper is decided by choosing the right comparison, not by long calculation.

The goal is not only the answer. Before touching the options, name the tool: configuration, radius rule, ionisation, electron gain, electronegativity, or oxide character. Most wrong answers here come from using the right number on the wrong question.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Rule to reach for | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position asked from a configuration | Period = highest $n$; block = last-entering subshell | d-block group counts $s$ + $d$ electrons; He is group 18 |
| Size comparison | Same electron count first, then rank by $Z$ | Never compare a van der Waals with a covalent radius |
| Ease of losing an electron | Ionisation enthalpy: size, $Z_{eff}$, shielding, configuration stability | Watch the Be–B/Mg–Al and N–O/P–S dips |
| Ease of gaining an electron | Electron gain enthalpy, signed | Chlorine beats fluorine; a second gain always costs |
| Pull on shared pairs | Electronegativity (Pauling unless told otherwise) | Not the same experiment as electron gain enthalpy |
| Oxide or hydride behaviour | Metallic character: left basic → right acidic | $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ and $\mathrm{BeO}$ are amphoteric |
| Second-period oddity | No vacant $d$ orbital; small size; strong $\pi$ bonding | $\mathrm{NCl_5}$, $\mathrm{CCl_6^{2-}}$, $\mathrm{NF_5}$ are impossible |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If you ranked $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$ smaller than $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$, credited fluorine with the most negative electron gain enthalpy, or allowed nitrogen a pentachloride, stop and rebuild from the rule — each of those is a specific module's headline correction, not a slip.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. The ordering variable

Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass. The modern periodic law orders them by:

- A. atomic density
- B. atomic number
- C. valency
- D. electronegativity

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Moseley's X-ray work fixed atomic number as the order parameter: $\sqrt{\nu}=a(Z-b)$ with no mass dependence.

</details>

### 2. An element's address

An element has the configuration $[\mathrm{Ne}]\,3s^2 3p^3$. Its period and group are:

- A. 3 and 13
- B. 3 and 15
- C. 3 and 5
- D. 2 and 15

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Highest $n = 3$, so period 3. Outer electrons $2 + 3 = 5$ in the p-block, so group $10 + 5 = 15$. The element is phosphorus.

</details>

### 3. Largest radius

Among Na, Mg, Al and Si, the largest atomic radius belongs to:

- A. Na
- B. Mg
- C. Al
- D. Si

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Across period 3 the radius shrinks as $Z_{eff}$ rises within the same shell: 186 pm (Na) down to 117 pm (Si).

</details>

### 4. Correct ionisation order

Which comparison is correct?

- A. $\Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{Al}) > \Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{Mg})$
- B. $\Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{S}) > \Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{P})$
- C. $\Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{N}) > \Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{O})$
- D. $\Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{B}) > \Delta_i H_1(\mathrm{Be})$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The N–O dip: nitrogen's half-filled $2p^3$ is specially stable, while oxygen's paired fourth $2p$ electron adds repulsion (1402 vs 1314 kJ mol⁻¹). Options A, B and D are each a famous dip read backwards: Mg beats Al (738 vs 578), P beats S (1012 vs 1000), Be beats B (900 vs 801).

</details>

### 5. Most willing acceptor

The most negative electron gain enthalpy among F, Cl, Br and I belongs to:

- A. F
- B. Cl
- C. Br
- D. I

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Chlorine's roomy $3p$ shell accepts an electron with less interelectronic repulsion than fluorine's cramped $2p$: $-349$ against $-328\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$.

</details>

### 6. Amphoteric oxide

Which oxide reacts with both aqueous HCl and aqueous NaOH?

- A. $\mathrm{Na_2O}$
- B. $\mathrm{MgO}$
- C. $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$
- D. $\mathrm{SO_3}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\mathrm{Al_2O_3 + 6HCl \rightarrow 2AlCl_3 + 3H_2O}$ and $\mathrm{Al_2O_3 + 2NaOH + 3H_2O \rightarrow 2Na[Al(OH)_4]}$: amphoteric, on the metal–non-metal boundary.

</details>

### 7. Block identity

Which of these is a p-block element?

- A. Cr ($[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^1$)
- B. Ga ($[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}4s^2 4p^1$)
- C. Gd ($[\mathrm{Xe}]\,4f^7 5d^1 6s^2$)
- D. Fe ($[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6 4s^2$)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Gallium's last electron enters $4p$: p-block, group 13, period 3 in the modern count after the d-block contraction. Cr and Fe are d-block; Gd is f-block.

</details>

### 8. Smallest ion

Among $\mathrm{Na^+}$, $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$, $\mathrm{F^-}$ and $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$ (all with 10 electrons), the smallest is:

- A. $\mathrm{Na^+}$
- B. $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$
- C. $\mathrm{F^-}$
- D. $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Same 10-electron cloud in each, so ranking is strictly by nuclear charge: $Z = 12$ is the largest pull, giving $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ the tightest cloud ($72\ \text{pm}$).

</details>

### 9. Diagonal partner

Which pair exemplifies a diagonal relationship?

- A. Na–K
- B. Li–Mg
- C. B–C
- D. C–N

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Li–Mg (with Be–Al and B–Si) is a classic diagonal pair: one period down and one group right, size and electronegativity changes roughly cancel.

</details>

### 10. Period arithmetic

How many elements does period 6 contain?

- A. 18
- B. 24
- C. 32
- D. 30

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $6s$ (2) + $4f$ (14) + $5d$ (10) + $6p$ (6) = 32, from caesium to radon.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Effective nuclear charge, Slater-style

Using the shielding estimate $\sigma$ for the outermost electron of potassium (electrons in the $(n-1)$ shell shield $0.85$ each; deeper shells shield $1.00$ each), find $Z_{eff} = Z - \sigma$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Shielding of the $4s$ electron: $8 \times 0.85$ from $3s^2 3p^6$, plus $8 \times 1.00$ from $2s^2 2p^6$, plus $2 \times 1.00$ from $1s^2$: $\sigma = 6.8 + 8 + 2 = 16.8$. Hence $Z_{eff} = 19 - 16.8 = 2.2$. The same calculation for sodium's $3s$ electron gives $2.2$ as well — nearly constant $Z_{eff}$ down a group is exactly why size and ionisation trends run as they do.

</details>

### 12. Spending to strip beryllium

Beryllium's successive ionisation enthalpies are 899, 1757, 14 849 and 21 007 $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. Find the total energy to remove all four outer electrons from one mole of gaseous Be atoms, in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ (three significant figures).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$899 + 1757 + 14\,849 + 21\,007 = 38\,512 \approx 3.85 \times 10^4\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. Sanity check: the third value alone already jumps by a factor near 8 over the second — the $1s^2$ core after two removals — which is why the total is dominated by the last two terms.

</details>

### 13. Fingerprint of an element

An element's successive ionisation enthalpies are 786, 1587, 3232, 4355 and 16 091 $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. Which group does it belong to?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The step ratios are about 2.0, 2.0, 1.3 and then 3.7. The jump after the fourth removal means the fifth electron must leave the noble-gas core, so the element has four valence electrons: group 14 (the values are silicon's).

</details>

### 14. Mulliken electronegativity

For chlorine, $\Delta_i H_1 = 12.97\ \text{eV}$ and the electron gain releases $3.61\ \text{eV}$ per atom. Compute the Mulliken electronegativity $\chi_{\mathrm{M}} = \frac12(\Delta_i H_1 + \Delta_{eg}H)$ in eV (two significant figures).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\chi_{\mathrm{M}} = \frac{1}{2}(12.97 + 3.61) = 8.29 \approx 8.3\ \text{eV}$. This is a Mulliken number, not a Pauling one — the scales differ by roughly a factor of three in size but preserve the ranking.

</details>

### 15. Counting a period

How many elements sit in the sixth period of the modern periodic table?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$32$: two $6s$ elements (Cs, Ba), fourteen lanthanoids filling $4f$, ten 5d metals (Lu across Hg in the standard count) and six $6p$ elements — caesium ($Z=55$) to radon ($Z=86$).

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

For each question choose: **(a)** both A and R true, and R is the correct explanation of A; **(b)** both A and R true, but R is not the correct explanation of A; **(c)** A is true, R is false; **(d)** A is false, R is true.

### 16. Beryllium versus boron

**A:** The first ionisation enthalpy of beryllium is higher than that of boron.
**R:** Beryllium's outermost electron occupies a penetrating, fully filled $2s$ subshell, whereas boron's newest electron sits in a $2p$ orbital.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a).** Both statements are true and the reason is exactly the mechanism ($900$ vs $801\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$): the $2s$ electron hugs the nucleus, is already in a stable filled subshell, and the $2p$ electron — although adding to $Z$ — starts a new, less-penetrating orbital.

</details>

### 17. Two fluorine records

**A:** Chlorine has a more negative electron gain enthalpy than fluorine.
**R:** Chlorine is the most electronegative element on the Pauling scale.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c).** The assertion is true ($-349$ vs $-328\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$), but the reason is false: fluorine (4.0), not chlorine (3.2), tops the Pauling scale. The real cause of fluorine's less negative enthalpy is the interelectronic repulsion in its cramped $2p$ shell — and the two quantities are different experiments anyway.

</details>

### 18. Gallium vs aluminium

**A:** Gallium is more electronegative than aluminium.
**R:** Gallium's outermost electrons occupy the $4p$ shell, whereas aluminium's occupy the $3p$ shell.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b).** The assertion is true (about 1.8 vs 1.6): gallium's $3d$ electrons shield the nucleus poorly, so the effective pull on its $4p$ electrons stays unusually high — the same d-contraction that shrinks Hf to Zr's radius. The reason as stated is merely a true factual difference in shells; it identifies the subshell but not the mechanism, so it does not *explain* the inversion.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Match the address

Match each configuration with its correct position:

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| (a) $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6 4s^2$ | (i) group 18, period 1 |
| (b) $[\mathrm{Ne}]\,3s^2 3p^1$ | (ii) group 13, period 3 |
| (c) $[\mathrm{Xe}]\,4f^1 5d^1 6s^2$ | (iii) group 8, period 4 |
| (d) $1s^2$ | (iv) period 6, f-block |

Options:

- A. a-iii, b-ii, c-iv, d-i
- B. a-i, b-ii, c-iii, d-iv
- C. a-iii, b-iv, c-ii, d-i
- D. a-ii, b-iii, c-iv, d-i

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** (a) iron: outer electrons $6+2 = 8$ in the d-block → group 8, period 4. (b) aluminium: p-block, $10+3 = 13$, period 3. (c) cerium: electron enters $4f$ with outermost $6s$ → period 6 f-block. (d) helium: $1s^2$ but noble-gas behaviour → group 18, period 1.

</details>

### 20. Period 3 oxide statements

Which of the following statements are correct?

- **I.** Among $\mathrm{Na_2O}$, $\mathrm{MgO}$ and $\mathrm{SO_3}$, the most basic is $\mathrm{Na_2O}$.
- **II.** $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$ dissolves in both aqueous HCl and aqueous NaOH.
- **III.** $\mathrm{Cl_2O_7}$ reacts with water to give a strong acid.
- **IV.** $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ is a basic oxide.

- A. I and II only
- B. I, II and III only
- C. II and III only
- D. I, II, III and IV

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** I holds: basic character tracks metallic character, and sodium is the most metallic of the three. II holds: amphoteric aluminium oxide (two equations in module 6 of this chapter). III holds: $\mathrm{Cl_2O_7 + H_2O \rightarrow 2HClO_4}$, strongly acidic. IV is false: silicon sits right of the amphoteric boundary, and $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ is a weakly acidic, covalent oxide.

</details>
