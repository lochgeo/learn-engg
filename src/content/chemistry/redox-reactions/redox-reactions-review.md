## Which tool do I reach for?

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| "Which species is oxidised/reduced?" | Assign oxidation numbers, compare start and finish |
| "Identify the oxidising/reducing agent" | Agent is reduced / oxidised respectively; look for the unchanged spectator |
| "Classify the reaction type" | Count elements changing and their directions; one element, two directions = disproportionation |
| "Balance the equation (molecular)" | Oxidation-number method: equalise total increases and decreases |
| "Balance in acid or base (ionic)" | Half-reactions: O with $\mathrm{H_2O}$, H with $\mathrm{H^+}$ or $\mathrm{OH^-}$, charge with electrons |
| "Titration volume or molarity needed" | Balanced ratio, then $n_1 = n_2$ scaled by coefficients |
| "Equivalent or normality asked" | $n$-factor first: $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ 5 (acid) / 1 (mild base), $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$ 6, oxalate 2 |
| "Fraction of reagent actually redox-active" | Compare species consumed with species changing oxidation number |

## Mastery check

Work each question on paper before opening the answer.

### 1. Reading the transfer

In $\mathrm{2Na + 2H_2O \rightarrow 2NaOH + H_2}$, identify the couple oxidised and the couple reduced, and say what the water does.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Sodium goes $0 \rightarrow +1$ (loses an electron: oxidation, so sodium is the reducing agent). Hydrogen in water is at $+1$ and drops to $0$ in $\mathrm{H_2}$ (gains an electron: reduction, so water is the oxidising agent). Oxygen stays $-2$ throughout. Water is not merely present — it is the electron acceptor.

</details>

### 2. Oxidation numbers across an interhalogen

Assign oxidation numbers in $\mathrm{ICl_3}$, $\mathrm{IF_5}$ and $\mathrm{I_2}$.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{ICl_3}$: chlorine at $-1$ each (iodine is less electronegative than chlorine), so iodine is $+3$. $\mathrm{IF_5}$: fluorine outranks iodine at $-1$ each, so iodine is $+5$. $\mathrm{I_2}$: element, so 0. In this family the oxidation number follows electronegativity, not position in the periodic table — the reason F is the only universal $-1$.

</details>

### 3. Spot the non-redox partner

Which species does not change oxidation number when acidified permanganate oxidises sulphite: $\mathrm{Mn}$, $\mathrm{S}$, $\mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{H}$?

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{Mn}$ falls $+7 \rightarrow +2$ and $\mathrm{S}$ climbs $+4 \rightarrow +6$. But the solvent oxygens stay $-2$ and the hydrogens stay $+1$ — they appear as $\mathrm{H^+}$, $\mathrm{H_2O}$ and $\mathrm{OH^-}$ only to balance atoms. A close inspection of any balancing question should begin by separating the electron-carrying atoms from the water scaffolding.

</details>

### 4. Disproportionation or not?

State whether $\mathrm{2NO_2 + H_2O \rightarrow HNO_2 + HNO_3}$ is disproportionation and justify it with numbers.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

In $\mathrm{NO_2}$ nitrogen is $+4$. It leaves as $+3$ (in $\mathrm{HNO_2}$) and as $+5$ (in $\mathrm{HNO_3}$). One element, one starting oxidation state, two destinations — yes, disproportionation. The intermediate state $+4$ is essential: nitrogen cannot split from $+2$ or below into a $+5$ product in a single step like this without also reversing the direction elsewhere.

</details>

### 5. Balancing in acid

Balance $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + Sn^{2+} + H^+ \rightarrow Cr^{3+} + Sn^{4+}}$ and quote the electrons transferred per dichromate ion.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Reduction half: $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 14H^+ + 6e^- \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 7H_2O}$. Oxidation half: $\mathrm{Sn^{2+} \rightarrow Sn^{4+} + 2e^-}$. LCM of 6 and 2 is 6, so multiply the tin half by 3:

$$
\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3Sn^{2+} + 14H^+ \rightarrow 2Cr^{3+} + 3Sn^{4+} + 7H_2O}
$$

Charge check: left $(-2) + 6 + 14 = +18$; right $6 + 12 = +18$ ✓. Six electrons per dichromate.

</details>

### 6. Balancing in basic medium

Balance $\mathrm{BiO_3^- + Sn(OH)_3^- \rightarrow Bi(OH)_3 + Sn(OH)_6^{2-}}$ in alkaline solution (stannite is oxidised to stannate by bismuthate) and check charge both ways.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Reduction half: $\mathrm{BiO_3^- \rightarrow Bi(OH)_3}$, bismuth $+5 \rightarrow +3$ (2 electrons in):

$$
\mathrm{BiO_3^- + 3H_2O + 2e^- \rightarrow Bi(OH)_3 + 3OH^-}
$$

Atom check: O $3+3 = 6 : 3+3 = 6$; H 6 : 6; charge $-1 - 2 = -3$ against $-3$ ✓.

Oxidation half: $\mathrm{Sn(OH)_3^- \rightarrow Sn(OH)_6^{2-}}$, tin $+2 \rightarrow +4$ (2 electrons out):

$$
\mathrm{Sn(OH)_3^- + 3OH^- \rightarrow Sn(OH)_6^{2-} + 2e^-}
$$

Atom check: O 6 : 6; H 6 : 6; charge left $-4$ against right $-2 - 2 = -4$ ✓. Electrons already equal at 2, so add directly:

$$
\mathrm{BiO_3^- + Sn(OH)_3^- + 3H_2O + 3OH^- \rightarrow Bi(OH)_3 + Sn(OH)_6^{2-} + 3OH^-}
$$

Cancel $3\ \mathrm{OH^-}$ appearing on both sides of the summed half-reactions by subtracting from both the left and the right amounts present — the final form is:

$$
\mathrm{BiO_3^- + Sn(OH)_3^- + 3H_2O \rightarrow Bi(OH)_3 + Sn(OH)_6^{2-}}
$$

Charge check: left $-1 - 1 = -2$; right $-2$ ✓.

</details>

### 7. Foreign reagent, leftover fraction

In $\mathrm{MnO_2 + 4HCl \rightarrow MnCl_2 + Cl_2 + 2H_2O}$, what fraction of the HCl performs the electron transfer, and what mass of $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ comes from $36.5\ \text{g}$ of HCl (one mole)?

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Of the four HCl consumed, only two provide the chloride ions that climb $-1 \rightarrow 0$; two end as spectators in $\mathrm{MnCl_2}$. Fraction $= \tfrac12$. One mole of HCl therefore contributes $\tfrac12$ mole of the redox-active chloride, which corresponds to $\tfrac{1}{4}$ mole of $\mathrm{Cl_2}$ (two Cl atoms per molecule), or $0.25 \times 71 = 17.75\ \text{g}$ of chlorine gas.

</details>

### 8. Titration arithmetic

$15.0\ \text{mL}$ of hydrogen peroxide solution is diluted to $150\ \text{mL}$. A $25.0\ \text{mL}$ aliquot needs $22.4\ \text{mL}$ of $0.016\ \text{M}$ $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ in acidic medium: $\mathrm{2MnO_4^- + 5H_2O_2 + 6H^+ \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 5O_2 + 8H_2O}$. Find the molarity of the original peroxide solution.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Permanganate moles in the titre: $0.016 \times 0.0224 = 3.58\times10^{-4}\ \text{mol}$. Ratio $2:5$:

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2O_2}) = \tfrac52 \times 3.58\times10^{-4} = 8.96\times10^{-4}\ \text{mol}
$$

in a $25.0\ \text{mL}$ aliquot, so the *diluted* solution is $0.0358\ \text{M}$. The tenfold dilution multiplies back to $0.358\ \text{M}$ in the original solution.

</details>

### 9. Equivalents per gram

One metal has equivalent mass $20$; another has equivalent mass $56$. Which exchanges more electron equivalents per gram: $1\ \text{g}$ of the first metal reacting, or $1\ \text{g}$ of the second metal depositing?

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Equivalents per gram equal $1/\text{equivalent mass}$: $\tfrac{1}{20} = 0.05$ equivalents per gram for the first metal; $\tfrac{1}{56} \approx 0.0179$ per gram for the second. The lighter-metal choice exchanges roughly $2.8$ times more electron equivalents per gram — equivalent mass is just the mass that carries one mole of electrons, which is why the two numbers differ by their ratio $56/20$.

</details>

### 10. Diagnose the mistake

A student balances the permanganate–sulphite reaction in basic medium and writes $\mathrm{MnO_4^- + SO_3^{2-} + 2H^+ \rightarrow Mn^{2+} + SO_4^{2-} + H_2O}$. Give three reasons this cannot be right.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

First, permanganate has been taken all the way to $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$, a five-electron change, which needs an acidic medium — but $\mathrm{H^+}$ is forbidden in a basic reaction. Second, the sulphur change is a two-electron oxidation, so the ledger $5 : 2$ cannot close with the single permanganate shown. Third, with $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ on the right the solution becomes acidic on its own, contradicting the stated basicity. The correct basic-medium answer keeps manganese at $+4$ as $\mathrm{MnO_2}$ and carries the coefficients $2\ \mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ and $3\ \mathrm{SO_3^{2-}}$, as derived in the balancing module.

</details>

## Self-check

You are ready for the timed paper when you can:

- assign oxidation numbers to every atom in any formula, including peroxides and fractional averages such as $\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$;
- name the oxidising agent, reducing agent, redox couples and spectators from a full equation;
- classify a reaction as combination, decomposition, displacement, disproportionation or comproportionation from a one-line description;
- balance any redox skeleton in acidic or basic medium and pass a charge audit;
- quote $n$-factors for $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$, $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$, oxalate, $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$, and titrate numbers with the $1:5$, $2:5$ and $1:6$ ratios without notes;
- separate endpoint from equivalence point and name the indicator for each common titrant.

If any bullet still needs notes, revisit that module first — the timed set punishes recalled-but-not-understood steps.
