## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Use the standard values: $c=3\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$, near point $D=25\ \text{cm}$, and refractive indices as given. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 5 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one calculation consume the paper.

The goal is not only an answer. Before writing an equation, name the system and choose the tool: mirror, lens, TIR, prism or instrument.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spherical mirror image | $1/v+1/u=1/f$, $f=R/2$ | Fix signs: real object $u<0$; concave $f<0$, convex $f>0$. |
| Thin lens image | $1/v-1/u=1/f$ | Converging $f>0$, diverging $f<0$; $m=v/u$. |
| Lens power | $P=1/f$ (metres), in dioptres | Powers in contact add. |
| Critical angle / TIR | $\sin C=n_{\text{rarer}}/n_{\text{denser}}$ | Denser-to-rarer travel required; $i>C$. |
| Apparent depth | real depth$/n$ | Near-normal viewing. |
| Prism deviation (thin) | $\delta=(n-1)A$ | $A$ in radians for the formula in radians; degrees give degrees. |
| Minimum deviation | $n=\sin((A+\delta_m)/2)/\sin(A/2)$ | Symmetric passage. |
| Simple microscope | $M=1+D/f$ or $D/f$ | Image at near point or infinity. |
| Compound microscope | $M\approx (L/f_o)(1+D/f_e)$ | $L$ is tube length. |
| Telescope | $M=f_o/f_e$ | Normal adjustment: final image at infinity. |
| Diffraction limit | $\theta_{\min}=1.22\lambda/D$ | $D$ is aperture diameter. |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">
If your answer gives a real image from a convex mirror, a virtual image from a single diverging lens of a real object at any position, or a critical angle with sine greater than 1, stop and revisit the setup. A numerical answer with the right unit can still come from the wrong convention.
</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Concave mirror image

A concave mirror has focal length $20\ \text{cm}$. An object is placed $30\ \text{cm}$ in front of it. The image is:

- A. real, inverted and diminished
- B. real, inverted and magnified
- C. virtual, erect and magnified
- D. virtual, erect and diminished

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $u=-30\ \text{cm}$, $f=-20\ \text{cm}$: $1/v=-1/20+1/30=-1/60$, so $v=-60\ \text{cm}$. Magnification $m=-v/u=-(-60)/(-30)=-2$. Real, inverted, magnified.

</details>

### 2. Lens combination

Two thin lenses of powers $+4\ \text{D}$ and $-1\ \text{D}$ are placed in contact. The focal length of the combination is:

- A. $25\ \text{cm}$
- B. $33.3\ \text{cm}$
- C. $50\ \text{cm}$
- D. $100\ \text{cm}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $P=+3\ \text{D}$, so $f=1/3\ \text{m}=33.3\ \text{cm}$.

</details>

### 3. Critical angle

The critical angle for a water–air boundary ($n_{\text{water}}=4/3$) is closest to:

- A. $37^\circ$
- B. $42^\circ$
- C. $49^\circ$
- D. $60^\circ$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\sin C=3/4$, $C=\sin^{-1}(0.75)\approx 48.6^\circ$.

</details>

### 4. Prism refractive index

A prism of angle $60^\circ$ gives minimum deviation $30^\circ$. Its refractive index is:

- A. $1.225$
- B. $1.414$
- C. $1.500$
- D. $1.732$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $n=\sin 45^\circ/\sin 30^\circ=0.7071/0.5=1.414$.

</details>

### 5. Telescope magnification

An astronomical telescope has $f_o=100\ \text{cm}$ and $f_e=5\ \text{cm}$. Its magnification in normal adjustment is:

- A. $5$
- B. $20$
- C. $25$
- D. $100$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $M=f_o/f_e=100/5=20$.

</details>

### 6. Apparent depth

A coin rests $12\ \text{cm}$ below the surface of water ($n=4/3$). Viewed normally from air, it appears at depth:

- A. $8\ \text{cm}$
- B. $9\ \text{cm}$
- C. $12\ \text{cm}$
- D. $16\ \text{cm}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Apparent depth $=12/(4/3)=9\ \text{cm}$.

</details>

### 7. Lensmaker result

A biconvex lens is made of glass of index $1.5$ with equal radii of curvature $R$. Its focal length is:

- A. $R/2$
- B. $R$
- C. $2R$
- D. $R/4$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $1/f=(1.5-1)(1/R-1/(-R))=0.5(2/R)=1/R$, so $f=R$.

</details>

### 8. Scattering dependence

Rayleigh scattering intensity varies with wavelength as:

- A. $\lambda^{-2}$
- B. $\lambda^{-3}$
- C. $\lambda^{-4}$
- D. $\lambda^{-1}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Rayleigh scattering $\propto 1/\lambda^4$.

</details>

### 9. Simple microscope

A magnifying glass of focal length $5\ \text{cm}$ has maximum angular magnification (image at near point, $D=25\ \text{cm}$):

- A. $5$
- B. $6$
- C. $7$
- D. $10$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $M=1+D/f=1+25/5=6$.

</details>

### 10. Resolving power

Which change most directly increases the resolving power of a microscope objective?

- A. Increasing its focal length
- B. Using oil immersion between lens and specimen
- C. Decreasing the wavelength of light is not possible, so none
- D. Reducing the tube length

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Resolving power $\propto n\sin\theta/\lambda$; oil immersion raises $n$ and hence the numerical aperture.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Concave mirror image distance

A concave mirror has radius of curvature $60\ \text{cm}$. An object is $45\ \text{cm}$ in front of it. Find the image distance in cm (magnitude).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$f=-30\ \text{cm}$, $u=-45\ \text{cm}$. $1/v=-1/30+1/45=-1/90$, so $v=-90\ \text{cm}$. Magnitude $90\ \text{cm}$.

</details>

### 12. Converging lens image distance

A converging lens of focal length $15\ \text{cm}$ has an object $20\ \text{cm}$ in front. Find the image distance in cm.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$1/v=1/15-1/20=1/60$, so $v=60\ \text{cm}$.

</details>

### 13. Critical angle of glass

For glass of refractive index $1.5$ to air, find the critical angle in degrees (nearest integer).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\sin C=1/1.5=2/3$, $C=\sin^{-1}(0.6667)\approx 41.8^\circ\approx 42^\circ$.

</details>

### 14. Telescope magnification

A telescope has $f_o=120\ \text{cm}$ and $f_e=4\ \text{cm}$. Find its magnification.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$M=f_o/f_e=120/4=30$.

</details>

### 15. Lens focal length

A biconvex lens has $n=1.5$ and radius of curvature $20\ \text{cm}$ for both surfaces. Find its focal length in cm.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$1/f=(1.5-1)(1/20-1/(-20))=0.5(0.1)=0.05\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, so $f=20\ \text{cm}$.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

### 16. Assertion–reason

**Assertion (A):** A concave mirror can form a virtual image of a real object.

**Reason (R):** When the object is placed between the pole and the focus of a concave mirror, the image is virtual, erect and magnified.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both are true and R explains A.

</details>

### 17. Assertion–reason

**Assertion (A):** Total internal reflection can occur when light travels from a rarer medium to a denser medium.

**Reason (R):** Total internal reflection requires the angle of incidence to exceed the critical angle.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** A is false (TIR needs denser-to-rarer travel); R is true.

</details>

### 18. Assertion–reason

**Assertion (A):** The sky appears blue.

**Reason (R):** Rayleigh scattering intensity is proportional to $1/\lambda^4$, so shorter (blue) wavelengths scatter much more than longer (red) ones.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both true and R explains A.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Match the instrument to its magnification formula

Match each instrument in column I with the correct expression in column II.

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| P. Simple microscope | 1. $f_o/f_e$ |
| Q. Compound microscope | 2. $1+D/f$ (near point) |
| R. Astronomical telescope | 3. $(L/f_o)(1+D/f_e)$ |
| S. Diffraction limit | 4. $1.22\lambda/D$ |

- A. P-2, Q-3, R-1, S-4
- B. P-3, Q-2, R-1, S-4
- C. P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4
- D. P-1, Q-3, R-2, S-4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** P-2, Q-3, R-1, S-4.

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement

Which of the following statements are correct?

1. Power of a lens in dioptres equals $1/f$ with $f$ in metres.
2. A convex lens has negative power.
3. When thin lenses are in contact, their powers add.
4. A single diverging lens can produce a real image of a real object.

- A. 1 and 3 only
- B. 1, 2 and 3
- C. 2 and 4 only
- D. All four

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is false (convex lenses have positive power) and statement 4 is false (a single diverging lens always gives a virtual image of a real object).

</details>
