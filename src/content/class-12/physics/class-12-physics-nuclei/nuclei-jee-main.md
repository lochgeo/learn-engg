## JEE Main sprint: nuclei — how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Use the constants $1\ \text{u}=931.5\ \text{MeV}$, $m_p=1.00728\ \text{u}$, $m_n=1.00867\ \text{u}$, $M(^1\mathrm H)=1.00783\ \text{u}$, $R_0=1.2\ \text{fm}$, $N_A=6.022\times10^{23}$, $1\ \text{MeV}=1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{J}$, $k=1.38\times10^{-23}\ \text{J/K}$, $\hbar c=197\ \text{MeV·fm}$. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C, and 5 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one calculation consume the paper.

The goal is not only an answer. Before writing an equation, name the system and choose the tool: radius and density, mass defect and binding energy, nuclear-force range, decay law, or reaction energy.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nuclear radius | $R=R_0 A^{1/3}$ | $R_0\approx1.2\ \text{fm}$; radius is not the mass. |
| Nuclear density | $\rho\approx2.3\times10^{17}\ \text{kg/m}^3$ | Independent of $A$ because of saturation. |
| Mass defect | $\Delta m=Z M(^1\mathrm H)+N m_n-M_{\text{atom}}$ | Use atomic masses so electrons cancel. |
| Binding energy | $B=\Delta m\,c^2$; $1\ \text{u}=931.5\ \text{MeV}$ | $\Delta m$ is a difference, not the whole mass. |
| Stability / energy release | $B/A$; peak near $A\approx56$ | Fusion for light, fission for heavy. |
| Nuclear-force range | $R\sim\hbar/(mc)$ | Pion mass gives $\sim1.4\ \text{fm}$. |
| Decay law | $N=N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$, $A=\lambda N$ | $\lambda$ is probability per unit time. |
| Half-life / mean life | $T_{1/2}=\ln2/\lambda$; $\tau=1/\lambda$ | Do not confuse the two. |
| Activity after time | $A=A_0\,2^{-t/T_{1/2}}$ | Base 2 uses half-life directly. |
| Alpha / beta decay | $\alpha$: $Z-2,A-4$; $\beta$: $Z\pm1,A$ | Gamma changes neither $Z$ nor $A$. |
| Fission energy | $\sim200\ \text{MeV}$ per event | Includes fragment kinetic energy, neutrons, etc. |
| Fusion condition | $\sim10^8\ \text{K}$, high density | Coulomb barrier; neutral neutrons not needed. |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If your answer gives a nuclear radius larger than the atom, a negative mass defect for a bound nucleus, or a half-life with the wrong unit, stop and revisit the setup. A number with the right unit can still come from the wrong model.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Radius scaling

If the mass number $A$ of a nucleus is increased by a factor of $8$, its radius:

- A. increases by a factor of $2$
- B. increases by a factor of $8$
- C. increases by a factor of $4$
- D. is unchanged

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** $R\propto A^{1/3}$, so $8^{1/3}=2$.
</details>

### 2. Nuclear density

Which statement about nuclear density is correct?

- A. It increases with $A$.
- B. It is approximately $2.3\times10^{17}\ \text{kg/m}^3$ for all nuclei.
- C. It equals the density of water times $10^3$.
- D. It is smaller for heavy nuclei.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Saturation makes the density universal.
</details>

### 3. Mass defect sign

For a stable bound nucleus, the mass defect $\Delta m$ is:

- A. zero
- B. negative
- C. positive
- D. equal to the atomic mass

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The bound nucleus is lighter than its free constituents, so $\Delta m>0$.
</details>

### 4. Binding energy per nucleon

The binding energy per nucleon is maximum near:

- A. hydrogen-1
- B. helium-4
- C. iron-56
- D. uranium-238

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The curve peaks near $A\approx56$, at iron-56, with $\sim8.8\ \text{MeV/nucleon}$.
</details>

### 5. Nuclear force range

The nuclear force becomes negligible beyond roughly:

- A. $10^{-10}\ \text{m}$
- B. $10^{-15}\ \text{m}$
- C. $10^{-12}\ \text{m}$
- D. $10^{-8}\ \text{m}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The range is a few femtometres, $\sim10^{-15}\ \text{m}$.
</details>

### 6. Half-life and mean life

For a given decay, the mean life $\tau$ and half-life $T_{1/2}$ are related by:

- A. $\tau = T_{1/2}/\ln2$
- B. $\tau = T_{1/2}\ln2$
- C. $\tau = T_{1/2}$
- D. $\tau = \ln2/T_{1/2}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** $\tau=1/\lambda=T_{1/2}/\ln2\approx1.443\,T_{1/2}$.
</details>

### 7. Activity after one half-life

If the initial activity is $A_0$, the activity after one half-life is:

- A. $A_0/2$
- B. $A_0/e$
- C. $2A_0$
- D. $A_0\ln2$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** One half-life halves the number of nuclei, hence the activity.
</details>

### 8. Alpha decay

In alpha decay, the changes in $(Z,A)$ are:

- A. $(+2,+4)$
- B. $(-2,-4)$
- C. $(+1,0)$
- D. $(0,-1)$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** An alpha particle is $^4_2\mathrm{He}$, so $Z\to Z-2$, $A\to A-4$.
</details>

### 9. Beta-minus decay

In $\beta^-$ decay:

- A. a proton becomes a neutron and $Z$ decreases by 1
- B. a neutron becomes a proton and $Z$ increases by 1
- C. the nucleus emits a photon
- D. $A$ decreases by 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $n\to p+e^-+\bar\nu_e$; $A$ is unchanged, $Z$ rises by 1.
</details>

### 10. Fusion temperature

Controlled fusion on Earth requires temperatures of order:

- A. $10^3\ \text{K}$
- B. $10^5\ \text{K}$
- C. $10^8\ \text{K}$
- D. $10^{12}\ \text{K}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\sim10^8\ \text{K}$ gives thermal energies comparable to the Coulomb barrier.
</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Nuclear radius

Find the radius (in fm) of a nucleus with $A=125$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$125^{1/3}=5$, so $R=1.2\times5=6.0\ \text{fm}$.
</details>

### 12. Deuteron binding energy

Find the binding energy (in MeV) of the deuteron, given $M_D=2.01410\ \text{u}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\Delta m = 1.00783+1.00867-2.01410 = 0.00240\ \text{u}$.

$B = 0.00240\times931.5 \approx 2.24\ \text{MeV}$.
</details>

### 13. Half-life

A nucleus has decay constant $\lambda=0.10\ \text{s}^{-1}$. Find its half-life in seconds.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$T_{1/2}=\ln2/\lambda = 0.693/0.10 = 6.93\ \text{s}$.
</details>

### 14. Activity

An initial activity of $640\ \text{Bq}$ falls to what value after $3$ half-lives?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$A = 640/2^3 = 640/8 = 80\ \text{Bq}$.
</details>

### 15. Fission rate

A reactor produces $32\ \text{MW}$ of thermal power. Find the number of fissions per second (use $200\ \text{MeV}$ per fission).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Energy per fission $=200\times1.6\times10^{-13}=3.2\times10^{-11}\ \text{J}$.

Rate $=32\times10^6/(3.2\times10^{-11}) = 1.0\times10^{18}$ fissions/s.
</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

### 16. Nuclear force charge independence

**Assertion (A):** The nuclear force between two protons is approximately the same as between a proton and a neutron (after removing Coulomb effects).

**Reason (R):** The nuclear force is charge-independent.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both are true and charge independence is exactly the reason.
</details>

### 17. Closest approach equals radius?

**Assertion (A):** The distance of closest approach of an alpha particle equals the nuclear radius.

**Reason (R):** At closest approach the alpha particle’s kinetic energy is fully converted to electrostatic potential energy.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** R is true (energy conversion), but A is false: $r_{\min}$ depends on projectile energy and is only an upper bound on nuclear size.
</details>

### 18. Fusion and binding energy

**Assertion (A):** Fusion of two light nuclei releases energy.

**Reason (R):** The product nucleus has a higher binding energy per nucleon than the reactants.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both are true and the rise in $B/A$ toward iron is why energy is released.
</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Match the decay

Match each decay to its change in $(Z,A)$.

| Decay | $\Delta Z$ | $\Delta A$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\alpha$ | ? | ? |
| $\beta^-$ | ? | ? |
| $\gamma$ | ? | ? |

Choose from $\Delta Z$: $-2,\ +1,\ 0$; $\Delta A$: $-4,\ 0$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- $\alpha$: $\Delta Z=-2$, $\Delta A=-4$
- $\beta^-$: $\Delta Z=+1$, $\Delta A=0$
- $\gamma$: $\Delta Z=0$, $\Delta A=0$

Gamma radiation changes only the internal energy state.
</details>

### 20. Multi-statement

Which of the following statements are correct?

1. Nuclear density is the same for all nuclei.
2. The nuclear force is a long-range inverse-square force.
3. Fusion of light nuclei releases energy.
4. $1\ \text{u}$ of mass corresponds to $931.5\ \text{MeV}$.

- A. 1 and 3 only
- B. 1, 3 and 4 only
- C. 2, 3 and 4 only
- D. 1, 2, 3 and 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Statements 1, 3 and 4 are correct. Statement 2 is false: the nuclear force is short-ranged and saturates.
</details>
