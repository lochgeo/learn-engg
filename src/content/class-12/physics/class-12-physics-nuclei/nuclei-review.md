## Mastery check

Use $1\ \text{u}=931.5\ \text{MeV}$, $m_p=1.00728\ \text{u}$, $m_n=1.00867\ \text{u}$, $M(^1\mathrm H)=1.00783\ \text{u}$, $R_0=1.2\ \text{fm}$, $N_A=6.022\times10^{23}$, and $1\ \text{MeV}=1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{J}$.

### 1. Nuclear radius

Find the radius of a $^{64}_{29}\mathrm{Cu}$ nucleus and compare it with the atomic radius ($\sim1.3\times10^{-10}\ \text{m}$).

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$64^{1/3}\approx4.0$, so $R\approx1.2\times4.0=4.8\ \text{fm}=4.8\times10^{-15}\ \text{m}$.

Ratio to atomic radius: $(4.8\times10^{-15})/(1.3\times10^{-10})\approx3.7\times10^{-5}$. The nucleus is about $10^5$ times smaller than the atom.
</details>

### 2. Density

Show that the density is independent of $A$ and compute its value.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$M\approx A u$ (in kg, $A\times1.66\times10^{-27}$), $V=\frac43\pi R_0^3 A$, so

$$
\rho=\frac{A u}{\frac43\pi R_0^3 A}=\frac{u}{\frac43\pi R_0^3}
\approx\frac{1.66\times10^{-27}}{\frac43\pi(1.2\times10^{-15})^3}
\approx2.3\times10^{17}\ \text{kg/m}^3.
$$

The $A$ cancels, giving universal nuclear density.
</details>

### 3. Mass defect of helium-4

Compute the binding energy of $^4_2\mathrm{He}$ from atomic masses ($M_{\mathrm{He}}=4.00260\ \text{u}$).

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\Delta m = 2(1.00783)+2(1.00867)-4.00260 = 0.03040\ \text{u}$.

$B = 0.03040\times931.5 \approx 28.3\ \text{MeV}$, so $B/A\approx7.08\ \text{MeV/nucleon}$.
</details>

### 4. Stability comparison

Which is more tightly bound per nucleon: $^{12}\mathrm C$ ($M=12.0000\ \text{u}$) or $^{56}\mathrm{Fe}$ ($M=55.9349\ \text{u}$)? Compute both.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Carbon: $\Delta m=6(1.00783)+6(1.00867)-12.0000=0.09894\ \text{u}$; $B=92.16\ \text{MeV}$; $B/A=7.68\ \text{MeV/nucleon}$.

Iron: $\Delta m=26(1.00783)+30(1.00867)-55.9349=0.5288\ \text{u}$; $B=492.6\ \text{MeV}$; $B/A=8.80\ \text{MeV/nucleon}$.

Iron is more tightly bound per nucleon, consistent with the peak near $A=56$.
</details>

### 5. Nuclear-force range

Estimate the range from a $770\ \text{MeV}/c^2$ meson and comment.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$R = 197/770 \approx 0.26\ \text{fm}$, much smaller than a nucleon spacing ($\sim2\ \text{fm}$). Such a heavy mediator cannot set the range of the whole-nucleus force; the pion ($\sim140\ \text{MeV}$) is the lightest relevant exchange particle and gives $\sim1.4\ \text{fm}$.
</details>

### 6. Decay-law calculation

A sample of activity $3.2\times10^5\ \text{Bq}$ has half-life $5.0\ \text{h}$. Find the activity after $15\ \text{h}$ and the number of nuclei initially present.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$15\ \text{h}=3\,T_{1/2}$, so $A=A_0/8=4.0\times10^4\ \text{Bq}$.

$\lambda=\ln2/(5.0\ \text{h})=0.1386\ \text{h}^{-1}=3.85\times10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1}$.

$N_0=A_0/\lambda=3.2\times10^5/(3.85\times10^{-5})\approx8.3\times10^9$ nuclei.
</details>

### 7. Identify the decay

A nucleus $^{238}_{92}\mathrm{U}$ decays to $^{234}_{90}\mathrm{Th}$. What radiation is emitted, and is $Q$ positive? (Masses: $M_U=238.0508\ \text{u}$, $M_{Th}=234.0436\ \text{u}$, $M_{He}=4.00260\ \text{u}$.)

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$Z$ fell by 2 and $A$ by 4: alpha decay. Mass of products $=234.0436+4.00260=238.0462\ \text{u}<238.0508\ \text{u}$, so $Q=(0.0046\ \text{u})c^2\approx4.3\ \text{MeV}>0$. The decay is allowed.
</details>

### 8. Beta decay

$^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C}$ decays to $^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}$. What is emitted, and why does $A$ stay the same?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

A neutron becomes a proton: $\beta^-$ decay, emitting an electron and an antineutrino. $A$ is unchanged because a nucleon is converted, not lost; $Z$ rises by 1.
</details>

### 9. Fission energy

How much energy is released if $1.0\ \text{g}$ of U-235 fissions completely? (Use $200\ \text{MeV}$ per fission.)

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Atoms in $1.0\ \text{g}$: $N_A/235\approx2.56\times10^{21}$.

Energy $=2.56\times10^{21}\times200\ \text{MeV}\times1.6\times10^{-13}\ \text{J/MeV}
\approx8.2\times10^{10}\ \text{J}$, about $82\ \text{GJ}$ (roughly $20$ tonnes of TNT).
</details>

### 10. Fusion condition

Why must a fusion reactor reach $\sim10^8\ \text{K}$, and why is this harder than fission?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The Coulomb barrier between two nuclei requires high thermal speeds to bring them within $\sim1$–$2\ \text{fm}$ where the strong force acts. $10^8\ \text{K}$ gives $kT\sim10\ \text{keV}$, comparable to the barrier. Fission, by contrast, is triggered by neutral neutrons that feel no Coulomb barrier, at room temperature.
</details>
