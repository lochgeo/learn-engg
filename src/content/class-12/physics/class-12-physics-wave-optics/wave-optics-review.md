## First choose the tool

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Wavefront meets a boundary; angles from the normal | Reflection: $\angle i=\angle r$; refraction: $n_1\sin i=n_2\sin r$ |
| “Critical angle” or “total internal reflection” | $\sin C=n_2/n_1$ with $n_1>n_2$ |
| Two sources, path difference given | Interference: constructive at $\Delta=m\lambda$, destructive at $(m+\tfrac12)\lambda$ |
| Fringe spacing on a screen | Young’s: $\beta=\lambda D/d$ |
| Position of $m$-th bright/dark fringe | $y_m=m\lambda D/d$ or $(m+\tfrac12)\lambda D/d$ |
| Single slit, width given | Minima: $a\sin\theta=m\lambda$; central width $2\lambda D/a$ |
| Intensity pattern of a single slit | $I=I_0(\sin\beta/\beta)^2$ |
| Light through polarisers | Malus: $I=I_0\cos^2\theta$ (already polarised); halve for unpolarised first |
| Brewster angle | $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1$ |
| Resolving two close sources | Rayleigh: $\theta_{\min}=1.22\lambda/D$ |

## Mastery check

Try each without looking at the answer first. Use $c=3.0\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$ unless stated otherwise.

### 1. Refraction at a surface

Light in air strikes glass ($n=1.50$) at $40^\circ$. Find the angle of refraction.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\sin r=\sin40^\circ/1.50=0.643/1.50=0.429$, so $r\approx25.4^\circ$. The ray bends toward the normal.
</details>

### 2. Critical angle

Find the critical angle for a glass–air boundary ($n_{\text{glass}}=1.50$).

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\sin C=1/1.50=0.667$, so $C\approx41.8^\circ$.
</details>

### 3. Interference condition

Two coherent sources emit $\lambda=5.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}$. At a point the path difference is $7.5\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}$. Is the point bright or dark?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\Delta/\lambda=1.5$, an odd multiple of $1/2$, so the point is dark (destructive).
</details>

### 4. Fringe width

In Young’s experiment, $\lambda=6.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}$, $D=1.5\ \text{m}$, $d=0.30\ \text{mm}$. Find the fringe width.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\beta=\lambda D/d=(6.0\times10^{-7}\times1.5)/(3.0\times10^{-4})=3.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}=3.0\ \text{mm}$.
</details>

### 5. Fringe position

Using the values in question 4, find the distance of the fourth bright fringe from the centre.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$y_4=4\beta=12\ \text{mm}$.
</details>

### 6. Single-slit minimum

A slit of width $0.10\ \text{mm}$ is illuminated by $\lambda=5.0\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}$. Find the angle of the first minimum.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\sin\theta_1=\lambda/a=5.0\times10^{-7}/1.0\times10^{-4}=5.0\times10^{-3}$, so $\theta_1\approx0.29^\circ$.
</details>

### 7. Single-slit central width

Using question 6 with a screen at $2.0\ \text{m}$, find the width of the central maximum.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$y_1=2.0\times5.0\times10^{-3}=0.010\ \text{m}=10\ \text{mm}$; central width $=2y_1=20\ \text{mm}$.
</details>

### 8. Malus’s law

Unpolarised light of intensity $200\ \text{W/m}^2$ passes through a polariser and then an analyser at $45^\circ$. Find the final intensity.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

After polariser: $100\ \text{W/m}^2$. After analyser: $100\cos^2 45^\circ=50\ \text{W/m}^2$.
</details>

### 9. Brewster angle

Find Brewster’s angle for light going from air to glass ($n=1.50$).

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\tan\theta_B=1.50$, so $\theta_B\approx56.3^\circ$.
</details>

### 10. Explain, don’t just calculate

Why does the central maximum of a single-slit pattern have twice the width of a side lobe?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Minima occur at $a\sin\theta=m\lambda$ for $m=\pm1,\pm2,\dots$. The central maximum lies between $m=-1$ and $m=+1$, spanning $2\lambda/a$ in $\sin\theta$, whereas each side lobe lies between consecutive integers, spanning only $\lambda/a$. Hence the central peak is twice as wide.
</details>
