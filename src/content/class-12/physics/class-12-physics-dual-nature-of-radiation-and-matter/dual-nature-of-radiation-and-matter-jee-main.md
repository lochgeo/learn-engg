## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Use $g=10\ \text{m/s}^2$ where needed and take $h=6.63\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$, $c=3.00\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$, $e=1.60\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}$, $m_e=9.11\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}$. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C, and 5 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one calculation consume the paper.

Before writing an equation, name the system: photon energy, photoelectric equation, de Broglie wavelength, or Bragg diffraction.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Threshold of a metal | $\phi=h\nu_0=hc/\lambda_0$ | Convert eV to joules if mixing with SI. |
| Photon energy | $E=h\nu=hc/\lambda$ | Use consistent units (eV·nm shortcut $1240/\lambda_{\text{nm}}$). |
| Photoelectric equation | $K_{\max}=h\nu-\phi$ | $K_{\max}$ is the maximum, for surface electrons. |
| Stopping potential | $eV_0=K_{\max}$ | $V_0$ in volts equals $K_{\max}$ in eV. |
| Intensity change | Photocurrent $\propto I$ | $K_{\max}$ and $V_0$ do not change. |
| de Broglie wavelength | $\lambda=h/(mv)$ | Use momentum, not kinetic energy, unless you convert. |
| Electron through $V$ volts | $\lambda=1.227/\sqrt{V}\ \text{nm}$ | $V$ in volts; result in nm. |
| Crystal diffraction | $n\lambda=2d\sin\theta$ | $\theta$ is the Bragg angle, not necessarily the detector angle. |
| Bohr quantisation | $2\pi r=n\lambda_{\text{dB}}$ | Gives $mvr=nh/2\pi$. |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Fastest sanity checks">

If your answer gives a threshold wavelength shorter than the incident wavelength yet claims no emission, or a Bragg angle with $\sin\theta>1$, stop and revisit the setup. A numerical answer with the right unit can still come from the wrong relation.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Threshold wavelength

A metal has work function $2.0\ \text{eV}$. Its threshold wavelength is closest to:

- A. $310\ \text{nm}$
- B. $414\ \text{nm}$
- C. $620\ \text{nm}$
- D. $720\ \text{nm}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\lambda_0=1240/2.0=620\ \text{nm}$.

</details>

### 2. Maximum kinetic energy

Light of wavelength $300\ \text{nm}$ falls on sodium (work function $2.3\ \text{eV}$). The maximum kinetic energy of a photoelectron is:

- A. $1.8\ \text{eV}$
- B. $2.3\ \text{eV}$
- C. $4.1\ \text{eV}$
- D. $6.4\ \text{eV}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Photon energy $=1240/300=4.13\ \text{eV}$; $K_{\max}=4.13-2.3=1.83\ \text{eV}$.

</details>

### 3. Intensity

In a photoelectric experiment the intensity of above-threshold light is doubled. Which quantity changes?

- A. Maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons
- B. Stopping potential
- C. Threshold frequency
- D. Photocurrent

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Intensity changes the number of photons and hence the number of emitted electrons. $K_{\max}$, $V_0$ and $\nu_0$ depend on frequency and the metal, not on intensity.

</details>

### 4. de Broglie wavelength

An electron is accelerated through $100\ \text{V}$. Its de Broglie wavelength is closest to:

- A. $0.012\ \text{nm}$
- B. $0.123\ \text{nm}$
- C. $1.23\ \text{nm}$
- D. $12.3\ \text{nm}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $\lambda=1.227/\sqrt{100}=0.1227\ \text{nm}$.

</details>

### 5. Same speed comparison

An electron and a proton move with the same speed. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength?

- A. The proton, because it is heavier
- B. The electron, because it is lighter
- C. Both have the same wavelength
- D. Neither has a wavelength

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $\lambda=h/(mv)$; at the same speed the lighter particle has the larger wavelength. The electron is about $1836$ times lighter.

</details>

### 6. Bragg angle

Electrons of wavelength $0.167\ \text{nm}$ are diffracted from crystal planes spaced $0.091\ \text{nm}$ apart (first order). The Bragg angle is closest to:

- A. $25^{\circ}$
- B. $42^{\circ}$
- C. $65^{\circ}$
- D. $80^{\circ}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\sin\theta=\lambda/(2d)=0.167/0.182=0.918$, so $\theta\approx66.7^{\circ}\approx65^{\circ}$.

</details>

### 7. Photon momentum

A photon has wavelength $500\ \text{nm}$. Its momentum is:

- A. $1.33\times10^{-27}\ \text{kg m/s}$
- B. $1.33\times10^{-30}\ \text{kg m/s}$
- C. $1.33\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg m/s}$
- D. $4.0\times10^{-19}\ \text{kg m/s}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** $p=h/\lambda=6.63\times10^{-34}/(500\times10^{-9})=1.33\times10^{-27}\ \text{kg m/s}$.

</details>

### 8. Bohr standing wave

The Bohr quantisation condition $mvr=nh/2\pi$ is equivalent to:

- A. $r=n\lambda/(2\pi)$
- B. $2\pi r=n\lambda$
- C. $\lambda=2\pi r/n^2$
- D. $r=n^2\lambda/(2\pi)$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $mvr=nh/2\pi \Rightarrow mv(2\pi r/n)=h \Rightarrow 2\pi r=n(h/mv)=n\lambda$.

</details>

### 9. Work function from stopping potential

For a metal, stopping potential is $2.0\ \text{V}$ when light of wavelength $300\ \text{nm}$ is used. The work function is:

- A. $1.1\ \text{eV}$
- B. $2.0\ \text{eV}$
- C. $2.1\ \text{eV}$
- D. $4.1\ \text{eV}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Photon energy $=1240/300=4.13\ \text{eV}$; $\phi=4.13-2.0=2.13\ \text{eV}\approx2.1\ \text{eV}$.

</details>

### 10. Threshold frequency

A metal has threshold frequency $5.0\times10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$. Light of frequency $7.0\times10^{14}\ \text{Hz}$ is incident. The maximum kinetic energy is:

- A. $0.41\ \text{eV}$
- B. $0.83\ \text{eV}$
- C. $1.24\ \text{eV}$
- D. $2.89\ \text{eV}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $h\nu=(4.14\times10^{-15})(7.0\times10^{14})=2.90\ \text{eV}$; $h\nu_0=(4.14\times10^{-15})(5.0\times10^{14})=2.07\ \text{eV}$; $K_{\max}=0.83\ \text{eV}$.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Stopping potential

Light of wavelength $250\ \text{nm}$ falls on a metal with work function $3.4\ \text{eV}$. Find the stopping potential.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Photon energy $=1240/250=4.96\ \text{eV}$. $K_{\max}=4.96-3.4=1.56\ \text{eV}$, so $V_0=1.56\ \text{V}$.

</details>

### 12. de Broglie wavelength

Find the de Broglie wavelength (in nm) of an electron accelerated through $225\ \text{V}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\lambda=1.227/\sqrt{225}=1.227/15=0.0818\ \text{nm}$.

</details>

### 13. Bragg angle

For first-order diffraction, electrons of wavelength $0.123\ \text{nm}$ strike crystal planes spaced $0.150\ \text{nm}$ apart. Find the Bragg angle in degrees.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\sin\theta=0.123/(2\times0.150)=0.410$, so $\theta=\sin^{-1}(0.410)\approx24.2^{\circ}$.

</details>

### 14. Radiation force

A $20\ \text{mW}$ beam of light of wavelength $400\ \text{nm}$ is fully reflected from a surface. Find the radiation force on the surface.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

For full reflection, force $=2P/c=2(0.020)/(3.00\times10^8)=1.33\times10^{-10}\ \text{N}$.

</details>

### 15. Orbit speed from standing wave

An electron in a circular orbit of radius $0.05\ \text{nm}$ is a standing wave with $n=1$. Find its speed.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$2\pi r=\lambda=h/(m v)$, so $v=h/(2\pi r m_e)$.

$v=6.63\times10^{-34}/(2\pi\times5.0\times10^{-11}\times9.11\times10^{-31})=6.63\times10^{-34}/(2.86\times10^{-40})=2.32\times10^6\ \text{m/s}$.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

### 16. Assertion–reason

**Assertion (A):** Increasing the intensity of incident light increases the photocurrent but not the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons.

**Reason (R):** Each photon interacts with one electron, and the photon energy $h\nu$ is fixed by the frequency.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true, and the one-photon–one-electron interaction explains why intensity changes the number of electrons but not the energy per electron.

</details>

### 17. Assertion–reason

**Assertion (A):** A $100\ \text{kg}$ object has a de Broglie wavelength.

**Reason (R):** The de Broglie relation applies only to charged particles.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** A is true (every particle has $\lambda=h/p$, however small). R is false because the relation applies to all matter, charged or uncharged.

</details>

### 18. Assertion–reason

**Assertion (A):** The Davisson–Germer experiment confirms the wave nature of electrons.

**Reason (R):** The observed diffraction peak occurs at an angle predicted by Bragg’s law using the de Broglie wavelength.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both are true and the Bragg-angle agreement is exactly what establishes the wave nature.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Matching

Match each quantity in List I with its correct expression in List II.

List I:
1. Photon energy
2. de Broglie wavelength (non-relativistic)
3. Bragg condition (first order)
4. Maximum photoelectron kinetic energy

List II:
P. $h/(mv)$
Q. $h\nu-\phi$
R. $h\nu$
S. $2d\sin\theta$

- A. 1-R, 2-P, 3-S, 4-Q
- B. 1-Q, 2-P, 3-S, 4-R
- C. 1-R, 2-S, 3-P, 4-Q
- D. 1-P, 2-R, 3-S, 4-Q

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Photon energy $=h\nu$ (R); de Broglie wavelength $=h/(mv)$ (P); Bragg condition $\lambda=2d\sin\theta$ (S); $K_{\max}=h\nu-\phi$ (Q).

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement

Which of the following statements is/are correct?

1. Photoelectric emission is instantaneous.
2. The stopping potential depends on the intensity of incident light.
3. de Broglie wavelength decreases as the speed of the particle increases.
4. In the Davisson–Germer experiment, the diffraction peak shifts when the accelerating voltage changes.

- A. 1 and 3 only
- B. 1, 3 and 4 only
- C. 2 and 4 only
- D. All four

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Statements 1, 3 and 4 are correct. Statement 2 is false: stopping potential depends on frequency, not intensity.

</details>
