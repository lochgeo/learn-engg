## First choose the tool

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Work function or threshold given | $\phi=h\nu_0=hc/\lambda_0$ |
| Photon energy from wavelength/frequency | $E=h\nu=hc/\lambda$ |
| Maximum kinetic energy of photoelectron | $K_{\max}=h\nu-\phi=hc/\lambda-\phi$ |
| Stopping potential | $eV_0=K_{\max}$ |
| Effect of intensity | Changes photocurrent (number of electrons), not $K_{\max}$ |
| de Broglie wavelength of a particle | $\lambda=h/p=h/(mv)$ |
| Electron accelerated through $V$ volts | $\lambda=1.227/\sqrt{V}\ \text{nm}$ |
| Diffraction from crystal planes | Bragg: $n\lambda=2d\sin\theta$ |
| Bohr orbit quantisation | $2\pi r=n\lambda_{\text{dB}}$ gives $mvr=nh/2\pi$ |
| Photon momentum / radiation pressure | $p=h/\lambda=E/c$; pressure $I/c$ or $2I/c$ |

## Mastery check

Try each without looking at the answer first. Use $h=6.63\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$, $c=3.00\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$, $e=1.60\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}$, $m_e=9.11\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}$.

### 1. Threshold wavelength

A metal has work function $3.0\ \text{eV}$. Find its threshold wavelength.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\lambda_0=hc/\phi=1240/3.0=413\ \text{nm}$. Light must be at most $413\ \text{nm}$ (violet or ultraviolet) to eject electrons.

</details>

### 2. Stopping potential

Light of wavelength $200\ \text{nm}$ falls on the metal in question 1. Find the stopping potential.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Photon energy $=1240/200=6.20\ \text{eV}$. $K_{\max}=6.20-3.0=3.20\ \text{eV}$, so $V_0=3.20\ \text{V}$.

</details>

### 3. Intensity and energy

If the intensity of the $200\ \text{nm}$ light is doubled, what happens to the photocurrent and to $V_0$?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The photocurrent doubles (more photons $\rightarrow$ more photoelectrons). $V_0$ stays $3.20\ \text{V}$, because each photon still carries $6.20\ \text{eV}$ and the work function is unchanged.

</details>

### 4. de Broglie wavelength

Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through $400\ \text{V}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\lambda=1.227/\sqrt{400}=1.227/20=0.0614\ \text{nm}=6.14\times10^{-11}\ \text{m}$.

</details>

### 5. Particle comparison

A proton and an electron have the same speed. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength, and by what factor?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\lambda=h/(mv)$, so at the same speed the lighter particle has the longer wavelength. The electron is about $1836$ times lighter, so its wavelength is $1836$ times longer than the proton’s.

</details>

### 6. Bragg verification

Electrons of wavelength $0.167\ \text{nm}$ are reflected from crystal planes spaced $0.091\ \text{nm}$ apart. Find the Bragg angle for first-order diffraction.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\sin\theta=\lambda/(2d)=0.167/(0.182)=0.918$, so $\theta\approx66.7^{\circ}$, close to the observed $65^{\circ}$.

</details>

### 7. Standing wave on an orbit

Use the standing-wave condition to derive Bohr’s quantisation rule $mvr=nh/2\pi$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Fit $n$ wavelengths around the circumference: $2\pi r=n\lambda=n h/(mv)$. Rearranging gives $mvr=nh/(2\pi)$.

</details>

### 8. Photon momentum

A $10\ \text{mW}$ laser beam of wavelength $500\ \text{nm}$ is fully absorbed by a surface. Find the radiation force on the surface.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Power $P=10\times10^{-3}\ \text{W}$. Momentum per second $=P/c$ (absorption). Force $=P/c=0.010/(3.00\times10^8)=3.33\times10^{-11}\ \text{N}$.

</details>

### 9. Conceptual

Why does the photoelectric effect support the particle nature of light while diffraction supports its wave nature?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The photoelectric effect shows energy delivered in discrete quanta $h\nu$ with a threshold frequency, which a continuous wave cannot explain. Diffraction and interference show spatial spreading and superposition, which particles alone cannot explain. Light (and matter) exhibits both aspects; the experiment selects which is observed.

</details>

### 10. Combined problem

An electron is accelerated through $100\ \text{V}$ and then strikes a crystal with plane spacing $0.20\ \text{nm}$. Find (i) its de Broglie wavelength and (ii) the first-order Bragg angle, if diffraction occurs.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

(i) $\lambda=1.227/\sqrt{100}=0.1227\ \text{nm}$.

(ii) $\sin\theta=\lambda/(2d)=0.1227/(0.40)=0.307$, so $\theta\approx17.9^{\circ}$. Since $\sin\theta<1$, first-order diffraction is possible.

</details>
