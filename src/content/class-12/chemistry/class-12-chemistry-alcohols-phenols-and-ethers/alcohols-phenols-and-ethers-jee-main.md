## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice paper for after you have completed the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 minutes on Section B, 10 minutes on Section C and 5 minutes on Section D. If a question takes longer than 90 seconds, mark it and move on; the paper rewards method selection as much as calculation.

Use the molar volume $22.4\ \text{L mol}^{-1}$ at STP where a gas volume is asked for. All concentrations are aqueous unless stated otherwise. No gravitational data are needed in this paper.

The goal is not only the answer. Before writing an equation, name the functional group and the reaction type: acid–base, substitution, elimination, oxidation, electrophilic aromatic substitution or ether cleavage.

## Conditions and formula sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naming an alcohol | Longest chain containing –OH; number to give –OH the lowest locant | The –OH carbon, not the nearest substituent, controls the numbering |
| Naming an ether | Larger group is the parent; smaller group is an alkoxy prefix | In aryl ethers the ring is the parent |
| Boiling-point comparison | Hydrogen bonding, then molar mass, then branching | An ether can accept but not donate an H-bond |
| Acidity comparison | $\mathrm{p}K_a$; resonance stabilisation; substituent effects | Phenol is between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid |
| Alcohol + HX | $\mathrm{ROH + HX \rightarrow RX + H_2O}$ | Reactivity $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$; HI > HBr > HCl |
| Alcohol oxidation | PCC for aldehyde; $\mathrm{KMnO_4/H^+}$ or $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7/H^+}$ for acid | $2^\circ$ gives a ketone; $3^\circ$ resists mild oxidation |
| Phenol test | $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ violet; bromine water white precipitate | Phenol does **not** give $\mathrm{CO_2}$ with $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$ |
| Ether synthesis | Williamson: alkoxide + methyl/primary halide | Tertiary halide eliminates instead |
| Ether cleavage | $\mathrm{R{-}O{-}R' + HX \rightarrow ROH + R'X}$ | Aryl alkyl ethers break at the alkyl–O bond |
| Percentage yield | Moles of limiting reagent, then theoretical mass | Actual mass = theoretical × yield fraction |
| Gas volume at STP | $V=n\times22.4\ \text{L}$ | The gas must be measured at STP |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If a proposed product has a $3^\circ$ alcohol oxidised to a ketone, an aryl ether cleaved to benzene and methanol, or phenol evolving $\mathrm{CO_2}$ from $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$, stop: one of those three is chemically impossible. A correct number with the wrong functional group still loses the mark.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Tertiary alcohol

Which of the following is a tertiary alcohol?

- A. butan-1-ol
- B. butan-2-ol
- C. 2-methylpropan-2-ol
- D. 2-methylpropan-1-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** In 2-methylpropan-2-ol, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3COH}$, the carbinol carbon is attached to three other carbons. Butan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-1-ol are primary; butan-2-ol is secondary.
</details>

### 2. IUPAC name of an ether

The IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_3OCH(CH_3)_2}$ is:

- A. 2-methoxypropane
- B. 1-methoxypropane
- C. ethoxyethane
- D. 2-methylpropan-2-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The parent chain is propane, with a methoxy group on C2. The common name is methyl isopropyl ether.
</details>

### 3. Highest boiling point

Which compound has the highest boiling point?

- A. propane
- B. dimethyl ether
- C. ethanol
- D. butane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Ethanol can both donate and accept hydrogen bonds, so it boils at $78^\circ\text{C}$. Butane boils near $0^\circ\text{C}$, dimethyl ether at $-24^\circ\text{C}$ and propane at $-42^\circ\text{C}$; those three have only dispersion and dipole–dipole forces.
</details>

### 4. Lucas test

Which alcohol gives immediate turbidity with Lucas reagent?

- A. ethanol
- B. propan-1-ol
- C. propan-2-ol
- D. 2-methylpropan-2-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The tertiary alcohol ionises fastest to a stable tertiary carbocation, so the insoluble alkyl chloride separates at once. Propan-2-ol is secondary and reacts within minutes; the primary alcohols react only slowly or on heating.
</details>

### 5. PCC oxidation

Propan-1-ol treated with PCC in dichloromethane gives:

- A. propanal
- B. propan-2-ol
- C. propanoic acid
- D. propanone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** PCC is a mild oxidant that stops the primary alcohol at the aldehyde. Strong acidic permanganate or dichromate would carry the oxidation on to propanoic acid.
</details>

### 6. Most acidic compound

Which compound is the most acidic?

- A. phenol
- B. $p$-cresol
- C. $p$-nitrophenol
- D. ethanol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The nitro group withdraws electron density and stabilises the phenoxide ion, so $p$-nitrophenol ($\mathrm{p}K_a\approx7.15$) is more acidic than phenol ($\mathrm{p}K_a\approx9.95$). $p$-Cresol is less acidic than phenol because the methyl group donates electron density; ethanol is the weakest of the four.
</details>

### 7. Phenol and bicarbonate

Phenol does **not** react appreciably with:

- A. NaOH
- B. $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$
- C. $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$
- D. bromine water

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Phenol is not acidic enough to protonate $\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$ to carbonic acid, so no $\mathrm{CO_2}$ is evolved. It does dissolve in NaOH, and it reacts slowly with $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}$ without effervescence. Bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
</details>

### 8. Cleavage of anisole

Anisole heated with concentrated HI gives:

- A. benzene and methanol
- B. phenol and iodomethane
- C. iodobenzene and methanol
- D. phenol and methane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The methyl–oxygen bond breaks. Iodide attacks the methyl carbon, and the aryl–oxygen bond stays intact because phenoxide is a poor leaving group and the aryl C–O bond has partial double-bond character.
</details>

### 9. Grignard addition

Methylmagnesium bromide reacts with propanone, followed by acidic hydrolysis. The product is:

- A. propan-2-ol
- B. 2-methylpropan-2-ol
- C. butan-2-ol
- D. butan-1-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Propanone is a ketone, so the Grignard adds a third carbon group to the carbonyl carbon:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3MgBr + (CH_3)_2C{=}O \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} (CH_3)_3COH}.
$$

The product is a tertiary alcohol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
</details>

### 10. Cumene process

The cumene process produces phenol together with:

- A. acetone
- B. ethanol
- C. acetic acid
- D. formaldehyde

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Acid cleavage of cumene hydroperoxide gives phenol and propanone (acetone) in equimolar amounts:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5C(CH_3)_2OOH \xrightarrow{H^+} C_6H_5OH + (CH_3)_2C{=}O}.
$$
</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Phenol from benzene

Benzene is converted to phenol by the cumene process. What mass of phenol is obtained from $39\ \text{g}$ of benzene at $80\%$ yield? Give your answer in grams.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The molar mass of benzene is $78\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$:

$$
n(\mathrm{C_6H_6})=\frac{39}{78}=0.50\ \text{mol}.
$$

One mole of benzene gives one mole of phenol, whose molar mass is $94\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. The theoretical mass is

$$
m=0.50\times94=47\ \text{g}.
$$

At $80\%$ yield,

$$
m_{\text{actual}}=47\times0.80=37.6\ \text{g}.
$$

**Answer: $37.6\ \text{g}$** (about $38\ \text{g}$ to two significant figures).
</details>

### 12. Hydrogen from ethanol

What volume of hydrogen, measured at STP, is produced when $9.2\ \text{g}$ of ethanol reacts completely with excess sodium?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
n(\mathrm{C_2H_5OH})=\frac{9.2}{46.0}=0.20\ \text{mol}.
$$

The equation is

$$
\mathrm{2C_2H_5OH + 2Na \rightarrow 2C_2H_5ONa + H_2},
$$

so two moles of ethanol give one mole of hydrogen:

$$
n(\mathrm{H_2})=\frac{0.20}{2}=0.10\ \text{mol}.
$$

At STP,

$$
V=0.10\times22.4=2.24\ \text{L}.
$$

**Answer: $2.24\ \text{L}$**.
</details>

### 13. pH of a phenol solution

Phenol has $K_a=1.0\times10^{-10}$. Find the pH of a $0.010\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$ solution.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let $x=[\mathrm{C_6H_5O^-}]=[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]$. The equilibrium expression is

$$
K_a=\frac{x^2}{0.010-x}\approx\frac{x^2}{0.010}.
$$

Thus

$$
x^2=(1.0\times10^{-10})(0.010)=1.0\times10^{-12},
$$

$$
x=1.0\times10^{-6}\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}.
$$

The approximation is valid because $x/0.010=1.0\times10^{-4}\ll1$. Therefore

$$
\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}(1.0\times10^{-6})=6.00.
$$

**Answer: $\mathrm{pH}=6.00$**.
</details>

### 14. Williamson yield

Sodium ethoxide ($0.20\ \text{mol}$) reacts with bromoethane ($0.20\ \text{mol}$) to give diethyl ether. What mass of ether is obtained at $70\%$ yield?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The reactants are in a $1:1$ ratio, matching the equation, so the theoretical amount of ether is $0.20\ \text{mol}$. The molar mass of diethyl ether is $74.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$:

$$
m_{\text{theoretical}}=0.20\times74.0=14.8\ \text{g}.
$$

At $70\%$ yield,

$$
m_{\text{actual}}=14.8\times0.70=10.36\ \text{g}.
$$

**Answer: $10.4\ \text{g}$** (to three significant figures).
</details>

### 15. Tribromophenol yield

Bromine water is added to $4.70\ \text{g}$ of phenol. What mass of 2,4,6-tribromophenol is obtained at $90\%$ yield?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
n(\mathrm{C_6H_5OH})=\frac{4.70}{94.1}=0.0500\ \text{mol}.
$$

One mole of phenol gives one mole of 2,4,6-tribromophenol, $\mathrm{C_6H_2Br_3OH}$. Its molar mass is

$$
6(12.0)+3(1.0)+3(79.9)+16.0=330.7\ \text{g mol}^{-1}.
$$

The theoretical mass is

$$
m=0.0500\times330.7=16.5\ \text{g}.
$$

At $90\%$ yield,

$$
m_{\text{actual}}=16.5\times0.90=14.9\ \text{g}.
$$

**Answer: $14.9\ \text{g}$**.
</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

For each question, choose:

- **A.** Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- **B.** Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- **C.** A is true but R is false.
- **D.** A is false but R is true.

### 16. Phenol acidity

**Assertion (A):** Phenol is more acidic than ethanol.

**Reason (R):** The phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance with the aromatic ring.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true, and R explains A. Phenol has $\mathrm{p}K_a\approx9.95$ and ethanol about $15.9$. The negative charge in phenoxide is delocalised onto the *ortho* and *para* ring carbons, lowering the energy of the ion. The ethoxide ion has no such delocalisation.
</details>

### 17. Ether as a Grignard solvent

**Assertion (A):** Diethyl ether is a better solvent than ethanol for preparing and using a Grignard reagent.

**Reason (R):** Diethyl ether is aprotic and does not donate a proton to the Grignard reagent.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true, and R explains A. A Grignard reagent, $\mathrm{RMgX}$, is destroyed by any acidic proton:

$$
\mathrm{RMgX + H_2O \rightarrow RH + Mg(OH)X}.
$$

Ethanol has an –OH hydrogen and would protonate the reagent. Diethyl ether has no –OH, so it is aprotic and keeps the reagent intact; its oxygen lone pairs also solvate the magnesium.
</details>

### 18. Tertiary alcohol oxidation

**Assertion (A):** Tertiary alcohols are easily oxidised to ketones.

**Reason (R):** The carbon bearing the –OH group in a tertiary alcohol has no hydrogen atom.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** A is false, R is true. Tertiary alcohols resist mild oxidation precisely because the carbinol carbon has no hydrogen to remove; a ketone cannot form without breaking a C–C bond. Strong hot acidic permanganate can cleave the carbon skeleton, but that is not a clean oxidation to a ketone.
</details>

## Section D — 2 matching and multi-statement questions

### 19. Matching tests

Match the reaction in Column I with its observation in Column II.

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| (a) Phenol + $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$ | (i) Violet colour |
| (b) Phenol + bromine water | (ii) White precipitate |
| (c) Ethanol + $\mathrm{I_2/NaOH}$ | (iii) Yellow precipitate |
| (d) 2-Methylpropan-2-ol + Lucas reagent | (iv) Immediate turbidity |

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a–i, b–ii, c–iii, d–iv.**

Phenol gives a violet complex with iron(III) chloride. Bromine water produces insoluble 2,4,6-tribromophenol as a white precipitate. Ethanol gives a yellow precipitate of iodoform with iodine and alkali. The tertiary alcohol gives immediate turbidity with Lucas reagent because it forms a stable carbocation and the insoluble alkyl chloride separates quickly.
</details>

### 20. Multi-statement: Williamson synthesis

Consider the following statements about the Williamson synthesis:

(i) It is an SN2 reaction.

(ii) A tertiary alkyl halide gives mainly an alkene.

(iii) Aryl ethers can be made from sodium phenoxide and a primary alkyl halide.

(iv) The reaction works best with a secondary alkyl halide.

Which statements are correct?

- A. (i), (ii) and (iii)
- B. (i), (iii) and (iv)
- C. (ii), (iii) and (iv)
- D. all four

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** (i) is correct: the alkoxide attacks the carbon bearing the halide from the back in a single SN2 step. (ii) is correct: a tertiary halide is too crowded for backside attack, and the alkoxide acts as a base, giving elimination. (iii) is correct: sodium phenoxide is a good nucleophile and a primary alkyl halide is a good substrate. (iv) is false: secondary halides give a mixture of ether and alkene, so methyl and primary halides are preferred.
</details>
