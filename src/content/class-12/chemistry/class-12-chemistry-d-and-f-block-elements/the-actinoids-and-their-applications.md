## The actinoids: filling the 5f subshell

The actinoids are the fourteen elements thorium (Th) through lawrencium (Lr) that follow actinium. Like the lanthanoids they are f-block metals, but their $5f$ electrons are less buried than the $4f$ electrons of the lanthanoids, so they participate more in bonding. The result is a block that is more chemically varied, more radioactive, and far more technologically consequential than the lanthanoids.

## 1. Position and electronic configuration

The actinoids occupy the second row of the f-block. Their general configuration is

$$
[\mathrm{Rn}]\ 5f^{1-14}\ 6d^{0-1}\ 7s^2.
$$

Actinium is $[\mathrm{Rn}]\,6d^1\,7s^2$ and lawrencium $[\mathrm{Rn}]\,5f^{14}\,6d^1\,7s^2$. The fourteen actinoids proper run Th ($5f^0$) to Lr ($5f^{14}$). Because the $5f$, $6d$ and $7s$ levels are close in energy, several configurations are possible for the early members, and the assignment of the first $5f$ electron is still debated for some elements.

## 2. Comparison with the lanthanoids

| Feature | Lanthanoids | Actinoids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subshell filled | $4f$ | $5f$ |
| Common oxidation state | +3 | +3, but more variable |
| Range of oxidation states | +2 to +4 (rarely higher) | +2 to +7 |
| Radioactivity | Only promethium is radioactive | All are radioactive |
| Magnetic behaviour | Well-shielded $4f$ moments | Less shielded, more complex |
| Complex formation | Moderate | Stronger |

The key difference is that the $5f$ orbitals extend further from the nucleus than the $4f$ orbitals, so they overlap with ligand orbitals and participate in bonding. This makes the early actinoids (Th, Pa, U, Np, Pu) resemble transition metals more than lanthanoids.

## 3. Oxidation states

The actinoids show a wider spread of oxidation states than the lanthanoids, and the highest states occur for the early members:

- **Thorium**: +4 (dominant).
- **Protactinium**: +5.
- **Uranium**: +3, +4, +5, +6 (the +6 state, as $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$, is very stable).
- **Neptunium**: +3 to +7.
- **Plutonium**: +3 to +7 (all common in solution).
- **Americium**: +3 (dominant), +4, +5, +6.
- **Later actinoids (Cm–Lr)**: mainly +3, like the lanthanoids.

The +3 state becomes dominant again after americium because the $5f$ electrons become more core-like.

## 4. The actinoid contraction

Just as in the lanthanoids, the poor shielding by $f$ electrons makes the radius shrink across the series. The actinoid contraction is slightly larger than the lanthanoid contraction because the $5f$ electrons shield even less effectively than the $4f$ electrons. The consequence is the same: the 6d elements that follow (Hf, Ta, W, …) have radii almost identical to their 5d predecessors.

## 5. Radioactivity and handling

Every actinoid is radioactive. The naturally occurring ones are thorium ($^{232}\mathrm{Th}$), protactinium ($^{231}\mathrm{Pa}$) and uranium ($^{238}\mathrm{U}$, $^{235}\mathrm{U}$). The rest are synthetic, produced in reactors or accelerators. Their handling requires shielding, containment, and careful waste management; the chemistry is often carried out on tracer scales.

## 6. Magnetic properties and colour

Actinoid ions are paramagnetic and many are coloured, but the magnetic moments are harder to interpret than for the lanthanoids because the $5f$ electrons are partially quenched by bonding and spin–orbit coupling is large. The colour of actinoid solutions is striking: $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$ is yellow, $\mathrm{NpO_2^+}$ is green, $\mathrm{PuO_2^{2+}}$ is pink.

## 7. Applications

The actinoids are not laboratory curiosities; they power and arm the modern world:

- **Uranium-235** is the fissile fuel of nuclear reactors and weapons; **uranium-238** is converted to plutonium-239 in breeder reactors.
- **Plutonium-239** is used as fuel in fast breeder reactors and in weapons.
- **Thorium-232** is a fertile material that can be converted to $^{233}\mathrm{U}$; thorium-based reactors are under development because thorium is more abundant and produces less long-lived waste.
- **Americium-241** is used in smoke detectors (its alpha particles ionise air) and in medical and industrial sources.
- **Curium** and **californium** are used as neutron sources in oil-well logging and in neutron activation analysis.
- **Depleted uranium** (mostly $^{238}\mathrm{U}$) is used in radiation shielding and in armour-piercing munitions because of its high density.

## 8. Worked example: oxidation state of uranium in an oxo ion

**Example.** Find the oxidation state of uranium in the uranyl ion $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$.

Let $x$ be the oxidation state of U. Each oxygen is −2:

$$
x + 2(-2) = +2 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 4 = +2 \;\Rightarrow\; x = +6.
$$

So uranium is in the +6 state in the uranyl ion, the most stable high oxidation state of uranium in aqueous solution. This is the state in which uranium is leached and processed during fuel reprocessing.

Sanity check: $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$ is a well-known, stable, yellow dioxo cation; a +6 state for uranium is consistent with its position early in the actinoid series, where high oxidation states are accessible.

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Actinoid mistakes">

- **Treating the actinoids as just heavier lanthanoids.** The $5f$ electrons participate in bonding, giving more variable oxidation states and stronger complex formation.
- **Assuming all actinoids are like uranium.** Only the early members (Th–Pu) show high oxidation states; from Am onward the +3 state dominates.
- **Ignoring radioactivity in stoichiometry problems.** Masses of actinoids are often given in grams but the chemistry is done on microgram scales; check the scale of the numbers.

</learning-block>

**C.** State one advantage of a thorium-based nuclear fuel cycle over a uranium-based one.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Thorium is more abundant in the Earth's crust than uranium, and the thorium cycle produces less long-lived radioactive waste and no plutonium, reducing proliferation risk. (Any one well-stated advantage is acceptable.)

</details>

## 9. Nuclear fuel cycles in brief

In a uranium fuel cycle, $^{235}\mathrm{U}$ fissions directly; the abundant $^{238}\mathrm{U}$ captures neutrons to become $^{239}\mathrm{Pu}$, which is also fissile. In a thorium cycle, $^{232}\mathrm{Th}$ captures a neutron to become $^{233}\mathrm{U}$, which is fissile. The thorium cycle generates less transuranic waste and $^{233}\mathrm{U}$ is harder to divert to weapons, but it requires a starter fissile material and more complex reprocessing.

## 10. Early actinoid oxidation states at a glance

| Element | Dominant states | Most stable oxo ion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Th | +4 | — |
| Pa | +5 | — |
| U | +3, +4, +5, +6 | $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$ (yellow) |
| Np | +3 to +7 | $\mathrm{NpO_2^+}$ (green) |
| Pu | +3 to +7 | $\mathrm{PuO_2^{2+}}$ (pink) |
| Am | +3 (main), +4 to +6 | — |
| Cm–Lr | +3 | — |

The steady narrowing of accessible states after plutonium mirrors the lanthanoids once the $5f$ shell becomes core-like.

## Remember

- Actinoids = Th–Lr; general configuration $[\mathrm{Rn}]\,5f^{1-14}\,6d^{0-1}\,7s^2$.
- All actinoids are radioactive; only Th, Pa and U occur naturally.
- Oxidation states are more variable than in the lanthanoids, especially for Th–Pu (+3 to +7).
- The $5f$ electrons participate in bonding, giving stronger complex formation.
- The actinoid contraction is slightly larger than the lanthanoid contraction.
- Key uses: nuclear fuel (U, Pu, Th), smoke detectors (Am), neutron sources (Cf).
