## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 15 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 10 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one calculation consume the paper.

The goal is not only an answer. Before writing a formula, name the species and the oxidation state or configuration it rests on.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground-state configuration | $(n-1)d^{1-10}\,ns^{0-2}$ | Check the Cr and Cu exceptions |
| Cation configuration | Remove $ns$ before $(n-1)d$ | Count unpaired electrons after removal |
| Magnetic moment | $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\ \mathrm{BM}$ | 3d estimate only; f-block moments differ |
| Oxidation state | Charge balance (O = −2) | Fractional value ⇒ mixed valence |
| Permanganate in acid | $\mathrm{MnO_4^- \rightarrow Mn^{2+}}$, 5 e⁻ | Medium determines the product |
| Dichromate in acid | $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} \rightarrow Cr^{3+}}$, 6 e⁻ | Chromate ⇌ dichromate with pH |
| Lanthanoid radius / basicity | Lanthanoid contraction | Poor 4f shielding |
| Actinoid oxidation state | Early members variable; later +3 | All are radioactive |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If your answer gives a colourless ion with unpaired d electrons, or a +7 oxidation state for a late lanthanoid, or a magnetic moment larger than $\sqrt{35}\ \mathrm{BM}$ for a 3d ion, stop and revisit the configuration.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Chromium configuration

The ground-state electronic configuration of chromium (Z = 24) is:

- A. $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^4\,4s^2$
- B. $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5\,4s^1$
- C. $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6\,4s^0$
- D. $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^3\,4s^2\,4p^1$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Chromium takes the half-filled $3d^5$ configuration with one $4s$ electron for extra exchange stability.

</details>

### 2. Magnetic moment

The spin-only magnetic moment of $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ is closest to:

- A. $3.87\ \mathrm{BM}$
- B. $4.90\ \mathrm{BM}$
- C. $5.92\ \mathrm{BM}$
- D. $2.83\ \mathrm{BM}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}} = [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6$ has 4 unpaired electrons, so $\mu = \sqrt{4(6)} = \sqrt{24} = 4.90\ \mathrm{BM}$.

</details>

### 3. Colourless ion

Which of the following ions is colourless?

- A. $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$
- B. $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$
- C. $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$
- D. $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}} = [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^0$ has no d electrons, so no d–d transition and no colour. The others have partially filled d subshells.

</details>

### 4. Oxidation state of manganese

The oxidation state of manganese in $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ is:

- A. +4
- B. +5
- C. +6
- D. +7

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** $1 + x + 4(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = +7$.

</details>

### 5. Permanganate reduction

In strongly acidic medium, $\mathrm{MnO_4^-}$ is reduced to:

- A. $\mathrm{MnO_2}$
- B. $\mathrm{MnO_4^{2-}}$
- C. $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$
- D. $\mathrm{Mn^{3+}}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** In acid, $\mathrm{MnO_4^- + 8H^+ + 5e^- \rightarrow Mn^{2+} + 4H_2O}$ (colourless product, self-indicating titration).

</details>

### 6. Lanthanoid contraction

The lanthanoid contraction is caused by:

- A. poor shielding by $5d$ electrons
- B. poor shielding by $4f$ electrons
- C. increasing number of neutrons
- D. relativistic contraction of the $6s$ orbital

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The $4f$ electrons are diffuse and shield the increasing nuclear charge poorly, so the radius shrinks from Ce to Lu.

</details>

### 7. Stable +4 lanthanoid

Which lanthanoid has the most stable +4 oxidation state?

- A. Nd
- B. Ce
- C. Gd
- D. Lu

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}$ has the $4f^0$ configuration, which is especially stable. $\mathrm{Tb^{4+}}$ ($4f^7$) is the other common +4 case.

</details>

### 8. Uranyl ion

The uranyl ion $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$ contains uranium in oxidation state:

- A. +4
- B. +5
- C. +6
- D. +7

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $x + 2(-2) = +2 \Rightarrow x = +6$.

</details>

### 9. Interstitial compound

Which of the following is an interstitial compound?

- A. $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$
- B. $\mathrm{TiC}$
- C. $\mathrm{NaCl}$
- D. $\mathrm{CaF_2}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $\mathrm{TiC}$ has carbon atoms in the interstices of the titanium lattice, giving a hard, high-melting, inert solid. The others are ionic or stoichiometric oxides.

</details>

### 10. Non-stoichiometric oxide

A non-stoichiometric compound among the following is:

- A. $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$
- B. $\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$
- C. $\mathrm{Fe_{0.95}O}$
- D. $\mathrm{CuO}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $\mathrm{Fe_{0.95}O}$ has a variable metal-to-oxygen ratio due to the variable oxidation state of iron; the others are stoichiometric.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Unpaired electrons in gadolinium(III)

Gd (Z = 64) has configuration $[\mathrm{Xe}]\,4f^7\,5d^1\,6s^2$. The number of unpaired electrons in $\mathrm{Gd^{3+}}$ is:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\mathrm{Gd^{3+}} = [\mathrm{Xe}]\,4f^7$ (the $5d$ and $6s$ electrons are removed). The $4f^7$ subshell is half-filled, so there are **7** unpaired electrons.

</details>

### 12. Magnetic moment of Mn²⁺

The spin-only magnetic moment of $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$ (in BM) is $\sqrt{35}$. Its numerical value to two decimal places is:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\sqrt{35} = 5.916\ldots \approx \mathbf{5.92}\ \mathrm{BM}$.

</details>

### 13. Equivalence of permanganate

The equivalent mass of $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ in acidic medium, given its molar mass is $158\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, is:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In acid, $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ gains 5 electrons per formula unit, so equivalent mass = $158/5 = \mathbf{31.6}\ \mathrm{g\,equiv^{-1}}$.

</details>

### 14. Dichromate oxidation state

The oxidation state of chromium in $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$ (an integer) is:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$2(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{+6}$.

</details>

### 15. Lanthanoid contraction magnitude

The approximate total decrease in the $\mathrm{Ln^{3+}}$ ionic radius from $\mathrm{La^{3+}}$ ($103\ \mathrm{pm}$) to $\mathrm{Lu^{3+}}$ ($86\ \mathrm{pm}$) is:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$103 - 86 = \mathbf{17\ \mathrm{pm}}$ (any value in the 15–18 pm range is accepted as the approximate contraction).

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

### 16. Zinc as a transition element

**Assertion (A):** Zinc is a transition element.  
**Reason (R):** Zinc has an incompletely filled d subshell in its common oxidation state.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. Both A and R are false.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Zinc is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}\,4s^2$ and $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}$; the d subshell is full, so zinc is **not** a transition element. Both statements are false.

</details>

### 17. Lanthanoid contraction and Hf/Zr

**Assertion (A):** Hafnium and zirconium have almost identical atomic radii.  
**Reason (R):** The $4f$ electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly, increasing the effective nuclear charge on hafnium.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. Both A and R are false.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The poor shielding by the $4f$ electrons is exactly why the 5d element Hf is shrunk to the size of the 4d element Zr. Both true and correctly linked.

</details>

### 18. Colour of KMnO₄

**Assertion (A):** Aqueous $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ is deep purple.  
**Reason (R):** Manganese in $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ is in the +7 oxidation state.

- A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. Both A and R are false.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Both statements are true, but the purple colour is a charge-transfer (ligand-to-metal) transition, not a consequence of the +7 oxidation state per se. R does not explain A.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Match the metal to its characteristic oxidation state

Match each metal (column I) with the oxidation state it most characteristically shows in its common oxo compound (column II).

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| P. Cr | 1. +7 |
| Q. Mn | 2. +6 |
| R. Fe | 3. +2 and +3 |
| S. Cu | 4. +1 and +2 |

- A. P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4
- B. P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4
- C. P-2, Q-1, R-4, S-3
- D. P-3, Q-1, R-2, S-4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Cr is +6 in dichromate/chromate, Mn is +7 in permanganate, Fe shows +2 and +3, Cu shows +1 and +2.

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement: actinoids

Which of the following statements about the actinoids are correct?

1. All actinoids are radioactive.
2. The +3 oxidation state is the only state shown by every actinoid.
3. The $5f$ electrons participate in bonding more than the $4f$ electrons of the lanthanoids.
4. The actinoid contraction is smaller than the lanthanoid contraction.

- A. 1 and 3 only
- B. 1, 2 and 3 only
- C. 1, 3 and 4 only
- D. 1, 2, 3 and 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Statement 1 is true (all actinoids are radioactive). Statement 2 is false (early actinoids show +4 to +7). Statement 3 is true ($5f$ orbitals are less buried). Statement 4 is false (the actinoid contraction is slightly larger, not smaller).

</details>
