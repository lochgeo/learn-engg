## Mastery check

Use $g$ is not needed here; use standard atomic data as given. Try each without looking at the answer first.

### 1. Configuration of a cation

Write the ground-state configuration of $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Cr is $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5\,4s^1$. Removing two electrons takes the $4s$ electron first, then one $3d$ electron: $\mathrm{Cr^{2+}} = [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^4$. Unpaired electrons = 4.

</details>

### 2. Magnetic moment

Compute the spin-only magnetic moment of $\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{Mn^{2+}} = [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5$, so $n = 5$. $\mu = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = \sqrt{35} = 5.92\ \mathrm{BM}$.

</details>

### 3. Oxidation state

Find the oxidation state of manganese in $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ and of chromium in $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{KMnO_4}$: $1 + x + 4(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = +7$. $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$: $2 + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = +6$.

</details>

### 4. Permanganate stoichiometry

In acid, $20.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$ of $0.0200\ \mathrm{M}$ $\mathrm{KMnO_4}$ is used to titrate $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$. How many moles of $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ are oxidised?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$n(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}) = 0.0200 \times 0.0200 = 4.00\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{mol}$. In acid each permanganate takes 5 electrons, so $n(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}) = 5 \times 4.00\times10^{-4} = 2.00\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol}$.

</details>

### 5. Dichromate stoichiometry

How many moles of $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$ are oxidised by $0.0100\ \mathrm{mol}$ of $\mathrm{K_2Cr_2O_7}$ in acid?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Each dichromate takes 6 electrons in acid, so $n(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}) = 6 \times 0.0100 = 0.0600\ \mathrm{mol}$.

</details>

### 6. Lanthanoid contraction

Explain why Hf and Zr have almost identical atomic radii and are so difficult to separate.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The lanthanoids (Ce–Lu) precede Hf and fill the $4f$ subshell. The $4f$ electrons shield the $5d$ electrons poorly, so the effective nuclear charge on Hf is much larger than a simple period trend predicts, shrinking its radius to match Zr's. Their compounds then have nearly identical solubilities.

</details>

### 7. Lanthanoid oxidation state

Which lanthanoid ions are +2 or +4, and what makes those states stable?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{Ce^{4+}}$ ($4f^0$) and $\mathrm{Tb^{4+}}$ ($4f^7$) are stable +4 ions; $\mathrm{Eu^{2+}}$ ($4f^7$) and $\mathrm{Yb^{2+}}$ ($4f^{14}$) are stable +2 ions. In each case the resulting $4f$ configuration is empty, half-filled or fully filled.

</details>

### 8. Actinoid oxidation state

Find the oxidation state of uranium in $\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$ and of thorium in $\mathrm{ThO_2}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{UO_2^{2+}}$: $x + 2(-2) = +2 \Rightarrow x = +6$. $\mathrm{ThO_2}$: $x + 2(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = +4$. Thorium's dominant state is +4; uranium's most stable aqueous high state is +6.

</details>

### 9. Colour and magnetism

Which is colourless and diamagnetic: $\mathrm{Sc^{3+}}$ or $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}}$? Justify.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{Sc^{3+}} = [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^0$ has no d electrons, so no d–d transition (colourless) and no unpaired electrons (diamagnetic). $\mathrm{Ti^{3+}} = [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^1$ is coloured (purple) and paramagnetic.

</details>

### 10. Property trend

Arrange $\mathrm{La^{3+}}$, $\mathrm{Gd^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{Lu^{3+}}$ in order of (a) increasing radius, (b) increasing basicity.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

(a) Radius decreases across the series: $\mathrm{Lu^{3+}} < \mathrm{Gd^{3+}} < \mathrm{La^{3+}}$. (b) Basicity follows radius: $\mathrm{Lu^{3+}} < \mathrm{Gd^{3+}} < \mathrm{La^{3+}}$.

</details>
