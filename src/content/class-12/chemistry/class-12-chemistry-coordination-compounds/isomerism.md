## Same formula, different arrangement

Two complexes can have the same molecular formula yet differ in how their atoms are connected or arranged in space. These are **isomers**, and they often have different colours, solubilities and reactivities. Coordination chemistry is where Werner’s theory becomes experimentally decisive: the number of isomers and their properties distinguish inner-sphere from outer-sphere binding.

## 1. Structural isomerism

The ligands or counter ions are connected differently.

- **Ionisation isomerism**: a ligand and a counter ion exchange places. Example: $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5SO_4}]\mathrm{Br}$ and $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5Br}]\mathrm{SO_4}$. The first has free bromide and gives a precipitate with $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$; the second has free sulphate and gives a precipitate with $\mathrm{BaCl_2}$.
- **Hydrate isomerism**: water is either a ligand or a water of crystallisation. Example: $[\mathrm{Cr(H_2O)_6}]\mathrm{Cl_3}$ (violet), $[\mathrm{Cr(H_2O)_5Cl}]\mathrm{Cl_2\cdot H_2O}$ (light green), $[\mathrm{Cr(H_2O)_4Cl_2}]\mathrm{Cl\cdot 2H_2O}$ (dark green).
- **Linkage isomerism**: an ambidentate ligand binds through different donor atoms. $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5NO_2}]^{2+}$ can be **nitro** (Co–N) or **nitrito** (Co–O); $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5SCN}]^{2+}$ can be thiocyanato (Co–S) or isothiocyanato (Co–N). The two forms have different colours and stabilities.
- **Coordination isomerism**: in a salt containing both a complex cation and a complex anion, ligands can be exchanged between the two metal centres. Example: $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}][\mathrm{Cr(CN)_6}]$ and $[\mathrm{Cr(NH_3)_6}][\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]$.

## 2. Stereoisomerism

Here the connectivity is the same but the spatial arrangement differs.

### Geometrical isomerism

- **Square planar (MA₂B₂)**: *cis* (adjacent) and *trans* (opposite). The classic example is $[\mathrm{Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2}]$: cisplatin (cis) is an anticancer drug, while the trans isomer is therapeutically inactive. This difference made geometrical isomerism medically important.
- **Octahedral (MA₄B₂)**: also cis and trans, e.g. $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2}]^+$.
- **Octahedral (MA₃B₃)**: *fac* (three identical ligands on one face) and *mer* (three in a meridian plane). $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_3(NO_2)_3}]$ exists as both fac and mer forms, which can be separated.
- **Octahedral (M(AA)₂B₂)** with a bidentate ligand: cis and trans forms, where the cis form is chiral.

### Optical isomerism

A complex is optically active if it is chiral — no plane or centre of symmetry. Octahedral complexes with three bidentate ligands, such as $[\mathrm{Co(en)_3}]^{3+}$, exist as non-superimposable mirror images (Δ and Λ). Certain cis-octahedral complexes with two bidentate ligands, such as cis-$[\mathrm{Co(en)_2Cl_2}]^+$, are also chiral; their trans counterparts have a plane of symmetry and are achiral.

The Δ/Λ notation describes the handedness of the helix formed by the chelate rings; d/l notation is older and refers to the direction of rotation of plane-polarised light.

## 3. Counting isomers: a worked example

Take $[\mathrm{Co(en)_2Cl_2}]^+$ (octahedral, two bidentate en and two chloro ligands).

- The two chlorides can be **trans** (opposite vertices): one achiral isomer.
- The two chlorides can be **cis** (adjacent vertices): this arrangement has no plane or centre of symmetry, so it exists as a pair of enantiomers.

Total: **three stereoisomers** (one trans + a pair of cis enantiomers). This is a standard result worth memorising, but the reasoning — check for symmetry elements — is the transferable skill.

## 4. A symmetry checklist

To decide whether a complex is optically active:

1. Draw the arrangement (or use the formula type).
2. Look for a **plane of symmetry** or a **centre of symmetry**.
3. If either is present, the complex is achiral (optically inactive).
4. If neither is present, it is chiral and may have an enantiomer.

Geometrical isomers are distinguished by the relative positions of identical ligands; optical isomers require chirality.

## 5. A reliable isomerism method

1. Write the formula type (MA₆, MA₅B, MA₄B₂, MA₃B₃, M(AA)₃, etc.).
2. List the structural isomerism types that the ligands permit (ionisation, hydrate, linkage, coordination).
3. For stereoisomerism, apply the geometry rules above and check symmetry.
4. Count isomers and note which are chiral.

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Isomerism errors">
- **Calling every cis complex optically active.** The cis form must lack both a plane and a centre of symmetry; many cis complexes still have a plane.
- **Confusing fac/mer with cis/trans.** fac/mer applies to MA₃B₃ octahedral complexes; cis/trans applies to MA₂B₂ or MA₄B₂.
- **Treating linkage isomers as geometrical isomers.** Linkage isomerism is structural: the donor atom differs.
- **Forgetting that ionisation isomers differ in the species precipitated** by $\mathrm{AgNO_3}$ or $\mathrm{BaCl_2}$.
</learning-block>

## Try it yourself

**A.** How many geometrical isomers does square-planar $[\mathrm{Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2}]$ have?

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Two: cis and trans. The cis form is the basis of cisplatin.

</details>

**B.** Is $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+}$ optically active?

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

No. It is octahedral with six identical ligands and has several planes of symmetry, so it is achiral.

</details>

**C.** Which type of isomerism is shown by $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5NO_2}]^{2+}$ and $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5ONO}]^{2+}$?

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Linkage isomerism: the nitrite ion binds through nitrogen in the first (nitro) and through oxygen in the second (nitrito).

</details>

## Check your understanding

Why does cis-$[\mathrm{Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2}]$ have medicinal value while its trans isomer does not, even though both have the same formula?

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

Biological recognition depends on shape. The cis arrangement places the two reactive chloride ligands on adjacent positions, allowing the complex to bind to adjacent DNA bases and disrupt replication. The trans arrangement cannot bind in the same way, so it is inactive.

</details>

## Remember

- Structural isomerism changes connectivity; stereoisomerism changes spatial arrangement.
- Square planar MA₂B₂ gives cis and trans; octahedral MA₃B₃ gives fac and mer.
- Chirality requires the absence of both a plane and a centre of symmetry.
- $[\mathrm{Co(en)_2Cl_2}]^+$ has three stereoisomers: trans (achiral) plus a cis enantiomeric pair.
- Always check symmetry before declaring a complex optically active.
