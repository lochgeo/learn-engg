## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 5 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one calculation consume the paper.

The goal is not only an answer. Before writing an equation, name the system: Werner sphere, nomenclature, isomer type, spin state, CFSE, or 18-electron count.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formula with brackets | Separate sphere from counter ions; charge-balance the metal | Do not count counter ions in CN |
| Coordination number | Count donor atoms, including denticity | en counts as 2, EDTA as 6 |
| Oxidation state | Metal + ligand charges = complex charge | Neutral ligands contribute 0 |
| Name ↔ formula | Ligands alphabetically; -o for anions; -ate for anionic complexes | Prefixes (di-, bis-) are ignored in alphabetising |
| Isomer count | Formula type (MA₆, MA₄B₂, MA₃B₃, M(AA)₃…) | Check symmetry before claiming chirality |
| High/low spin | Spectrochemical series vs pairing energy $P$ | Only $d^4$–$d^7$ octahedral have spin ambiguity |
| CFSE (octahedral) | $(-0.4 n_{t_{2g}} + 0.6 n_{e_g})\Delta_o$ | Add pairing correction when comparing spin states |
| Magnetic moment | $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\ \mathrm{BM}$ | $n=0$ ⇒ diamagnetic |
| Tetrahedral splitting | $\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$, inverted | Almost always high spin |
| Metal carbonyl | 18-electron rule: metal valence + $2\times$CO | Look for a metal–metal bond if short |
| EAN | $Z - \text{ox. state} + \text{donated e}^-$ | A guide, not a law |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="Fastest sanity checks">
If your answer gives a negative number of unpaired electrons, a coordination number larger than 12 for a simple complex, or an EAN that you treat as proof of stability, stop and revisit the setup. A correct-looking number can still come from the wrong rule.
</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Coordination number

The coordination number of cobalt in $[\mathrm{Co(en)_2Cl_2}]^+$ is:

- A. 4
- B. 5
- C. 6
- D. 8

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Each ethane-1,2-diamine (en) is bidentate (2 donor atoms), so two give 4; two chlorides give 2. Total CN = 6.

</details>

### 2. Nomenclature

The correct IUPAC name of $\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6]}$ is:

- A. potassium hexacyanoiron(II)
- B. potassium hexacyanoferrate(II)
- C. potassium hexacyanoferrate(III)
- D. tetrapotassium hexacyanoiron(II)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The complex is anionic, so iron → ferrate. Charge balance: $x + 6(-1) = -4$, so Fe is +2. Hence **potassium hexacyanoferrate(II)**.

</details>

### 3. Isomerism type

The pair $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5NO_2}]^{2+}$ and $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5ONO}]^{2+}$ differs by:

- A. ionisation isomerism
- B. linkage isomerism
- C. geometrical isomerism
- D. optical isomerism

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The nitrite ligand is ambidentate: it binds through N (nitro) in the first and through O (nitrito) in the second. This is linkage isomerism.

</details>

### 4. Spin state

$[\mathrm{Fe(CN)_6}]^{4-}$ is:

- A. high spin, paramagnetic
- B. high spin, diamagnetic
- C. low spin, paramagnetic
- D. low spin, diamagnetic

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is a strong-field ligand, so Fe(II) ($d^6$) is low spin: $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$, with 0 unpaired electrons ⇒ diamagnetic.

</details>

### 5. CFSE

The CFSE of a $d^3$ octahedral complex is:

- A. $-0.4\Delta_o$
- B. $-0.8\Delta_o$
- C. $-1.2\Delta_o$
- D. $+1.2\Delta_o$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** $d^3 \rightarrow t_{2g}^3 e_g^0$. CFSE $= (-0.4)(3)\Delta_o = -1.2\Delta_o$.

</details>

### 6. Tetrahedral splitting

For a tetrahedral complex, $\Delta_t$ is approximately:

- A. $\frac{9}{4}\Delta_o$
- B. $\frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$
- C. $\frac{2}{3}\Delta_o$
- D. $2\Delta_o$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$, and the splitting is inverted relative to the octahedral case.

</details>

### 7. 18-electron rule

$\mathrm{Ni(CO)_4}$ has how many valence electrons around nickel?

- A. 10
- B. 14
- C. 16
- D. 18

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Ni(0) contributes 10 valence electrons; four CO contribute $4\times 2 = 8$. Total = 18.

</details>

### 8. EAN

The effective atomic number of cobalt in $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+}$ is:

- A. 24
- B. 27
- C. 33
- D. 36

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Co has $Z=27$ and oxidation state +3, contributing $27-3=24$ electrons. Six $\mathrm{NH_3}$ donate $6\times 2=12$. EAN = 24 + 12 = 36 (krypton).

</details>

### 9. Strongest field ligand

Among $\mathrm{Cl^-}$, $\mathrm{H_2O}$, $\mathrm{NH_3}$ and CO, the strongest field ligand is:

- A. $\mathrm{Cl^-}$
- B. $\mathrm{H_2O}$
- C. $\mathrm{NH_3}$
- D. CO

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The spectrochemical series places CO at the strong-field end. It is both a good sigma donor and a pi acceptor.

</details>

### 10. Medicinal complex

The anticancer drug cisplatin has the formula:

- A. trans-$[\mathrm{Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2}]$
- B. cis-$[\mathrm{Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2}]$
- C. $[\mathrm{Pt(NH_3)_4}]^{2+}$
- D. $[\mathrm{PtCl_4}]^{2-}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Only the cis isomer can bind adjacent DNA bases and disrupt replication; the trans isomer is inactive.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Magnetic moment

Find the spin-only magnetic moment (in BM) of $[\mathrm{Mn(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}$. (Mn(II) = $d^5$, high spin.)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

High spin $d^5$: $t_{2g}^3 e_g^2$, all five electrons unpaired, so $n=5$.

$$
\mu = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = \sqrt{35} \approx 5.92\ \mathrm{BM}.
$$

</details>

### 12. CFSE in kJ mol⁻¹

For $[\mathrm{Cr(H_2O)_6}]^{3+}$, $\Delta_o = 2.0\times 10^2\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Find the CFSE.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Cr(III) = $d^3 \rightarrow t_{2g}^3 e_g^0$.

$$
\mathrm{CFSE} = (-0.4)(3)\Delta_o = -1.2\Delta_o = -1.2(2.0\times10^2) = -2.4\times10^2\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

</details>

### 13. EAN

Compute the EAN of nickel in $[\mathrm{Ni(CN)_4}]^{2-}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Charge balance: $x + 4(-1) = -2$, so Ni is +2. $Z(\mathrm{Ni})=28$. Metal contributes $28-2=26$ electrons; four cyanides donate $4\times 2=8$. EAN = $26+8 = 34$.

</details>

### 14. Low-spin CFSE

For low-spin $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}]^{3+}$, $\Delta_o = 2.30\times 10^4\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. Find the CFSE in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Co(III) = $d^6$, low spin: $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$.

$$
\mathrm{CFSE} = (-0.4)(6)\Delta_o = -2.4\Delta_o = -2.4(2.30\times10^4) = -5.52\times10^4\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}.
$$

</details>

### 15. Ionisable chlorides

How many ionisable chlorides does $\mathrm{CoCl_3\cdot 4NH_3}$ contain in Werner’s formulation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The formula corresponds to $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2}]\mathrm{Cl}$: four ammine and two chloro ligands in the sphere, one chloride outside. Hence **1** ionisable chloride.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

### 16.

**Assertion (A):** $[\mathrm{CoF_6}]^{3-}$ is paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons.

**Reason (R):** $\mathrm{F^-}$ is a weak-field ligand, so Co(III) ($d^6$) is high spin.

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** High spin $d^6$: $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$, giving 4 unpaired electrons. The weak field of $\mathrm{F^-}$ is exactly why the complex is high spin, so R explains A.

</details>

### 17.

**Assertion (A):** Tetrahedral complexes of first-row transition metals are almost always high spin.

**Reason (R):** The tetrahedral splitting $\Delta_t$ is much smaller than the pairing energy $P$.

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** $\Delta_t \approx \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$, so $\Delta_t < P$ for these metals; electrons remain unpaired. R explains A.

</details>

### 18.

**Assertion (A):** CO is a strong-field ligand.

**Reason (R):** CO accepts electron density from the metal into its $\pi^*$ orbitals (back-donation).

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Back-donation is part of the synergic effect that makes CO a strong field ligand: it lowers the $t_{2g}$ energy and increases $\Delta$. Thus R explains A.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Matching: isomerism type

Match each pair with the type of isomerism it illustrates.

| Pair | Type |
| --- | --- |
| (i) $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5NO_2}]^{2+}$ and $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5ONO}]^{2+}$ | (p) linkage |
| (ii) cis- and trans-$[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2}]^+$ | (q) geometrical |
| (iii) $\Delta$- and $\Lambda$-$[\mathrm{Co(en)_3}]^{3+}$ | (r) optical |
| (iv) $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_6}][\mathrm{Cr(CN)_6}]$ and $[\mathrm{Cr(NH_3)_6}][\mathrm{Co(CN)_6}]$ | (s) coordination |

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i)–(p) linkage; (ii)–(q) geometrical; (iii)–(r) optical; (iv)–(s) coordination.

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement

Which of the following statements are correct?

- (a) In an octahedral field, the $t_{2g}$ set lies lower in energy than the $e_g$ set.
- (b) High-spin $d^6$ has four unpaired electrons.
- (c) $\Delta_t = \frac{9}{4}\Delta_o$.
- (d) Back-donation in metal carbonyls lowers the C–O stretching frequency.

- A. (a), (b) and (d) only
- B. (a), (b) and (c) only
- C. (a) and (d) only
- D. All four

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** (a) true; (b) true ($t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$); (c) false ($\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o$); (d) true. Hence (a), (b) and (d) only.

</details>
