## Mastery check

Use standard atomic numbers where needed (Co = 27, Fe = 26, Pt = 78, Cr = 24, Ni = 28); no value of $g$ is required in this chapter.

### 1. Coordination number versus oxidation number

In $[\mathrm{Co(en)_2Cl_2}]^+$, state the coordination number, the oxidation number of cobalt, and whether the complex is homoleptic or heteroleptic.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Two en (bidentate) give 4 donor atoms; two Cl⁻ give 2; CN = 6. Charge balance: $x + 2(0) + 2(-1) = +1$, so $x = +3$. The ligands are of two kinds, so the complex is **heteroleptic**.

</details>

### 2. Naming

Name $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_4(H_2O)Cl}]^{2+}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Ligands: four ammine, one aqua, one chloro. Ignoring prefixes, “ammine” (a-m) comes before “aqua” (a-q), which comes before “chloro” (c). Hence the name is **tetraammineaquachlorocobalt(III) ion**. (Charge balance: Co is +3 because 4(0)+0+(-1)+x = +2.)

</details>

### 3. Formula from a name

Write the formula of **potassium hexacyanoferrate(III)**.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}$. Iron is +3; six cyanides give −6; net −3; three $\mathrm{K^+}$ balance.

</details>

### 4. Isomer count

How many stereoisomers does $[\mathrm{Co(en)_2Cl_2}]^+$ have, and which are chiral?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Three: one trans (achiral, with a plane of symmetry) and a pair of cis enantiomers (chiral).

</details>

### 5. Linkage isomerism

Give the two linkage isomers of $[\mathrm{Co(NH_3)_5NO_2}]^{2+}$ and identify the donor atom in each.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Nitro (Co–N) and nitrito (Co–O). The nitrite ion is ambidentate.

</details>

### 6. High spin or low spin?

For $[\mathrm{Fe(CN)_6}]^{4-}$ and $[\mathrm{Fe(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}$, state the spin state, the $t_{2g}/e_g$ configuration and the number of unpaired electrons.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

- $[\mathrm{Fe(CN)_6}]^{4-}$: $\mathrm{CN^-}$ is strong field → low spin: $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$, 0 unpaired.
- $[\mathrm{Fe(H_2O)_6}]^{2+}$: $\mathrm{H_2O}$ is weak field → high spin: $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$, 4 unpaired.

</details>

### 7. CFSE

Compute the CFSE of $[\mathrm{Cr(H_2O)_6}]^{3+}$ (Cr(III) = $d^3$) in terms of $\Delta_o$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$d^3$ → $t_{2g}^3 e_g^0$. $\mathrm{CFSE}=(-0.4)(3)\Delta_o = -1.2\Delta_o$.

</details>

### 8. Magnetic moment

A complex has 3 unpaired electrons. Compute its spin-only magnetic moment.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mu = \sqrt{3(3+2)} = \sqrt{15} \approx 3.87\ \mathrm{BM}$.

</details>

### 9. 18-electron rule

Verify the 18-electron rule for $\mathrm{Fe(CO)_5}$ and state what it predicts about stability.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Fe(0) contributes 8 valence electrons; five CO contribute $5\times 2 = 10$. Total = 18, predicting a stable, diamagnetic carbonyl.

</details>

### 10. Application

Which coordination compound is used in the Mond process, and what is the key reaction?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$\mathrm{Ni(CO)_4}$: impure nickel reacts with CO at moderate temperature to form the volatile carbonyl, which decomposes to pure nickel at a higher temperature.

</details>
