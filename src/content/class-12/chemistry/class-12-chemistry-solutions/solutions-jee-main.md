## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Use $g=10\ \text{m/s}^2$ where relevant (rarely needed here). Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 20 on Section B, 10 on Section C, and 5 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one calculation consume the paper.

Name the system before writing an equation: is the solute volatile or non-volatile, an electrolyte or a non-electrolyte, and which concentration unit does the formula demand?

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concentration from masses | Moles of solute and solvent; mole fraction, molarity or molality | State the denominator (solution volume vs solvent mass). |
| Gas in liquid; pressure given | Henry’s law $p=K_H x$ | $K_H$ units and temperature. |
| Solvent vapour pressure above solution | Raoult’s law $p_1=x_1p_1^\circ$ | Mole fraction, not molarity. |
| Two volatile liquids | Dalton’s law $p_{\text{total}}=x_1p_1^\circ+x_2p_2^\circ$ | Vapour is richer in the more volatile component. |
| Heat of mixing / volume change | Ideal vs non-ideal deviation sign | Positive deviation ⇒ lower boiling point. |
| Boiling or freezing point shift | $\Delta T_b=K_b m$, $\Delta T_f=K_f m$ | Molality, with van’t Hoff factor if electrolyte. |
| Osmotic pressure | $\pi=MRT$ | Molarity, with van’t Hoff factor if electrolyte. |
| Unknown molar mass | Rearrange the colligative formula, include $i$ | Units and dilute-solution assumption. |
| Degree of dissociation | $i=1+\alpha(n-1)$ | $n$ = number of ions. |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">
If your answer gives a negative molar mass, a boiling point below 0 °C for water at 1 atm without a stated solute, or an $i$ larger than the number of possible ions, stop and revisit the setup. A number with the right unit can still come from the wrong concentration unit.
</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Mole fraction

A solution contains $0.5\ \text{mol}$ of solute and $4.5\ \text{mol}$ of solvent. The mole fraction of solute is:

- A. $0.05$
- B. $0.10$
- C. $0.11$
- D. $0.50$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** $x=0.5/(0.5+4.5)=0.5/5.0=0.10$.

</details>

### 2. Molarity versus molality

Which statement is correct for an aqueous solution at 25 °C?

- A. Molarity and molality are always equal.
- B. Molality is temperature-independent; molarity is not.
- C. Molarity uses the mass of the solvent; molality uses the volume of the solution.
- D. Neither depends on temperature.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Molality is based on solvent mass and does not change with temperature; molarity is based on solution volume and does.

</details>

### 3. Henry’s law

For a gas in a liquid, if the partial pressure of the gas is doubled at constant temperature, its solubility (mole fraction) becomes:

- A. half
- B. unchanged
- C. double
- D. four times

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Henry’s law $p=K_H x$ gives $x\propto p$ at fixed $T$.

</details>

### 4. Raoult’s law

The vapour pressure of a solvent above a solution of a non-volatile solute:

- A. increases
- B. decreases
- C. is unchanged
- D. depends on the solute’s chemical identity, not its amount

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Raoult’s law $p_1=x_1p_1^\circ$ with $x_1<1$ lowers the vapour pressure.

</details>

### 5. Deviation and boiling point

A mixture shows negative deviation from Raoult’s law. Its boiling point relative to the ideal prediction is:

- A. lower
- B. higher
- C. unchanged
- D. unpredictable

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Negative deviation means lower vapour pressure, hence a higher boiling point.

</details>

### 6. Colligative property choice

Which colligative property is most suitable for determining the molar mass of a protein?

- A. Elevation of boiling point
- B. Depression of freezing point
- C. Osmotic pressure
- D. Relative lowering of vapour pressure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Osmotic pressure is large even at very low concentrations, so measurable pressures are obtained with tiny solute amounts.

</details>

### 7. van’t Hoff factor

For complete dissociation of Al₂(SO₄)₃ in water, the van’t Hoff factor is:

- A. $2$
- B. $3$
- C. $5$
- D. $7$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Al₂(SO₄)₃ → 2 Al³⁺ + 3 SO₄²⁻ gives $i=5$.

</details>

### 8. Freezing point of an electrolyte

Compared with a non-electrolyte of the same molality, an electrolyte solution has a freezing point that is:

- A. higher
- B. lower
- C. the same
- D. not defined

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Dissociation increases the particle count, so $\Delta T_f=iK_f m$ is larger and the freezing point is lower.

</details>

### 9. Azeotrope

An azeotrope is best described as:

- A. a pure compound with a fixed boiling point
- B. a mixture that boils at constant composition
- C. an ideal solution
- D. a saturated solution

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** At the azeotropic composition the liquid and vapour have the same composition, so simple distillation cannot separate it.

</details>

### 10. Association effect

A solute that associates in solution (for example, dimerises) has a van’t Hoff factor:

- A. greater than 1
- B. equal to 1
- C. less than 1
- D. equal to the number of monomers

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Association reduces the particle count, so $i<1$.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Molarity from mass

$5.85\ \text{g}$ of NaCl ($M=58.5$) is dissolved in water and the solution made up to $250\ \text{mL}$. Find the molarity.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n=5.85/58.5=0.10\ \text{mol}$. $M=0.10/0.250=0.40\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$.

</details>

### 12. Freezing-point depression

$1.86\ \text{g}$ of a non-electrolyte ($M=93\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$) is dissolved in $100\ \text{g}$ of water. Find $\Delta T_f$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n=1.86/93=0.020\ \text{mol}$. $m=0.020/0.100=0.20\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}$. $\Delta T_f=1.86\times0.20=0.372\ \text{K}$.

</details>

### 13. Osmotic pressure

An aqueous solution at $300\ \text{K}$ has molarity $0.040\ \text{M}$ (non-electrolyte). Find the osmotic pressure in bar ($R=0.0831\ \text{L bar mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\pi=MRT=0.040\times0.0831\times300=0.997\ \text{bar}\approx1.00\ \text{bar}$.

</details>

### 14. Molar mass from freezing point

$0.80\ \text{g}$ of an unknown non-electrolyte in $80\ \text{g}$ of water depresses the freezing point by $0.372\ \text{K}$. Find its molar mass.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$M_2=\frac{K_f\,w_2\,1000}{\Delta T_f\,w_1}=\frac{1.86\times0.80\times1000}{0.372\times80}=\frac{1488}{29.76}=50\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

</details>

### 15. Electrolyte molar mass

$1.11\ \text{g}$ of CaCl₂ ($M=111$) is dissolved in $100\ \text{g}$ of water. Assuming complete dissociation, find $\Delta T_f$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n=1.11/111=0.010\ \text{mol}$. $m=0.010/0.100=0.10\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}$. With $i=3$, $\Delta T_f=3\times1.86\times0.10=0.558\ \text{K}$.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

### 16. Assertion and reason

**Assertion (A):** Molality is preferred over molarity for colligative-property calculations.

**Reason (R):** Molality does not change with temperature, whereas molarity does.

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both are true but R is not the correct explanation.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The temperature-independence of molality is exactly why it is used in $\Delta T_b=K_b m$ and $\Delta T_f=K_f m$.

</details>

### 17. Assertion and reason

**Assertion (A):** An aqueous solution of NaCl has a lower vapour pressure than pure water at the same temperature.

**Reason (R):** NaCl dissociates into Na⁺ and Cl⁻, increasing the number of solute particles.

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both are true but R is not the correct explanation.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Both are true, but the vapour-pressure lowering follows from Raoult’s law (solvent mole fraction $<1$) regardless of dissociation; dissociation affects the magnitude, not the existence of the lowering. The reason is a true statement about particle count but not the direct cause of the lowering.

</details>

### 18. Assertion and reason

**Assertion (A):** The osmotic pressure of a $0.1\ \text{M}$ glucose solution is lower than that of a $0.1\ \text{M}$ NaCl solution at the same temperature.

**Reason (R):** NaCl dissociates into two ions, doubling the particle concentration.

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both are true but R is not the correct explanation.
- C. A is true, R is false.
- D. A is false, R is true.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** $\pi=iMRT$; glucose $i=1$, NaCl $i\approx2$, so NaCl has the higher osmotic pressure. The dissociation is the correct explanation.

</details>

## Section D — 2 multi-statement questions

### 19. Matching: concentration and colligative

Match the quantity to its defining expression:

| Quantity | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| P. Molarity | 1. moles of solute per kg of solvent |
| Q. Molality | 2. moles of solute per litre of solution |
| R. Mole fraction | 3. dimensionless ratio of moles |
| S. Osmotic pressure | 4. $\pi=MRT$ |

- A. P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4
- B. P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4
- C. P-2, Q-1, R-4, S-3
- D. P-3, Q-1, R-2, S-4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** P-2 (molarity = mol L⁻¹ of solution), Q-1 (molality = mol kg⁻¹ of solvent), R-3 (mole fraction is dimensionless), S-4 ($\pi=MRT$).

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement

Which of the following statements are correct?

I. A solution of a volatile solute in a volatile solvent can form a minimum-boiling azeotrope when the deviation is positive.

II. The apparent molar mass of an electrolyte calculated without the van’t Hoff factor is smaller than its true molar mass.

III. Henry’s law constant $K_H$ has the same value for all gases in water.

- A. I and II only
- B. II and III only
- C. I and III only
- D. I, II and III

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** I is correct (positive deviation ⇒ minimum-boiling azeotrope). II is correct (dissociation inflates the particle count, so the uncorrected formula underestimates molar mass). III is false: $K_H$ is specific to each gas–solvent pair and temperature.

</details>
