## First choose the tool

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Mass or volume of solute and solvent given | Concentration: mass %, mole fraction, molarity or molality |
| Mixing or diluting solutions | Conserve solute: $M_1V_1=M_2V_2$ or weighted average |
| Gas dissolving in a liquid; pressure given | Henry’s law: $p=K_H x$ |
| Vapour pressure of solvent above solution | Raoult’s law: $p_1=x_1p_1^\circ$ |
| Two volatile components; total pressure asked | Dalton’s law: $p_{\text{total}}=x_1p_1^\circ+x_2p_2^\circ$ |
| Heat of mixing or volume change mentioned | Ideal vs non-ideal; check deviation sign |
| Boiling point or freezing point shift | $\Delta T_b=K_b m$, $\Delta T_f=K_f m$ (molality!) |
| Osmotic pressure given or asked | $\pi=MRT$ (molarity) |
| Unknown molar mass of a non-electrolyte | Rearrange a colligative formula |
| Electrolyte or associating solute | Include van’t Hoff factor $i$ |
| Degree of dissociation asked | $i=1+\alpha(n-1)$ |

## Mastery check

Try each without looking at the answer first. Use water constants $K_b=0.512$, $K_f=1.86$ and $g$-free SI units unless stated.

### 1. Mole fraction from masses

$18\ \text{g}$ of glucose ($M=180$) is dissolved in $180\ \text{g}$ of water. Find the mole fraction of glucose.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$n_{\text{glucose}}=0.10\ \text{mol}$, $n_{\text{water}}=10.0\ \text{mol}$. $x=0.10/10.10=0.0099$.

</details>

### 2. Dilution

How much water must be added to $50\ \text{mL}$ of $0.40\ \text{M}$ NaOH to make $0.10\ \text{M}$?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$0.40\times50=0.10\times V_2$, so $V_2=200\ \text{mL}$. Add $150\ \text{mL}$ of water.

</details>

### 3. Henry’s law

The Henry’s law constant for N₂ in water at 298 K is $8.42\times10^4\ \text{bar}$. Find the mole fraction of N₂ dissolved under a partial pressure of $0.78\ \text{bar}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$x=0.78/(8.42\times10^4)=9.26\times10^{-6}$.

</details>

### 4. Raoult’s law

Pure water vapour pressure at 300 K is $35.7\ \text{mmHg}$. A solution has $x_{\text{water}}=0.90$. Find the vapour pressure.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$p=0.90\times35.7=32.1\ \text{mmHg}$.

</details>

### 5. Ideal versus non-ideal

A mixture shows $\Delta H_{\text{mix}}>0$ and $\Delta V_{\text{mix}}>0$. Classify the deviation and state what happens to the boiling point.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Positive deviation: A–B interactions are weaker, vapour pressure is higher than Raoult’s law predicts, and the boiling point is lower (possible minimum-boiling azeotrope).

</details>

### 6. Freezing-point depression for an electrolyte

$1.49\ \text{g}$ of KCl is dissolved in $100\ \text{g}$ of water. Assuming complete dissociation, find the freezing point.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$m=(1.49/74.5)/0.100=0.200\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}$. With $i=2$, $\Delta T_f=2\times1.86\times0.200=0.744\ \text{K}$. Freezing point $=-0.74\ ^\circ\text{C}$.

</details>

### 7. Molar mass from osmotic pressure

An aqueous solution has $\pi=1.23\ \text{bar}$ at $300\ \text{K}$ and contains $5.0\ \text{g}$ of a non-electrolyte per litre. Find its molar mass.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$M=\pi/(RT)=1.23/(0.0831\times300)=0.0493\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$. Molar mass $=5.0/0.0493=101\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

</details>

### 8. Degree of dissociation

A $0.020\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}$ solution of a weak acid HX gives $\Delta T_f=0.0409\ \text{K}$. Find $\alpha$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Calculated $=1.86\times0.020=0.0372\ \text{K}$. $i=0.0409/0.0372=1.10$. $\alpha=i-1=0.10$.

</details>

### 9. Vapour pressure of a volatile mixture

Benzene ($p^\circ=95\ \text{mmHg}$) and toluene ($p^\circ=28\ \text{mmHg}$) form an ideal solution with $x_{\text{benzene}}=0.40$. Find the total vapour pressure and the mole fraction of benzene in the vapour.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$p_{\text{benzene}}=0.40\times95=38.0$; $p_{\text{toluene}}=0.60\times28=16.8$; $p_{\text{total}}=54.8\ \text{mmHg}$. $y_{\text{benzene}}=38.0/54.8=0.693$.

</details>

### 10. Molar mass with association

A solute of formula mass $60\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$ dimerises in a solvent. A $1.0\ \text{mol kg}^{-1}$ (by formula mass) solution gives $\Delta T_f=2.56\ \text{K}$ with $K_f=5.12$. Find the apparent molar mass.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$i=2.56/5.12=0.50$. Apparent molar mass $=M_{\text{true}}/i=60/0.50=120\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, consistent with complete dimerisation.

</details>
