## First choose the tool

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| "Is it a carbohydrate?" | Look for polyhydroxy aldehyde/ketone or a hydrolysis product — not the formula |
| "How many stereoisomers?" | $2^n$ from the number of chiral centres |
| "$D$ or $L$, $\alpha$ or $\beta$?" | $D/L$ from the penultimate carbon; $\alpha/\beta$ from the anomeric carbon |
| "Reducing or not?" | Is there a free anomeric carbon? Tollens' / Fehling's answer the test |
| "Bond or mass of a peptide?" | Linear chain: $n-1$ bonds, $n-1$ waters lost |
| "Protein shape or activity lost?" | Denaturation disrupts secondary and higher structure, not primary |
| "Enzyme rate?" | Michaelis–Menten $v=V_{\max}[S]/(K_m+[S])$; at $[S]=K_m$, $v=V_{\max}/2$ |
| "Stored in fat or excreted?" | ADEK are fat-soluble; B-complex and C are water-soluble |
| "Vitamin as coenzyme?" | Name the coenzyme form: TPP, FAD/$NAD^+$, CoA, PLP |
| "DNA base percentages?" | $[A]=[T]$, $[G]=[C]$; purines = pyrimidines |
| "Count hydrogen bonds" | 2 per A–T pair, 3 per G–C pair |
| "Complementary strand?" | Pair A–T and G–C, then reverse to write $5'\to3'$ |
| "Peptide / steroid hormone?" | Peptide: surface receptor and second messenger; steroid: inside the cell |
| "Glucose $mg/dL$ to $mmol/L$?" | Convert to $g/L$, then divide by $180\ \text{g/mol}$ |

## Mastery check

Try each question before revealing the answer.

### 1. Define and classify

Why is deoxyribose a carbohydrate even though its formula $C_5H_{10}O_4$ does not fit $C_n(H_2O)_m$? Name its family and sugar class.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The definition is structural, not formula-based: deoxyribose is a polyhydroxy aldehyde (once the ring opens), so it is a carbohydrate. The $C_n(H_2O)_m$ pattern is a rough guide with exceptions. It has five carbons and an aldehyde, so it is an aldopentose.

</details>

### 2. Count stereoisomers

How many stereoisomers are possible for a ketohexose such as fructose, and how many chiral centres does it have?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The carbonyl is at C2, leaving C3, C4 and C5 as chiral centres: $n=3$, so $2^3=8$ stereoisomers. Contrast an aldohexose, which has four chiral centres and $2^4=16$ stereoisomers.

</details>

### 3. Reducing behaviour

Sucrose and maltose are both disaccharides of glucose-containing units, but only one reduces Fehling's solution. Explain.

<details><summary>Show the reasoning</summary>

Maltose has an $\alpha(1\to4)$ link, leaving the anomeric carbon of the second glucose free; its ring can open to an aldehyde that reduces $Cu^{2+}$ to $Cu_2O$. In sucrose the link joins *both* anomeric carbons ($\alpha$-$C1$ of glucose to $\beta$-$C2$ of fructose), so no ring can open and sucrose is non-reducing.

</details>

### 4. Peptide mass

A linear pentapeptide is built from five amino acids of average molar mass $128\ \text{g/mol}$. Find its molar mass.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

A linear chain of 5 residues forms $5-1=4$ peptide bonds and loses 4 waters:

$$
M=5(128)-4(18)=640-72=568\ \text{g/mol}.
$$

Sanity check: the peptide must be lighter than the free amino acids by exactly the mass of water eliminated.

</details>

### 5. Enzyme rate

An enzyme has $V_{\max}=200\ \mu\text{mol/min}$ and $K_m=1.0\ \text{mmol/L}$. Find the rate at $[S]=3.0\ \text{mmol/L}$, and state what $[S]$ would give $150\ \mu\text{mol/min}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$$
v=\frac{200(3.0)}{1.0+3.0}=\frac{600}{4.0}=150\ \mu\text{mol/min}.
$$

Setting $150=200[S]/(1.0+[S])$ gives $150+150[S]=200[S]$, so $[S]=3.0\ \text{mmol/L}$ — the same value. The rate is $75\%$ of $V_{\max}$, consistent with $[S]$ being three times $K_m$.

</details>

### 6. Coenzymes

Name the vitamin precursor of $NAD^+$ and of coenzyme A, and classify both vitamins by solubility.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$NAD^+$ comes from niacin (vitamin $B_3$) and coenzyme A comes from pantothenic acid (vitamin $B_5$). Both are B vitamins, hence water soluble. Their coenzyme roles link this lesson back to enzyme catalysis.

</details>

### 7. Tablet arithmetic

A $250\ \text{mg}$ tablet of vitamin C ($C_6H_8O_6$, $M=176\ \text{g/mol}$) contains how many moles and how many molecules?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$250\ \text{mg}=0.250\ \text{g}$, so

$$
n=\frac{0.250}{176}=1.42\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol},
\qquad N=nN_A=(1.42\times10^{-3})(6.022\times10^{23})=8.55\times10^{20}\ \text{molecules}.
$$

</details>

### 8. Chargaff and hydrogen bonds

A double-stranded DNA has $24\%$ guanine and 2000 bases in total. Find all base percentages and the total number of hydrogen bonds.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$[C]=[G]=24\%$; the remaining $52\%$ splits as $[A]=[T]=26\%$. Of 2000 bases, $n_G=n_C=480$ and $n_A=n_T=520$.

$$
H=2(520)+3(480)=1040+1440=2480.
$$

Check: $520+480=1000$ base pairs, exactly half of 2000 bases, and purines equal pyrimidines at $50\%$ each.

</details>

### 9. Reading the code

One DNA strand is $5'$-ATGCTT-$3'$. Give the complementary strand $5'\to3'$, and state how many distinct codons the genetic code has.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The partner read antiparallel is $3'$-TACGAA-$5'$, which is $5'$-AAGCAT-$3'$ when written in the conventional direction. There are $4^3=64$ codons: 61 specifying amino acids and 3 stop signals.

</details>

### 10. Glucose and feedback

A fasting glucose reading is $180\ \text{mg/dL}$. Convert it to $\text{mmol/L}$, interpret it, and explain how the hormone response changes when glucose is high.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$180\ \text{mg/dL}=1.80\ \text{g/L}$, so $c=1.80/180=0.0100\ \text{mol/L}=10.0\ \text{mmol/L}$. This is well above the diabetes threshold of $7.0\ \text{mmol/L}$. High glucose stimulates pancreatic $\beta$ cells to release insulin, which promotes glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis; as glucose falls, insulin secretion falls — negative feedback. Glucagon secretion is suppressed at the same time.

</details>
