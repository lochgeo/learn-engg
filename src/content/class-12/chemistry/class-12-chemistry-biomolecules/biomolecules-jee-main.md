## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Spend about 15 minutes on Section A, 15 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 10 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one stereochemistry count or one base-composition calculation consume the paper.

The paper tests recognition as much as arithmetic. Before writing an equation, decide which family you are in — sugar, protein, vitamin, nucleic acid or hormone — and name the tool: stereoisomer count, peptide bookkeeping, Michaelis–Menten, Chargaff, or unit conversion.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classify a sugar | Carbonyl position and carbon count | Aldehyde at C1 gives an aldose; internal carbonyl gives a ketose |
| Count sugar stereoisomers | $2^n$ from chiral centres | $n=4$ for an aldohexose, $3$ for a ketohexose |
| $D/L$, $\alpha/\beta$ | Penultimate carbon; anomeric carbon | They are independent labels |
| Reducing behaviour | Free anomeric carbon? | Sucrose ties up both anomeric carbons |
| Peptide bonds and mass | $n-1$ bonds and $n-1$ waters for a linear chain | A cyclic peptide has $n$ of each |
| Enzyme rate | $v=V_{\max}[S]/(K_m+[S])$ | At $[S]=K_m$, $v=V_{\max}/2$ |
| Vitamin solubility | ADEK fat-soluble; B and C water-soluble | Fat-soluble vitamins accumulate and can be toxic |
| DNA composition | $[A]=[T]$, $[G]=[C]$ | Applies to double-stranded DNA |
| Hydrogen bonds | 2 for A–T, 3 for G–C | Count pairs, not individual bases |
| Complementary strand | Pair, then reverse for $5'\to3'$ | The two strands are antiparallel |
| Blood glucose units | Convert $mg/dL$ to $g/L$, then divide by $180\ \text{g/mol}$ | $1\ \text{dL}=0.10\ \text{L}$ |

Use $M(\text{sucrose})=342\ \text{g/mol}$, $M(\text{glucose})=M(\text{fructose})=180\ \text{g/mol}$, $M(\text{ascorbic acid})=176\ \text{g/mol}$ and $N_A=6.022\times10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ where needed.

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If a stereoisomer count is not a power of two, if a "peptide" mass is larger than the sum of its amino acids, if a DNA purine total differs from the pyrimidine total, or if a blood glucose conversion is off by a factor of ten, stop and re-read. Most errors here are bookkeeping errors, not missing knowledge.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. A ketohexose

Which of the following is a ketohexose?

- A. $D$-glucose
- B. $D$-fructose
- C. $D$-galactose
- D. $D$-ribose

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Fructose has six carbons and its carbonyl is at C2, so it is a ketohexose. Glucose and galactose are aldohexoses; ribose is an aldopentose.

</details>

### 2. Chiral centres

The number of chiral centres in open-chain $D$-glucose is:

- A. 3
- B. 4
- C. 5
- D. 6

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** C1 is the aldehyde carbon and C6 is $CH_2OH$, so only C2, C3, C4 and C5 carry four different groups. Four chiral centres give $2^4=16$ stereoisomers.

</details>

### 3. Sucrose linkage

The glycosidic linkage in sucrose is:

- A. $\alpha(1\to4)$
- B. $\beta(1\to4)$
- C. $\alpha$-$C1 \to \beta$-$C2$
- D. $\alpha(1\to6)$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Sucrose is $\alpha$-$D$-glucopyranosyl-(1$\to$2)-$\beta$-$D$-fructofuranoside. The bond joins the anomeric carbon of glucose to the anomeric carbon of fructose; $\alpha(1\to4)$ is maltose and $\beta(1\to4)$ is lactose.

</details>

### 4. Non-reducing sugar

Which sugar does **not** reduce Fehling's solution?

- A. Maltose
- B. Lactose
- C. Sucrose
- D. Glucose

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Sucrose has no free anomeric carbon because both are locked in the glycosidic bond, so its rings cannot open to expose a carbonyl. Maltose and lactose each have one free anomeric carbon, and glucose has one in its own ring.

</details>

### 5. Peptide bonds

A linear decapeptide contains how many peptide bonds?

- A. 8
- B. 9
- C. 10
- D. 11

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** A linear chain of $n$ residues has $n-1$ bonds, so $10-1=9$ peptide bonds and 9 water molecules were eliminated.

</details>

### 6. Michaelis–Menten

For an enzyme obeying Michaelis–Menten kinetics, when $[S]=K_m$ the rate is:

- A. $V_{\max}$
- B. $V_{\max}/2$
- C. $V_{\max}/4$
- D. zero

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Substituting $[S]=K_m$ gives $v=V_{\max}K_m/(K_m+K_m)=V_{\max}/2$. This is the operational definition of $K_m$.

</details>

### 7. Fat-soluble vitamin

Which vitamin is fat-soluble?

- A. Retinol
- B. Ascorbic acid
- C. Thiamine
- D. Niacin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Retinol is vitamin A, one of the fat-soluble group ADEK. Ascorbic acid (C), thiamine ($B_1$) and niacin ($B_3$) are water soluble.

</details>

### 8. Deficiency disease

Scurvy results from a deficiency of:

- A. Thiamine
- B. Niacin
- C. Ascorbic acid
- D. Calciferol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) is needed for collagen hydroxylation; its deficiency causes scurvy. Thiamine deficiency causes beriberi, niacin deficiency pellagra and calciferol deficiency rickets.

</details>

### 9. Hydrogen bonds in DNA

The number of hydrogen bonds between guanine and cytosine in DNA is:

- A. 1
- B. 2
- C. 3
- D. 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** G–C pairs share three hydrogen bonds; A–T pairs share two. This difference is why GC-rich DNA has a higher melting temperature.

</details>

### 10. Glucose control

The hormone that directly lowers blood glucose is:

- A. Glucagon
- B. Insulin
- C. Adrenaline
- D. Cortisol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Insulin, from pancreatic $\beta$ cells, promotes glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis. Glucagon, adrenaline and cortisol all tend to raise blood glucose.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Sugar stereoisomers

How many stereoisomers are possible for an aldohexose?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

An aldohexose has four chiral centres, so $2^4=\mathbf{16}$. Eight belong to the $D$ series and eight to the $L$ series.

</details>

### 12. Hydrolysis mass

$68.4\ \text{g}$ of sucrose is hydrolysed completely. What mass of glucose is formed? ($M(\text{sucrose})=342\ \text{g/mol}$, $M(\text{glucose})=180\ \text{g/mol}$.)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
n=\frac{68.4}{342}=0.200\ \text{mol}.
$$

One mole of sucrose gives one mole of glucose, so $m=0.200\times180=\mathbf{36.0\ \text{g}}$. The same $0.200\ \text{mol}$ of fructose is formed, and $0.200\ \text{mol}=3.6\ \text{g}$ of water is consumed: $68.4+3.6=36.0+36.0$, so mass balances.

</details>

### 13. Peptide molar mass

A linear pentapeptide is made from amino acids of average molar mass $110\ \text{g/mol}$. Find its molar mass.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A linear pentapeptide loses $5-1=4$ water molecules:

$$
M=5(110)-4(18)=550-72=\mathbf{478\ \text{g/mol}}.
$$

</details>

### 14. Enzyme rate

An enzyme has $V_{\max}=80\ \mu\text{mol/min}$ and $K_m=2.0\ \text{mmol/L}$. Find the initial rate at $[S]=6.0\ \text{mmol/L}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
v=\frac{80(6.0)}{2.0+6.0}=\frac{480}{8.0}=\mathbf{60\ \mu\text{mol/min}}.
$$

This is $75\%$ of $V_{\max}$, as expected when $[S]$ is three times $K_m$.

</details>

### 15. Hydrogen bonds in a DNA sample

A double-stranded DNA contains $30\%$ adenine and 1000 bases in total. Find the total number of hydrogen bonds.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$[T]=[A]=30\%$, so G and C share the remaining $40\%$ equally: $[G]=[C]=20\%$. Thus $n_A=n_T=300$ and $n_G=n_C=200$.

$$
H=2(300)+3(200)=600+600=\mathbf{1200}.
$$

Check: $300+200=500$ base pairs from 1000 bases, and purines $300+200=500$ equal pyrimidines $300+200=500$.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

For each, choose: **A** — both true and R explains A; **B** — both true but R does not explain A; **C** — A true but R false; **D** — A false but R true.

### 16. Sucrose

**Assertion (A):** Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar.
**Reason (R):** Both anomeric carbons of sucrose are tied up in its glycosidic bond.

- A. Both true, and R explains A
- B. Both true, but R does not explain A
- C. A true, R false
- D. A false, R true

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true and R explains A: with no free anomeric carbon, neither ring can open to give the carbonyl that would reduce Fehling's or Tollens' reagent.

</details>

### 17. Denaturation

**Assertion (A):** Denaturation breaks the peptide bonds of a protein.
**Reason (R):** Denaturation disrupts hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions that stabilise the folded shape.

- A. A
- B. B
- C. C
- D. D

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** A is false: denaturation destroys secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure but leaves the primary sequence and its peptide bonds intact. R is true, since those interactions are exactly what hold the fold together.

</details>

### 18. Chargaff across species

**Assertion (A):** Double-stranded DNA from different species can have different base compositions.
**Reason (R):** Chargaff's rule requires $[A]=[T]$ and $[G]=[C]$ in double-stranded DNA.

- A. A
- B. B
- C. C
- D. D

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Both statements are true, but R does not explain A. Chargaff's rule constrains the *pairs* to be equal; the $G+C$ fraction can still vary widely between species (for example, $40\%$ versus $60\%$), giving different compositions that all satisfy the rule.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching / multi-statement questions

### 19. Match the vitamin

Match each vitamin in Column I with its characteristic deficiency in Column II.

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| (i) Retinol | (p) Rickets |
| (ii) Calciferol | (q) Pernicious anaemia |
| (iii) Ascorbic acid | (r) Night blindness |
| (iv) Cobalamin | (s) Scurvy |

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(i)–(r), (ii)–(p), (iii)–(s), (iv)–(q).

Retinol (vitamin A) is part of the visual pigment, so deficiency gives night blindness. Calciferol (vitamin D) drives calcium absorption, so deficiency gives rickets in children. Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) is required for collagen synthesis, so deficiency gives scurvy. Cobalamin (vitamin $B_{12}$) needs intrinsic factor for absorption, and its deficiency causes pernicious anaemia.

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement

Which statements about nucleic acids are correct?

(i) RNA contains ribose and uracil.
(ii) An A–T base pair is held by three hydrogen bonds.
(iii) The genetic code contains 64 codons.
(iv) The two strands of DNA run antiparallel.

- A. (i), (ii) and (iii)
- B. (i), (iii) and (iv)
- C. (ii) and (iv) only
- D. all four

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** (i) is correct — RNA uses ribose and uracil in place of deoxyribose and thymine. (ii) is **incorrect** — A–T has two hydrogen bonds; G–C has three. (iii) is correct — $4^3=64$ codons, of which 61 code for amino acids. (iv) is correct — one strand runs $5'\to3'$ and the other $3'\to5'$. Hence (i), (iii) and (iv) are the true statements.

</details>
