## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice paper. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 15 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 10 on Section D. If a question takes longer than 90 seconds, mark it and move on. The paper tests selection as much as memory: before writing a reagent, decide whether the substrate is $sp^3$ or $sp^2$, whether the conditions favour substitution or elimination, and whether the question is asking for a product, a rate law or a trend.

Answers are worked in full. Mark a question wrong if you chose the right option for the wrong reason; that is still a gap to close.

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### A1. Name the structure

The IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_3CH(Br)CH_2CH(CH_3)CH_3}$ is:

- A. 2-bromo-4-methylpentane
- B. 4-bromo-2-methylpentane
- C. 2-bromo-2-methylpentane
- D. 1-bromo-3-methylpentane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The longest chain has five carbons. Numbering from the left gives bromine at 2 and methyl at 4; numbering from the right gives methyl at 2 and bromine at 4. Both give the set $\{2,4\}$, so bromo (cited first alphabetically) takes the lower locant.

</details>

### A2. Longest bond

Which carbon–halogen bond is the longest?

- A. C–F
- B. C–Cl
- C. C–Br
- D. C–I

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Bond length increases with halogen size: C–F $139\ \text{pm}$, C–Cl $178\ \text{pm}$, C–Br $193\ \text{pm}$, C–I $214\ \text{pm}$.

</details>

### A3. Highest boiling point

Which methyl halide has the highest boiling point?

- A. $\mathrm{CH_3F}$
- B. $\mathrm{CH_3Cl}$
- C. $\mathrm{CH_3Br}$
- D. $\mathrm{CH_3I}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** $\mathrm{CH_3I}$ boils at $42\ ^\circ\text{C}$, above $\mathrm{CH_3Br}$ ($3.6\ ^\circ\text{C}$), $\mathrm{CH_3Cl}$ ($-24\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and $\mathrm{CH_3F}$ ($-78\ ^\circ\text{C}$). Iodine is the most polarisable halogen, so dispersion forces dominate.

</details>

### A4. SN2 rate

For an SN2 reaction, doubling the concentration of the alkyl halide while keeping the nucleophile concentration constant will:

- A. double the rate
- B. quadruple the rate
- C. halve the rate
- D. leave the rate unchanged

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The SN2 rate law is $k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{Nu^-}]$, which is first order in each reactant. Doubling only $[\mathrm{RX}]$ doubles the rate; doubling both would quadruple it.

</details>

### A5. Fastest SN1 substrate

Which substrate reacts fastest with water by an SN1 mechanism?

- A. bromomethane
- B. 1-bromobutane
- C. 2-bromobutane
- D. 2-bromo-2-methylpropane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** SN1 proceeds through a carbocation, and the tertiary carbocation from 2-bromo-2-methylpropane is the most stable. Primary and methyl halides form unstable carbocations and react very slowly by SN1.

</details>

### A6. Hydrolysis reagent

Which reagent converts 1-bromopropane mainly to propan-1-ol?

- A. alcoholic KOH
- B. aqueous KOH
- C. KCN in ethanol
- D. NaI in acetone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Aqueous KOH supplies hydroxide as a nucleophile, so the primary halide undergoes SN2 hydrolysis to propan-1-ol. Alcoholic KOH would favour elimination to propene; KCN gives butanenitrile; NaI gives 1-iodopropane.

</details>

### A7. Elimination product

The major product of 2-bromobutane with alcoholic KOH at reflux is:

- A. but-1-ene
- B. but-2-ene
- C. butan-2-ol
- D. butane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Alcoholic KOH promotes E2 elimination, and Zaitsev's rule selects the more substituted alkene. But-2-ene is more stable than but-1-ene.

</details>

### A8. Haloarene reactivity

Chlorobenzene is much less reactive than chloromethane towards nucleophilic substitution because:

- A. the C–Cl bond in chlorobenzene has partial double-bond character
- B. chlorine is more electronegative in chlorobenzene
- C. the benzene ring is electron-rich and repels nucleophiles
- D. chlorobenzene is non-polar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The chlorine lone pair is delocalised into the aromatic ring, so the C–Cl bond is shorter and stronger, and the $sp^2$ carbon does not support backside attack. The ring's electron richness is not the reason; if anything, it makes the ring attractive to electrophiles.

</details>

### A9. Chloroform oxidation

Chloroform exposed to air and light forms:

- A. CO
- B. $\mathrm{CO_2}$
- C. $\mathrm{COCl_2}$
- D. $\mathrm{CCl_4}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Chloroform is oxidised to phosgene, $\mathrm{COCl_2}$, which is highly toxic. This is why chloroform is stored in dark, filled bottles with ethanol.

</details>

### A10. Directing but deactivating

Which substituent is ortho/para-directing but deactivating in electrophilic aromatic substitution?

- A. $-\mathrm{OH}$
- B. $-\mathrm{Cl}$
- C. $-\mathrm{NO_2}$
- D. $-\mathrm{CH_3}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Chlorine donates a lone pair by resonance, which directs ortho/para, but withdraws inductively, which deactivates the ring. $-\mathrm{OH}$ and $-\mathrm{CH_3}$ are activating; $-\mathrm{NO_2}$ is meta-directing and strongly deactivating.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### B11. Alkene mass

$13.7\ \text{g}$ of 2-bromobutane is completely dehydrohalogenated. What is the theoretical mass of but-2-ene? Use $M(\mathrm{C_4H_9Br}) = 136.9\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$ and $M(\mathrm{C_4H_8}) = 56.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n(\mathrm{C_4H_9Br}) = 13.7/136.9 = 0.100\ \text{mol}$. One mole of halide gives one mole of alkene, so the theoretical mass is $0.100 \times 56.0 = 5.60\ \text{g}$.

</details>

### B12. Chlorine percentage

What is the mass percentage of chlorine in DDT, $\mathrm{C_{14}H_9Cl_5}$? Use $Cl=35.5$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$M = 14(12.0) + 9(1.0) + 5(35.5) = 354.5\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. The chlorine mass is $177.5\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, so

$$
\%\mathrm{Cl} = \frac{177.5}{354.5}\times100 = 50.1\%.
$$

</details>

### B13. SN2 initial rate

An SN2 reaction has $k = 2.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{L mol}^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-1}$, $[\mathrm{RX}] = 0.10\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$ and $[\mathrm{Nu^-}] = 0.20\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$. Calculate the initial rate.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
\text{rate} = k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{Nu^-}] = (2.0\times10^{-3})(0.10)(0.20) = 4.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{mol L}^{-1}\ \text{s}^{-1}.
$$

</details>

### B14. Molar mass from vapour density

A haloalkane vapour has density $4.47\ \text{g L}^{-1}$ at $373\ \text{K}$ and $1.00\ \text{atm}$. Calculate its molar mass to the nearest integer. Use $R = 0.0821\ \text{L atm mol}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$
M = \frac{\rho RT}{P} = \frac{(4.47)(0.0821)(373)}{1.00} = 136.9\ \text{g mol}^{-1}.
$$

To the nearest integer, $M = 137\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, consistent with $\mathrm{C_4H_9Br}$.

</details>

### B15. Thionyl chloride mass

What mass of $\mathrm{SOCl_2}$ is required to convert $15.0\ \text{g}$ of propan-1-ol completely to 1-chloropropane? Use $M(\mathrm{C_3H_8O}) = 60.1$ and $M(\mathrm{SOCl_2}) = 119.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$n(\mathrm{C_3H_8O}) = 15.0/60.1 = 0.2496\ \text{mol}$. The reaction is 1:1, so

$$
m(\mathrm{SOCl_2}) = 0.2496 \times 119.0 = 29.7\ \text{g}.
$$

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

For each question choose:

- **(a)** Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- **(b)** Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- **(c)** A is true, but R is false.
- **(d)** A is false, but R is true.

### C16. Chlorobenzene versus chloromethane

**Assertion (A):** Chlorobenzene undergoes SN2 substitution faster than chloromethane.

**Reason (R):** The C–Cl bond in chlorobenzene has partial double-bond character because of resonance with the ring.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**d.** The assertion is false: chlorobenzene is much slower, not faster, because backside attack is blocked and the bond is stronger. The reason is true: chlorine donates a lone pair into the ring, giving the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character.

</details>

### C17. Inversion in SN2

**Assertion (A):** The SN2 reaction of $(R)$-2-bromobutane with hydroxide gives $(S)$-butan-2-ol.

**Reason (R):** SN2 proceeds with inversion of configuration at the carbon under attack.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**a.** Both statements are true, and the reason explains the assertion. The nucleophile attacks from the side opposite the leaving group, so the configuration at the stereocentre inverts. The CIP priorities of bromine and oxygen are both rank 1, so the descriptor changes from $R$ to $S$.

</details>

### C18. E2 and carbocations

**Assertion (A):** Tertiary alkyl halides undergo E2 elimination faster than primary alkyl halides with a strong base.

**Reason (R):** Tertiary carbocations are more stable, so the E2 transition state is lower in energy.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**c.** The assertion is generally true: tertiary substrates eliminate readily with strong bases. The reason is false because E2 is a concerted reaction and does not form a carbocation. The greater rate comes from the more substituted, more stable alkene being formed and from the availability of β-hydrogens, not from a carbocation intermediate.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching or multi-statement questions

### D19. Match the reaction

Match each reaction in Column I with its main outcome in Column II.

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| A. 2-Bromobutane + alcoholic KOH, heat | P. SN1; gives 2-methylpropan-2-ol |
| B. 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane + aqueous KOH | Q. E2; gives but-2-ene |
| C. Chlorobenzene + NaOH, $623\ \text{K}$, $300\ \text{atm}$ | R. SNAr; gives 2,4-dinitrophenol |
| D. 2,4-Dinitrochlorobenzene + aqueous NaOH, $370\ \text{K}$ | S. Dow process; gives phenol |

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A–Q, B–P, C–S, D–R.**

- A is a secondary halide with a strong base and heat: E2, with Zaitsev's rule giving but-2-ene.
- B is tertiary with aqueous KOH: SN1 gives 2-methylpropan-2-ol (with some elimination).
- C is the unactivated Dow process, requiring extreme conditions to give phenol.
- D is an activated aryl halide: SNAr through a Meisenheimer complex gives 2,4-dinitrophenol.

</details>

### D20. Multi-statement

Consider the reaction of 2-bromo-2-methylbutane with sodium ethoxide in ethanol at reflux. Which statements are correct?

I. The major product is 2-methylbut-2-ene.

II. The reaction proceeds mainly by E2.

III. The rate is independent of the ethoxide concentration.

IV. With potassium tert-butoxide, the major product would be 2-methylbut-1-ene.

- A. I and II only
- B. I, II and III only
- C. I, II and IV only
- D. I, II, III and IV

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** I is true: ethoxide is a small base, so Zaitsev's rule gives the more substituted 2-methylbut-2-ene. II is true: a strong base and heat favour E2. III is false: the E2 rate law is $k[\mathrm{RX}][\mathrm{Base}]$, so the rate depends on ethoxide concentration. IV is true: the bulky tert-butoxide gives the Hofmann product, 2-methylbut-1-ene.

</details>
