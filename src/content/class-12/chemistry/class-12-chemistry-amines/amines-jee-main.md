## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** practice set for after you complete the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 15 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 10 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; do not let one structure or one pH calculation consume the paper.

The paper tests recognition as much as calculation. Before writing an equation, name the class of amine, decide whether the nitrogen is acting as a base or a nucleophile, and check the carbon count.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classify an amine | Count carbon groups on nitrogen | Branches in the carbon skeleton do not change the class |
| Count isomers | $\text{DoU}=(2C+2+N-H-X)/2$ | Include nitrogen in the formula |
| Compare basicity | $K_b$ or $pK_b$; smaller $pK_b$ is stronger | In water, solvation can reverse the gas-phase order |
| Aromatic amine basicity | Lone pair delocalised into the ring | Electron-withdrawing substituents weaken the base |
| pH of a weak amine | $K_b=x^2/(C-x)$; then $pH=14-pOH$ | Check $x/C$ before dropping $x$ from the denominator |
| One carbon more | Alkyl halide → nitrile → reduction | Nitrile formation adds one carbon |
| One carbon fewer | Amide → Hofmann degradation | The carbonyl carbon is lost as carbonate |
| Clean primary amine | Gabriel synthesis | Only primary amines; aryl halides fail |
| Secondary amine | Reductive amination | Carbonyl plus primary amine, then reduction |
| Distinguish $1^\circ/2^\circ/3^\circ$ | Hinsberg or carbylamine test | Carbylamine needs an N–H pair |
| Diazotisation | $\mathrm{NaNO_2+HCl}$ at $273$–$278\ \text{K}$ | Keep cold and acidic; use fresh |
| Replace $\mathrm{N_2^+}$ | Sandmeyer, KI, $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ or water | Fluoride needs $\mathrm{HBF_4}$ |
| Overall yield | Multiply step yields | The result is lower than every individual yield |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If your amine has the wrong number of carbons, if an aliphatic primary amine is supposed to give a stable diazonium salt, or if a pH for a weak amine falls outside $7$–$14$, stop and re-read the question. A chemically impossible product is usually a sign that the wrong route was chosen, not that the arithmetic slipped.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Isomers of $\mathrm{C_3H_9N}$

The number of structural isomers of $\mathrm{C_3H_9N}$ is:

- A. 2
- B. 3
- C. 4
- D. 5

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The degree of unsaturation is

$$
\frac{2(3)+2+1-9}{2}=0,
$$

so the isomers are saturated acyclic amines: propan-1-amine, propan-2-amine and N-methylethanamine. That is three.

</details>

### 2. Strongest base in water

Which is the strongest base in aqueous solution?

- A. $\mathrm{NH_3}$
- B. $\mathrm{CH_3NH_2}$
- C. $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH}$
- D. $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3N}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The aqueous order is $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH>CH_3NH_2>(CH_3)_3N>NH_3}$. Dimethylamine has two electron-donating methyl groups and still one N–H bond, so its conjugate acid is both inductively stabilised and solvated. Trimethylamine's conjugate acid has no N–H bond and is poorly solvated.

</details>

### 3. Why aniline is a weak base

Aniline is a much weaker base than ethylamine mainly because:

- A. the aromatic ring withdraws electron density inductively
- B. the nitrogen lone pair is delocalised into the aromatic ring
- C. the nitrogen is $sp^2$ hybridised and has no lone pair
- D. aniline is insoluble in water

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The lone pair participates in resonance with the ring, so it is less available for protonation. Protonating aniline would remove that resonance stabilisation. The inductive effect of the ring is minor by comparison, and aniline does have a lone pair.

</details>

### 4. Hofmann degradation of propanamide

Propanamide treated with bromine and concentrated sodium hydroxide gives:

- A. propan-1-amine
- B. ethanamine
- C. methanamine
- D. propan-2-amine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Hofmann bromamide degradation removes the carbonyl carbon, so a three-carbon amide gives a two-carbon amine. Propanamide, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CONH_2}$, gives ethanamine, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2NH_2}$.

</details>

### 5. The Gabriel synthesis

The Gabriel synthesis is used to prepare:

- A. primary amines
- B. secondary amines
- C. tertiary amines
- D. quaternary ammonium salts

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Phthalimide has only one replaceable hydrogen on nitrogen, so only one alkyl group can be introduced. The product after hydrolysis is a primary amine.

</details>

### 6. Diazonium salt with potassium iodide

Benzenediazonium chloride treated with potassium iodide gives:

- A. chlorobenzene
- B. iodobenzene
- C. phenol
- D. benzene

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Iodide replaces the diazonium group and nitrogen is released:

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^-+KI \rightarrow C_6H_5I+N_2+KCl}.
$$

Chlorobenzene would require $\mathrm{CuCl/HCl}$, phenol warm water, and benzene $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$.

</details>

### 7. Bromine water and aniline

Aniline treated with bromine water gives mainly:

- A. p-bromoaniline
- B. m-bromoaniline
- C. 2,4,6-tribromoaniline
- D. no reaction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The amino group is strongly activating and ortho/para directing, so bromine enters all three available positions and a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline forms. A single bromine requires protection of the amine as acetanilide.

</details>

### 8. Why acetylate aniline before nitration?

Aniline is converted into acetanilide before nitration mainly to:

- A. increase the yield of m-nitroaniline
- B. prevent protonation and oxidation of the amino group and favour the para product
- C. make the nitrogen more basic
- D. convert the amine into a diazonium salt

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** In strong acid aniline is protonated to the meta-directing anilinium ion, and the free amine is easily oxidised. Acetylation protects the amine, reduces its activating power and leaves an ortho/para-directing acetamido group, so nitration gives mainly p-nitroacetanilide, which is hydrolysed to p-nitroaniline.

</details>

### 9. The carbylamine test

Which reagent gives a foul-smelling product with a primary amine?

- A. bromine water
- B. chloroform and alcoholic potassium hydroxide
- C. sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid
- D. benzenesulphonyl chloride

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Chloroform with alcoholic potassium hydroxide converts a primary amine into an isocyanide (carbylamine), which has a powerful unpleasant smell. Secondary and tertiary amines do not give the test.

</details>

### 10. Hypophosphorous acid and a diazonium salt

Benzenediazonium chloride treated with $\mathrm{H_3PO_2}$ gives:

- A. phenol
- B. benzene
- C. chlorobenzene
- D. phenylhydrazine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Hypophosphorous acid replaces the diazonium group by hydrogen, so the amino group is removed completely and benzene is obtained. This is the standard deamination reaction.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Primary amine isomers

How many primary amine isomers have the molecular formula $\mathrm{C_4H_{11}N}$?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**4.** The primary amines are butan-1-amine, butan-2-amine, 2-methylpropan-1-amine and 2-methylpropan-2-amine. The other four isomers of $\mathrm{C_4H_{11}N}$ are secondary or tertiary.

</details>

### 12. pH of methylamine

Find the pH of $0.10\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$ methylamine at $25^\circ\text{C}$, given $K_b=4.4\times10^{-4}$. Give your answer to two decimal places.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**11.81.** Let $x=[\mathrm{OH^-}]$. Then

$$
\frac{x^2}{0.10-x}=4.4\times10^{-4},
$$

which gives $x=6.42\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$. Hence $pOH=2.19$ and $pH=14.00-2.19=11.81$.

</details>

### 13. Hofmann degradation mass

What mass of propan-1-amine, $M=59.1\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, is obtained from $1.00\ \text{g}$ of butanamide, $M=87.1\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, at $100\%$ yield?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**0.68 g.** Moles of amide:

$$
n=\frac{1.00}{87.1}=1.15\times10^{-2}\ \text{mol}.
$$

One mole of amide gives one mole of amine, so

$$
m=(1.15\times10^{-2})(59.1)=0.678\ \text{g}\approx0.68\ \text{g}.
$$

The carbon count falls from four to three, as Hofmann degradation requires.

</details>

### 14. Sodium nitrite for diazotisation

What mass of sodium nitrite, $M=69.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, is needed to diazotise $9.30\ \text{g}$ of aniline, $M=93.1\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, completely?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**6.89 g.** Moles of aniline:

$$
n=\frac{9.30}{93.1}=9.99\times10^{-2}\ \text{mol}.
$$

The reaction consumes one mole of nitrite per mole of amine, so

$$
m=(9.99\times10^{-2})(69.0)=6.89\ \text{g}.
$$

In practice a slight excess is used to ensure complete diazotisation.

</details>

### 15. Sandmeyer percentage yield

Diazotisation of $4.65\ \text{g}$ of aniline followed by Sandmeyer bromination gives $5.50\ \text{g}$ of bromobenzene. Find the percentage yield. Use $M(\text{aniline})=93.1$ and $M(\text{bromobenzene})=157.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**70.2%.** Moles of aniline:

$$
n=\frac{4.65}{93.1}=4.99\times10^{-2}\ \text{mol}.
$$

The theoretical mass of bromobenzene is

$$
m_{\text{theoretical}}=(4.99\times10^{-2})(157.0)=7.84\ \text{g}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\text{yield}=\frac{5.50}{7.84}\times100\%=70.2\%.
$$

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

For each question, choose:

- A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
- B. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- C. A is true but R is false.
- D. A is false but R is true.

### 16. Trimethylamine in water

**Assertion (A):** Trimethylamine is a stronger base than dimethylamine in aqueous solution.

**Reason (R):** Three methyl groups donate more electron density to nitrogen than two methyl groups do.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The assertion is false: in water dimethylamine is the stronger base. The reason is true—three methyl groups do donate more electron density—but in water the conjugate acid of trimethylamine has no N–H bond and is poorly solvated, which outweighs the extra inductive donation. The gas-phase order is different.

</details>

### 17. Aniline and p-nitroaniline

**Assertion (A):** Aniline is a stronger base than p-nitroaniline.

**Reason (R):** The nitro group withdraws electron density and destabilises the conjugate acid.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true, and the reason explains the assertion. The nitro group withdraws electron density by both induction and resonance, making the nitrogen lone pair less available and the protonated form less stable. p-Nitroaniline is therefore an extremely weak base.

</details>

### 18. Keeping diazonium salts cold

**Assertion (A):** Benzenediazonium chloride is kept at $273$–$278\ \text{K}$ during preparation.

**Reason (R):** Above about $5^\circ\text{C}$ the diazonium ion decomposes to phenol and nitrogen.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both are true and the reason is the correct explanation. The aromatic diazonium ion is thermally unstable; warming it hydrolyses the group to $-\mathrm{OH}$ with loss of $\mathrm{N_2}$. Cold conditions preserve it for the intended substitution or coupling step.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching and multi-statement questions

### 19. Matching reactions and products

Match each reaction with its main organic product.

| Reaction | Product |
| --- | --- |
| P. Aniline + $\mathrm{CHCl_3}$ + alcoholic KOH | 1. Phenyl isocyanide |
| Q. Aniline + $\mathrm{NaNO_2/HCl}$ at $0$–$5^\circ\text{C}$ | 2. Benzenediazonium chloride |
| R. Aniline + bromine water | 3. 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline |
| S. Aniline + acetyl chloride | 4. Acetanilide |

- A. P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4
- B. P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4
- C. P-1, Q-3, R-2, S-4
- D. P-1, Q-2, R-4, S-3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** P-1 is the carbylamine reaction; Q-2 is diazotisation; R-3 is the tribromo substitution of the strongly activated ring; S-4 is acylation to the amide.

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement question

Which of the following statements are correct?

(i) The Gabriel synthesis gives only primary amines.

(ii) Hofmann bromamide degradation keeps the number of carbon atoms unchanged.

(iii) Aromatic primary amines form stable diazonium salts at $0$–$5^\circ\text{C}$.

(iv) Tertiary amines give the carbylamine test.

- A. (i) and (iii) only
- B. (i), (ii) and (iii)
- C. (ii) and (iv) only
- D. (i), (iii) and (iv)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** (i) is correct: the imide nitrogen accepts only one alkyl group. (iii) is correct: aromatic diazonium salts are stable when cold. (ii) is false because Hofmann degradation removes the carbonyl carbon, and (iv) is false because the carbylamine test requires a primary amine with two N–H hydrogens.

</details>
