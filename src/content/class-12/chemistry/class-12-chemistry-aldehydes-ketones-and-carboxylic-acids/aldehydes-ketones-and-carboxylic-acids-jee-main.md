## JEE Main sprint: how to use this set

This is a **50-minute, no-notes** paper for after you have completed the chapter lessons. Spend roughly 15 minutes on Section A, 18 on Section B, 10 on Section C and 7 on Section D. Mark a question for review after 90 seconds; a numerical-value question that is not converging should not consume the paper.

The paper tests recognition as much as calculation. Before writing an equation, name the functional group and the reaction type: nucleophilic addition, nucleophilic acyl substitution, aldol, Cannizzaro, haloform, or acid–base. Most wrong answers in this chapter come from choosing the wrong reaction class, not from arithmetic.

## Conditions sheet

| Situation | Start with | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A carbonyl is attacked by a nucleophile | Attack at $\mathrm{C{=}O}$, tetrahedral alkoxide, then protonate | Aldehydes react faster than ketones; acid work-up is needed after a Grignard reaction |
| Aldehyde versus ketone | Tollens' or Fehling's | Fehling's is negative for aromatic aldehydes |
| Ethanal or a methyl ketone | Iodoform test, $\mathrm{I_2/NaOH}$ | Ethanal and methyl carbinols also give a positive test |
| No α-hydrogen and concentrated base | Cannizzaro reaction | The aldehyde must lack α-hydrogens |
| α-Hydrogen present | Aldol condensation or enolate chemistry | Dehydration follows heating |
| Acid strength is compared | Stability of the carboxylate ion | Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity; distance matters |
| pH of a weak acid | $K_a=x^2/(c-x)$ | Check that $x$ is small compared with $c$ |
| Esterification | $1:1$ mole ratio, reversible | Remove water or distil the ester to improve yield |
| Acid derivatives are compared | Leaving-group ability and resonance | Acyl chloride > anhydride > ester > amide |
| A formula and structures are given | $\text{DBE}=(2c+2+n-h-x)/2$ | A carbonyl contributes one degree of unsaturation |

<learning-block type="mistake" title="The fastest sanity checks">

If a pH comes out below $0$ or above $14$ for a dilute weak acid, stop. If an aldehyde is said to reduce Fehling's when it is aromatic, stop. If an amide is called basic, stop. These are the three most common slips in this chapter.

</learning-block>

## Section A — 10 rapid MCQs

### 1. Nucleophilic addition reactivity

Which carbonyl compound is most reactive towards nucleophilic addition?

- A. $\mathrm{HCHO}$
- B. $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$
- C. $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$
- D. $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Methanal has two hydrogens on the carbonyl carbon and no electron-donating alkyl group. Its carbonyl carbon is the most electrophilic and the least hindered. Each alkyl group added reduces the positive charge and increases steric hindrance, so reactivity falls in the order $\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{CH_3COCH_3} > \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$.

</details>

### 2. Tollens' versus Fehling's

Which compound gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent but **no** red precipitate with Fehling's solution?

- A. ethanal
- B. benzaldehyde
- C. propanone
- D. methanoic acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Benzaldehyde is an aldehyde, so it reduces Tollens' reagent to silver. It is aromatic, however, and is not oxidised easily enough by Fehling's solution, so no red $\mathrm{Cu_2O}$ forms. Ethanal and methanoic acid reduce both reagents; propanone reduces neither.

</details>

### 3. Iodoform test

Which compound does **not** give a yellow precipitate with iodine and sodium hydroxide?

- A. ethanol
- B. ethanal
- C. propanone
- D. propan-1-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** The iodoform test is positive for ethanal, methyl ketones and secondary alcohols that can be oxidised to a methyl ketone. Ethanol is oxidised to ethanal under the reaction conditions and gives a positive test. Propan-1-ol is a primary alcohol that would oxidise to propanal, not to a methyl ketone, so it does not give iodoform.

</details>

### 4. Cyanohydrin formation

The product of propanone with hydrogen cyanide is:

- A. 2-hydroxypropanenitrile
- B. 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile
- C. propanenitrile
- D. 2-methylpropan-2-ol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Cyanide attacks the carbonyl carbon of propanone, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CO}$, and protonation of the alkoxide gives $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C(OH)CN}$, which is 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile. Option A would come from ethanal, and option D is the alcohol formed by Grignard addition or reduction, not by HCN addition.

</details>

### 5. Cannizzaro reaction

Benzaldehyde treated with concentrated sodium hydroxide gives:

- A. benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate
- B. benzoic acid and benzene
- C. benzyl alcohol only
- D. 3-hydroxybutanal

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Benzaldehyde has no α-hydrogen, so it cannot undergo aldol condensation. In concentrated base it disproportionates: one molecule is reduced to benzyl alcohol and another is oxidised to sodium benzoate.

$$
\mathrm{2C_6H_5CHO + NaOH \rightarrow C_6H_5CH_2OH + C_6H_5COONa}.
$$

</details>

### 6. α-Hydrogen

Which compound has **no** α-hydrogen?

- A. benzaldehyde
- B. ethanal
- C. propanal
- D. butan-2-one

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The α-carbon of benzaldehyde is a ring carbon that already carries no hydrogen available for enolisation, so benzaldehyde cannot form an enolate. Ethanal, propanal and butan-2-one all have hydrogens on the carbon next to the carbonyl.

</details>

### 7. Acidity order

Which order is correct for increasing acid strength?

- A. $\mathrm{CH_3COOH < ClCH_2COOH < Cl_2CHCOOH < Cl_3CCOOH}$
- B. $\mathrm{Cl_3CCOOH < Cl_2CHCOOH < ClCH_2COOH < CH_3COOH}$
- C. $\mathrm{CH_3COOH < Cl_3CCOOH < ClCH_2COOH < Cl_2CHCOOH}$
- D. $\mathrm{ClCH_2COOH < CH_3COOH < Cl_2CHCOOH < Cl_3CCOOH}$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Each chlorine withdraws electron density inductively and stabilises the carboxylate ion. More chlorines means greater stabilisation, so acidity increases from ethanoic acid to trichloroethanoic acid. The approximate $\mathrm{p}K_a$ values are $4.76$, $2.87$, $1.29$ and $0.65$.

</details>

### 8. Acid-derivative reactivity

Which acid derivative is most reactive towards nucleophilic substitution?

- A. ethanoyl chloride
- B. ethanoic anhydride
- C. ethyl ethanoate
- D. ethanamide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Chloride is the weakest base and the best leaving group of the four. The ester oxygen and especially the amide nitrogen donate electron density into the carbonyl, reducing its electrophilicity. The order is acyl chloride > anhydride > ester > amide.

</details>

### 9. Reaction with bicarbonate

Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydrogen carbonate to give:

- A. sodium ethanoate, carbon dioxide and water
- B. sodium ethanoate and hydrogen only
- C. ethanol and carbon dioxide
- D. no reaction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Carboxylic acids are strong enough to protonate hydrogen carbonate, so carbon dioxide is released:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH + NaHCO_3 \rightarrow CH_3COONa + CO_2 + H_2O}.
$$

This reaction distinguishes a carboxylic acid from a phenol, which does not liberate $\mathrm{CO_2}$ from $\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$.

</details>

### 10. Clemmensen reduction

Propanone treated with zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid gives:

- A. propan-1-ol
- B. propan-2-ol
- C. propane
- D. propanoic acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The Clemmensen reduction removes the carbonyl oxygen completely and replaces it with two hydrogens. Propanone, $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$, becomes propane, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_3}$. Reduction to propan-2-ol would require a hydride reagent such as $\mathrm{NaBH_4}$.

</details>

## Section B — 5 numerical-value questions

### 11. Isomers of a ketone

A saturated acyclic ketone has molar mass $86\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. How many structural isomers does it have?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A saturated acyclic ketone has formula $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n}O}$, so

$$
14n+16=86 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 14n=70 \quad\Rightarrow\quad n=5.
$$

The formula is $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O}$. The ketone isomers are pentan-2-one, pentan-3-one and 3-methylbutan-2-one. There are **3** structural isomers.

</details>

### 12. Iodoform mass

Calculate the mass of iodoform produced when $0.10\ \text{mol}$ of propanone reacts completely with excess iodine and sodium hydroxide. Take $M(\mathrm{CHI_3})=394\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The reaction is

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \rightarrow CHI_3 + CH_3COONa + 3NaI + 3H_2O}.
$$

The stoichiometry is $1:1$, so $0.10\ \text{mol}$ of propanone gives $0.10\ \text{mol}$ of iodoform:

$$
m=0.10\times394=39.4\ \text{g}.
$$

The answer is **39.4 g**. The large mass is expected because iodoform contains three heavy iodine atoms.

</details>

### 13. Ester yield

Ethanoic acid ($0.20\ \text{mol}$) is heated with excess ethanol and a little sulphuric acid. Ethyl ethanoate is obtained in $80\%$ yield. Calculate its mass. Take $M=88\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The theoretical amount of ester is $0.20\ \text{mol}$. At $80\%$ yield,

$$
n_{\text{actual}}=0.80\times0.20=0.16\ \text{mol}.
$$

Thus

$$
m=0.16\times88=14.08\ \text{g}\approx14.1\ \text{g}.
$$

The answer is **14.1 g**.

</details>

### 14. pH of a weak acid

Calculate the pH of $0.010\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$ ethanoic acid. Take $K_a=1.8\times10^{-5}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Let $x=[\mathrm{H^+}]$. Then

$$
K_a\approx\frac{x^2}{0.010},
$$

so

$$
x^2=(1.8\times10^{-5})(0.010)=1.8\times10^{-7},
$$

$$
x=4.24\times10^{-4}\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}(4.24\times10^{-4})=3.37.
$$

The answer is **3.37**. The ionisation is $4.24\%$, so the approximation is acceptable.

</details>

### 15. Combustion volume

Butanal ($0.10\ \text{mol}$) is burned completely in oxygen. What volume of carbon dioxide is produced at STP? Take the molar volume as $22.4\ \text{L mol}^{-1}$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The balanced equation is

$$
\mathrm{C_4H_8O + 5.5O_2 \rightarrow 4CO_2 + 4H_2O}.
$$

One mole of butanal gives four moles of carbon dioxide, so $0.10\ \text{mol}$ gives $0.40\ \text{mol}$ of $\mathrm{CO_2}$:

$$
V=0.40\times22.4=8.96\ \text{L}.
$$

The answer is **8.96 L**.

</details>

## Section C — 3 assertion–reason questions

Use the standard codes:

- **A.** Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- **B.** Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
- **C.** A is true, but R is false.
- **D.** A is false, but R is true.

### 16. Aldehyde versus ketone reactivity

**Assertion (A):** Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones towards nucleophilic addition.

**Reason (R):** Alkyl groups donate electron density and also cause steric hindrance at the carbonyl carbon.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true, and the reason explains the assertion. In a ketone, two alkyl groups reduce the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon by the inductive effect and obstruct the approach of the nucleophile. An aldehyde has at most one alkyl group, so its carbonyl carbon is more electrophilic and more accessible.

</details>

### 17. Acid strength

**Assertion (A):** Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than alcohols.

**Reason (R):** The carboxylate ion is stabilised by resonance over two equivalent oxygen atoms.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Both statements are true, and the reason is the correct explanation. In a carboxylate, the negative charge is delocalised equally over two oxygen atoms, which lowers the energy of the ion. An alkoxide has the charge localised on a single oxygen, so the alcohol is much less willing to lose a proton.

</details>

### 18. Benzaldehyde and Fehling's test

**Assertion (A):** Benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling's solution.

**Reason (R):** Benzaldehyde has no α-hydrogen atom.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The assertion is true: benzaldehyde gives no red precipitate with Fehling's solution. The reason is false: benzaldehyde does have a hydrogen on the carbonyl carbon, and the absence of an α-hydrogen is not why it fails the test. Aromatic aldehydes are less easily oxidised than aliphatic aldehydes because the carbonyl is conjugated with the ring, and Fehling's reagent is not a strong enough oxidant for them. Benzaldehyde does reduce Tollens' reagent.

</details>

## Section D — 2 matching and multi-statement questions

### 19. Matching named reactions

Match each reaction in Column I with its characteristic reagent or condition in Column II.

| Column I | Column II |
| --- | --- |
| (A) Cannizzaro reaction | (p) $\mathrm{Zn{-}Hg/HCl}$ |
| (B) Aldol condensation | (q) $\mathrm{I_2/NaOH}$ |
| (C) Haloform reaction | (r) concentrated $\mathrm{NaOH}$ |
| (D) Clemmensen reduction | (s) dilute $\mathrm{NaOH}$ |

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A–r, B–s, C–q, D–p.**

- The Cannizzaro reaction uses concentrated sodium hydroxide and an aldehyde with no α-hydrogen.
- Aldol condensation uses dilute base; the enolate forms and adds to a second carbonyl.
- The haloform reaction uses iodine and sodium hydroxide to convert a methyl ketone or ethanal into iodoform.
- Clemmensen reduction uses zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid to remove the carbonyl oxygen.

</details>

### 20. Multi-statement question

Consider the following statements about acid derivatives:

(i) Ethanoyl chloride reacts vigorously with water.

(ii) Amides are more reactive than esters towards nucleophilic substitution.

(iii) Ethanoic anhydride is less reactive than ethanoyl chloride.

(iv) Amides are strongly basic because of the nitrogen lone pair.

Which statements are correct?

- A. (i) and (ii) only
- B. (i) and (iii) only
- C. (ii) and (iv) only
- D. (iii) and (iv) only

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Statement (i) is correct: acyl chlorides hydrolyse vigorously, releasing hydrogen chloride. Statement (iii) is correct: chloride is a better leaving group than carboxylate, so the acyl chloride is more reactive than the anhydride. Statement (ii) is false: amides are the least reactive acid derivatives because the nitrogen donates its lone pair strongly into the carbonyl and $\mathrm{NH_2^-}$ is a poor leaving group. Statement (iv) is false: the amide nitrogen lone pair is delocalised into the carbonyl, so amides are essentially neutral, not strongly basic.

</details>
