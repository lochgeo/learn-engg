## First choose the tool

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| A structure must be named or drawn | Longest chain, carbonyl lowest locant, priority order |
| A formula is given and structures are asked for | Degree of unsaturation, $\text{DBE}=(2c+2+n-h-x)/2$ |
| An aldehyde is wanted from an alcohol | PCC in $\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}$, or distill the aldehyde as it forms |
| A ketone is wanted | Oxidation of a secondary alcohol, alkyne hydration, or Friedel–Crafts acylation |
| A carbonyl is attacked by a nucleophile | Addition at $\mathrm{C{=}O}$, then protonation; compare aldehyde vs ketone |
| A Grignard reagent is used | Count the carbon groups on the carbonyl to predict primary/secondary/tertiary alcohol |
| An α-hydrogen is present | Aldol condensation, haloform reaction, or enolate chemistry |
| No α-hydrogen and strong base | Cannizzaro reaction |
| Aldehyde versus ketone | Tollens' (both aromatic and aliphatic aldehydes) or Fehling's (aliphatic aldehydes only) |
| A methyl ketone or ethanal is present | Iodoform test |
| Acid strength is compared | Stability of the carboxylate; inductive effects and distance |
| pH or degree of ionisation is asked | $K_a=x^2/(c-x)$, then $\mathrm{pH}=-\log[\mathrm{H^+}]$ |
| Acid derivatives are compared | Leaving-group ability and resonance donation: acyl chloride > anhydride > ester > amide |
| A yield or atom economy is asked | Mole ratio first, then percentage yield or product mass divided by reactant mass |

## Mastery check

Try each question without looking at the answer. Use molar masses to the nearest whole number unless a question gives a value.

### 1. Name from structure

Give the IUPAC name of $\mathrm{CH_3CH(CH_3)CH_2CHO}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The aldehyde carbon is C1. Numbering from the aldehyde gives a four-carbon chain with a methyl substituent at C3: **3-methylbutanal**. Numbering from the other end would place the aldehyde at C4, which is not allowed because the aldehyde must be C1.

</details>

### 2. Degree of unsaturation

A compound has formula $\mathrm{C_5H_8O}$. Find its degree of unsaturation and draw two possible structures with different functional groups.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$$
\text{DBE}=\frac{2(5)+2-8}{2}=2.
$$

One degree is the carbonyl; the other can be a $\mathrm{C{=}C}$ bond or a ring. Two possibilities are pent-2-enal, $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH{=}CHCHO}$ (carbonyl plus alkene), and cyclopentanone (carbonyl plus ring). Both have formula $\mathrm{C_5H_8O}$.

</details>

### 3. Choosing a preparation

How would you prepare propanone from (a) propan-2-ol and (b) propene?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

(a) Oxidise propan-2-ol with acidified potassium dichromate or PCC. The secondary alcohol becomes propanone and stops there. (b) Hydrate propene with dilute sulphuric acid to give propan-2-ol, then oxidise as in (a). Ozonolysis of propene would give ethanal and methanal, not propanone, so it is not the right route.

</details>

### 4. Reactivity towards nucleophiles

Arrange $\mathrm{HCHO}$, $\mathrm{CH_3CHO}$, $\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}$ and $\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}$ in order of decreasing reactivity towards nucleophilic addition. Explain the order.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$$
\mathrm{HCHO} > \mathrm{CH_3CHO} > \mathrm{CH_3COCH_3} > \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}.
$$

Methanal has two hydrogens and no electron-donating alkyl group, so its carbonyl carbon is the most electrophilic and the least hindered. Ethanal has one methyl group, which donates electron density and adds some steric hindrance. Propanone has two methyl groups, so it is less reactive still. Acetophenone is the least reactive because the phenyl ring donates electron density into the carbonyl by resonance and the carbonyl is part of a conjugated system.

</details>

### 5. Grignard calculation

Ethanal ($2.2\ \text{g}$) reacts with excess methylmagnesium bromide, followed by acid work-up. The isolated alcohol has mass $2.4\ \text{g}$. Identify the alcohol and calculate the percentage yield.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$$
n(\mathrm{CH_3CHO})=\frac{2.2}{44}=0.050\ \text{mol}.
$$

One methyl group is added to the aldehyde carbon, giving propan-2-ol, a secondary alcohol. The theoretical amount is $0.050\ \text{mol}$, with molar mass $60\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$:

$$
m_{\text{theoretical}}=0.050\times60=3.0\ \text{g}.
$$

The percentage yield is

$$
\frac{2.4}{3.0}\times100\%=80\%.
$$

</details>

### 6. Cannizzaro or aldol?

Which of benzaldehyde, ethanal, propanone and propanal undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction with concentrated sodium hydroxide? Explain.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Only **benzaldehyde** undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction. It has no α-hydrogen, so it cannot form an enolate and cannot undergo aldol condensation. In concentrated base it disproportionates to benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate. Ethanal, propanone and propanal all have α-hydrogens and undergo aldol reactions instead.

</details>

### 7. Distinguishing three carbonyl compounds

You are given ethanal, propanone and benzaldehyde. Describe how Tollens', Fehling's and iodoform tests together identify each compound.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Tollens' reagent gives a silver mirror with ethanal and benzaldehyde, but not with propanone. Fehling's solution gives a red precipitate with ethanal only; benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling's. The iodoform test gives a yellow precipitate with ethanal and propanone, but not with benzaldehyde. Combining the results: ethanal is positive to all three; propanone is positive only to iodoform; benzaldehyde is positive to Tollens' but negative to Fehling's and iodoform.

</details>

### 8. Acidity order

Arrange trichloroethanoic acid, chloroethanoic acid, benzoic acid and ethanoic acid in order of increasing acidity.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The order is

$$
\text{ethanoic acid} < \text{benzoic acid} < \text{chloroethanoic acid} < \text{trichloroethanoic acid}.
$$

Three chlorines withdraw electron density more strongly than one, so trichloroethanoic acid is the strongest. Chloroethanoic acid is stronger than benzoic acid because the α-chlorine has a strong inductive effect, while the phenyl group in benzoic acid withdraws only weakly. Ethanoic acid is the weakest because its methyl group donates electron density and destabilises the carboxylate.

</details>

### 9. pH of a dilute acid

Find the pH of a $0.010\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$ solution of ethanoic acid. Take $K_a=1.8\times10^{-5}$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$$
K_a\approx\frac{x^2}{0.010},
$$

so

$$
x^2=(1.8\times10^{-5})(0.010)=1.8\times10^{-7},
$$

$$
x=4.24\times10^{-4}\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}.
$$

Thus

$$
\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}(4.24\times10^{-4})=3.37.
$$

The degree of ionisation is $4.24\times10^{-4}/0.010=4.24\%$, which is small enough for the approximation to be reasonable.

</details>

### 10. Esterification and yield

Ethanoic acid ($0.20\ \text{mol}$) is heated with excess ethanol. The yield of ethyl ethanoate is $70\%$. Calculate the mass of ester obtained and explain why the reaction does not go to completion.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The theoretical amount of ester is $0.20\ \text{mol}$. At $70\%$ yield, the actual amount is

$$
0.70\times0.20=0.14\ \text{mol}.
$$

Ethyl ethanoate has molar mass $88\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$, so

$$
m=0.14\times88=12.3\ \text{g}.
$$

Esterification is reversible: the ester and water can hydrolyse back to the acid and alcohol. Removing water or distilling the ester shifts the equilibrium towards the product, but a closed reaction at equilibrium does not reach $100\%$ conversion.

</details>
