## The big idea

Scientists around the world use the **International System of Units (SI)** so that a measurement means the same thing in every lab, classroom, and country.

## Start here

The SI system is built on seven base quantities. You do not need to memorise them all in one breath; start with the ones you meet most often.

| Base quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

Other units are derived from these. Force, for instance, is mass multiplied by acceleration, so one newton is one kilogram metre per second squared: $1\ \text{N} = 1\ \text{kg·m/s}^2$.

### Scale with prefixes

Prefixes let us describe very large and very small measurements without long strings of zeroes. A kilometre is $10^3$ metres; a millimetre is $10^{-3}$ metres — writing a wire's thickness as $0.5\ \text{mm}$ is far friendlier than $0.0005\ \text{m}$. The prefix is a power-of-ten multiplier, so convert by tracking that factor.

## Worked example

### Convert 72 km/h to m/s

Replace each kilometre by 1000 metres and each hour by 3600 seconds:

$$
72\ \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}} \times \frac{1\ \text{h}}{3600\ \text{s}} = 20\ \text{m/s}
$$

The kilometres and hours cancel. Keeping units in the calculation is a simple way to catch a mistaken conversion.

## Try it yourself

**Express 3500 milligrams in grams.**

<details>
<summary>Reveal the explanation</summary>

The prefix milli means $10^{-3}$, so $1000\ \text{mg} = 1\ \text{g}$. Therefore $3500\ \text{mg} = 3.5\ \text{g}$.
</details>

## Remember

- Use the unit symbol as part of the answer.
- Convert units by multiplying by a ratio equal to one.
- Prefixes are powers of ten; keep track of the exponent.
