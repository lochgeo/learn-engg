## First choose the tool

| Clue in the question | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Force and displacement are known | Work: $W=F\Delta r\cos\theta$ |
| Force changes with position | Signed area or $W=\int F_x dx$ |
| Initial/final speed and net work | Work–energy: $W_{net}=\Delta K$ |
| Only conservative forces transfer energy | $K_i+U_i=K_f+U_f$ |
| Friction or an external push does work | $\Delta K+\Delta U=W_{nonconservative}$ |
| Collision lasts briefly | Momentum conservation; add kinetic energy only if elastic |
| Collision is only partly elastic | Momentum plus restitution: $e=(v_2-v_1)/(u_1-u_2)$ |
| Motion in a vertical circle | Energy for speed **and** radial Newton’s law for tension |
| Blocks are connected by a string | Energy of the whole system; internal tension cancels |
| A graph of $U(x)$ is given | $F_x=-dU/dx$; equilibrium and turning points |
| “How fast is energy transferred?” | Power: $P=\Delta E/\Delta t$ or $\vec F\cdot\vec v$ |

## Mastery check

Try each without looking at the answer first. Use $g=10\ \text{m/s}^2$ unless stated otherwise.

### 1. A force pulls at an angle

A $25\ \text{N}$ force pulls a sled $8\ \text{m}$ at $60^\circ$ above its horizontal displacement. How much work does the pull do?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$W=F\Delta r\cos\theta=(25)(8)(0.5)=100\ \text{J}$. Only the horizontal component contributes.
</details>

### 2. Net work changes speed

A $4\ \text{kg}$ block moving at $2\ \text{m/s}$ receives $48\ \text{J}$ of net work. Find its final speed.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$K_i=\frac12(4)(2^2)=8\ \text{J}$. Thus $K_f=56\ \text{J}$ and $v=\sqrt{2K_f/m}=\sqrt{28}\approx5.29\ \text{m/s}$.
</details>

### 3. Spring and gravity together

A $0.50\ \text{kg}$ block falls $0.80\ \text{m}$ before compressing a vertical spring of constant $250\ \text{N/m}$ by $0.20\ \text{m}$. Assuming it starts and momentarily ends at rest, find whether the data are consistent with no losses.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The gravitational energy released over the total descent $1.0\ \text{m}$ is $mgh=(0.5)(10)(1.0)=5.0\ \text{J}$. Spring energy at maximum compression is $\frac12kx^2=\frac12(250)(0.20)^2=5.0\ \text{J}$. The values are consistent with no losses.
</details>

### 4. Friction and energy

A $3\ \text{kg}$ object slides on a level surface at $6\ \text{m/s}$ until friction stops it. How much work does friction do?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

The net work equals $\Delta K=0-\frac12(3)(36)=-54\ \text{J}$. On a level surface with friction as the only horizontal force, friction does $-54\ \text{J}$ of work.
</details>

### 5. Power at a steady speed

A winch pulls upward with a constant $600\ \text{N}$ force while raising a load at $0.40\ \text{m/s}$. What is its instantaneous mechanical power?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Force and velocity are aligned, so $P=Fv=(600)(0.40)=240\ \text{W}$.
</details>

### 6. Vertical circle

A $0.40\ \text{kg}$ mass moves at the top of a vertical circle of radius $0.50\ \text{m}$ at $3.0\ \text{m/s}$. Find the string tension at the top. Use $g=10\ \text{m/s}^2$.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

At the top, both weight and tension point inward: $mg+T=mv^2/R$. Thus $T=m(v^2/R-g)=0.40(9/0.50-10)=3.2\ \text{N}$. It is positive, so a string can provide it.
</details>

### 7. Perfectly inelastic collision

A $1\ \text{kg}$ cart moving right at $5\ \text{m/s}$ hits a $4\ \text{kg}$ cart moving left at $1\ \text{m/s}$. They stick. Find the final velocity and the decrease in kinetic energy.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Take right as positive. $p_i=(1)(5)+(4)(-1)=1\ \text{kg·m/s}$, so $v_f=p_i/(5)=0.20\ \text{m/s}$ right. $K_i=\frac12(1)(25)+\frac12(4)(1)=14.5\ \text{J}$; $K_f=\frac12(5)(0.20)^2=0.10\ \text{J}$. The decrease is $14.4\ \text{J}$, transferred to other forms during impact.
</details>

### 8. Explain, don’t just calculate

Why can friction do negative work while total energy remains conserved?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Negative friction work means the mechanical energy of the chosen moving-object system decreases. That energy is transferred mainly into thermal energy of the surfaces and surroundings. If those are included in a larger system, total energy is conserved.
</details>

### 9. Partly elastic collision

A $2\ \text{kg}$ cart moving right at $3\ \text{m/s}$ hits a stationary $1\ \text{kg}$ cart head-on with $e=0.50$. Find both final velocities and the kinetic-energy loss.

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

Momentum gives $2v_1+v_2=6$; restitution gives $v_2-v_1=1.5$. Solving, $v_1=1.5\ \text{m/s}$ and $v_2=3.0\ \text{m/s}$, both to the right. $K_i=9\ \text{J}$ and $K_f=6.75\ \text{J}$, so $2.25\ \text{J}$ is lost.
</details>

### 10. Potential-energy graph

For $U(x)=x^2-4x$ joules, find the force at a general $x$, locate the equilibrium, and classify it. If the total energy is $E=-3\ \text{J}$, between which points can the particle move?

<details><summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

$F_x=-dU/dx=4-2x$. The equilibrium is at $x=2\ \text{m}$, where $F_x=0$. Since $d^2U/dx^2=2>0$, $U$ has a minimum and the equilibrium is stable. The turning points satisfy $U(x)=E$: $x^2-4x=-3$, so $x=1\ \text{m}$ and $x=3\ \text{m}$. The particle moves between these points.
</details>
