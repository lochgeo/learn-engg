## The big idea

Forces describe interactions. They do not keep an object moving; an unbalanced force changes its velocity. To reason clearly, isolate one object and draw every external force acting on it.

## Start here

Newton’s first law says velocity stays constant when the net external force is zero. The second law makes the link quantitative: $\vec F_{\text{net}}=m\vec a$. Newton’s third law says interacting bodies exert equal and opposite forces on one another; the pair acts on different bodies, so it does not cancel on a single free-body diagram.

Momentum is $\vec p=m\vec v$. Impulse $\vec J=\Delta\vec p$ measures the effect of a force over time. Friction acts along a contact surface and opposes relative motion or its tendency. For dry surfaces, the limiting static friction is approximately $f_s\leq\mu_sN$ and kinetic friction is $f_k=\mu_kN$.

## Worked example

A $4\ \text{kg}$ trolley has a net horizontal force of $12\ \text{N}$. Its acceleration is

$$
a=F_{\text{net}}/m=12/4=3\ \text{m/s}^2.
$$

This is the net force after all horizontal pushes and friction have been combined with signs.

## Try it yourself

**A $2\ \text{kg}$ box rests on a horizontal floor. What is its normal force if $g=9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and there are no other vertical forces?**

<details><summary>Reveal the explanation</summary>

There is no vertical acceleration, so the normal force balances weight: $N=mg=19.6\ \text{N}$ upward.
</details>

## Remember

- Draw forces on one chosen body at a time.
- Use the net force in Newton’s second law.
- Third-law partners act on different objects.
