## The big idea

To describe motion along a line, choose an origin and a positive direction. Position then has a sign, and changes in position tell us both how far and which way an object moved.

## Start here

Displacement is $\Delta x=x_2-x_1$. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time; average speed instead uses total distance. On a position–time graph, the slope is velocity. The slope of a velocity–time graph is acceleration, while the area under it gives displacement.

For constant acceleration, the definitions lead to a compact set of relations:

$$
v=u+at,\qquad x-x_0=ut+\frac12at^2,\qquad v^2=u^2+2a(x-x_0).
$$

Here $u$ and $v$ are initial and final velocities. These equations only apply when acceleration stays constant. Pick a sign convention first and keep it throughout the solution.

## Worked example

A cyclist starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at $2\ \text{m/s}^2$ for $5\ \text{s}$. The distance is

$$
x-x_0=ut+\frac12at^2=0+\frac12(2)(5^2)=25\ \text{m}.
$$

The final speed is $v=u+at=10\ \text{m/s}$. The units check: acceleration times time squared is a length.

## Try it yourself

**A car slows from $20\ \text{m/s}$ to rest in $4\ \text{s}$. What is its acceleration?**

<details><summary>Reveal the explanation</summary>

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Then $a=(v-u)/t=(0-20)/4=-5\ \text{m/s}^2$. The negative sign means acceleration points opposite to the car’s motion.
</details>

## Remember

- Speed is scalar; velocity includes direction.
- Graph slope and area have physical meaning.
- Use the constant-acceleration equations only when their assumption holds.
